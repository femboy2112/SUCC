## 5. The new FINITARY OMEGA SUPPORT THEOREM

Let T_0 subseteq T_1 subseteq ... be a monotone sequence of
first-order theories, and let
T_omega=union_(n<omega)T_n. With ordinary FINITARY proof rules,

\[
\boxed{
T_\omega\vdash\varphi
\iff \exists n<\omega\quad T_n\vdash\varphi.
}
\]

**Proof:** A finite T_omega derivation invokes only finitely many
nonlogical axioms A_1,...,A_m. Each belongs to T_{n_i};
take n=max n_i. Monotonicity puts all A_i in T_n, and the
same finite proof works in T_n. The converse is monotonicity.

This classical finitary compactness/support theorem has now
been turned into a CHECKED finite-provenance algorithm in
observer_reflection.py: given a real finite proof, recover the
earliest named theory stage containing every axiom it uses,
then recheck the entire proof at that stage.

**Interpretation:** Mere passage through an omega colimit
does not create a new *finitary theorem*. A new theorem
at omega+1 requires additional nonfinitary inference,
external reflection, or a separate mathematical argument
about the completed structure.

The infinitary omega rule

\[
\frac{P(0),P(1),P(2),\dots}{\forall n\,P(n)}
\]

is NOT an ordinary finite proof step. The program REJECTS it
as a proof rule. A finite proof of a universally quantified

This answers the author's question more precisely than

## 7. Turing, Church, recursive descriptions, ordinal reflection

A finite two-counter program M has a decidable predicate
P_M(n): M does not halt within n steps. A total procedure
deciding forall n P_M(n) uniformly over ALL two-counter
programs would solve the halting problem. No such uniform
algorithm exists. Our earlier godel_succ.py uses an actual
bounded two-counter machine: a looping machine and a
delayed-halting machine are observationally identical

undecidability of the single RH proposition.

\[

T_\lambda=\bigcup_{\beta<\lambda}T_\beta.
