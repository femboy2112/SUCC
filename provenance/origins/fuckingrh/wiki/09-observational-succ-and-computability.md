## 9.3 Omega is a formal limit; omega+1 is a typed report

\[
\mathcal O_0\to\mathcal O_1\to\dots,\qquad
\mathcal O_\omega=\operatorname{colim}_n\mathcal O_n,\qquad
\mathcal R_{\omega+1}=\mathsf{Report}(\mathcal O_\omega).
\]

This does not mean that a physical observer literally
finishes an infinite-time computation. Nor is a
post-limit report automatically a proof.

A classic **finitary compactness** result establishes that,
for a monotone theory chain with ordinary finitary rules:

\[
T_\omega=\bigcup_n T_n
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T_\omega\vdash\varphi
\iff\exists n\quad T_n\vdash\varphi.
\]

A finite proof contains finitely many axioms, all of which
already occur at some finite stage. This has an executable
stage-support certificate in the new logic package.

This is not a statement that infinite mathematical truths
cannot have finite proofs. It states that simply taking
a union of finite recorded axioms does not manufacture a
new finitary proof rule.

## 9.5 Turing, recursion and the two successors

A two-counter machine has a decidable predicate:
it has survived its first n steps.
Whether it survives every step is not uniformly
decidable across ALL programs. The existing finite
observation engine compares a real loop and a
delayed halt that look identical before the
later event becomes visible.

Turing's 1939 ordinal logics motivate the
distinction between:

- SUCCESSOR OF OBSERVATION: a new causally justified datum;
- SUCCESSOR OF THEORY: a new axiom, consistency claim or

A typical theoretical progression adds
Con(T_alpha) at a successor and unions
theories at constructive limit ordinals.
That new consistency axiom is NOT deduced from
a finite history just by renaming the stage.

The program implements a typed external-axiom
tag and the finite-support theorem. The

constructive ordinal notations are still
external classical mathematics.
