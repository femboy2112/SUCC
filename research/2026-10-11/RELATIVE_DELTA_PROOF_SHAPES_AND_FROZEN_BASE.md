# Relative Knowledge SUCC: operational placement, moving bases and proof-shaped probes

**Date:** 2026-10-11. **Status:** user-originated hypothesis plus exact new-to-repository finite constructions; elementary mathematical theorems with explicit assumptions; physical, infinitary and general categorical claims remain UNVERIFIED. No academic priority or RH proof claimed.

**Dependencies:** the existing SUCC finite/omega/dark trichotomy, absorbing Hard SUCC and knowledge-aided observer ladder (draft PRs #6–#8). Imported source files and historical provenance are untouched.

## 0. Faithful interpretation of the clarified proposal

A and B share **the same justified knowledge, the same access to empirical probes, and the same reasoning capability** when synchronized. A performs several absorbing observational steps. A communicates **path and value deltas relative to B's most recently agreed position**, rather than a full absolute state. B receives a finite message and reconstructs the field delta through one epistemic macro-update.

The common conceptual basis itself can evolve, just as proofs and concepts built by previous mathematicians change what later mathematicians can express or test. The user suspects the full ideal infinite ladder cannot always be transmitted as a finite code to an epistemically frozen observer at its original base. They further suggest that a proof can make the inferential result of a class of probes recognizable from the probes' mathematical structure itself.

The necessary mathematical distinctions are (a) a fully justified empirical result, (b) a source-compatible inference, (c) a mathematically constructible probe type, (d) an actually realizable and authenticated physical observation, and (e) a finite certified communication.

## 1. Operational placement rather than agreement on conclusions

Fix a declared source class X; total deterministic probe responses p:X→Y_p; a sound, common rule/interpreter basis R; and physically feasible probe sets Φ_i. The historical observation record H_i is immutable, with original event ownership retained.

For each observer i let K_i be the sources consistent with H_i, Th(K_i) its universally agreed probe predictions, Cl_R(H_i) its inferential closure, E_i the epistemically accessible probes, and U_i the already measured probe/value pairs. Define

\[
\mathrm{Pos}_{\mathcal B}(i)=
(\mathcal B,K_i,\mathrm{Th}(K_i),\mathrm{Cl}_R(H_i),
 E_i,\Phi_i,U_i,(E_i\cap\Phi_i)\setminus U_i).
\]

Equality requires identical source-compatible knowledge, reasoning/interpreter version, physically and epistemically available probes, previously executed observations, and runnable future probe frontier. Raw ownership tags and mere local step counters are separately tracked; those need not be identical for two epistemically synchronized observers.

**Finite deterministic continuation theorem (PROVED LOCAL).** If two observers have equal Pos and operate under the same deterministic non-disturbing one-use probe semantics, then every true source compatible with their history produces the same ordered next observable outcomes and equal next Pos. By induction all corresponding future local successors retain equality until saturation. The implementation additionally enumerates every surviving source as a finite regression check. This does not prove equal future quantum, time-sensitive or asynchronous behavior.

**Hostile controls:** matching abstract conclusions but different already-observed probes fails the criterion; matching knowledge but different physical instrument feasibility also fails. Earlier weak semantic transport may still work, but the user's strong equal-placement criterion requires rejecting that pair.

## 2. Finite relative path-and-value transport

A delta takes the typed form

\[
\delta_{A\to B} =
(\text{basis version},\text{recipient position},
 \Delta_{\rm path},\Delta_{\rm value},
 \text{endpoint commitment},\text{source provenance}).
\]

The receiver reconstructs A's next probe menu from its own current model, the current shared rules, and A's registered physical feasibility. Path indices indicate directions within that current menu—not absolute global probe names. Each outcome token is either:

- a **null marker** when all currently compatible sources agree on the probe's result, which the receiver reconstructs from the shared mathematics; or
- an index of the actually observed result among the canonically ordered still-possible outcomes.

The receiver checks that every batch is exactly a lawful next source-observation step, recomputes the compatible-source fiber and closure, and verifies the declared endpoint and replayed history. This is one epistemic knowledge-aided macro-step for B, not B physically executing A's observations. The sender's identity remains in the history.

**Strong position-anchored catch-up theorem (PROVED LOCAL):** Identical prior *event transcripts* are not necessary. Suppose A and B have the same Pos under a shared basis (including knowledge, physical probe feasibility, reasoning rules, already executed probe/value pairs and current runnable frontier), although they may have gathered prior observations independently and retained different observer-origin labels. A performs a finite sequence of new, lawful local probe batches. If their outcomes are genuine/trusted and the shared source/probe model is sound, a finite path+value delta anchored to their **equal prior operational position** lets B reconstruct A's resulting Pos in one K-SUCC update.

**Proof:** Induct over A's new batches. At the base, A_before and B_before have equal Pos, hence identical enabled sender probe menu, compatible source outcomes and closure under R. The relative next-path directions therefore select exactly the same probes and each decoded value is uniquely determined by its code in that shared source fiber. Applying the events yields equal new operational positions. Iterate through all finite batches. B retains its own old, independently performed events and appends the new A-origin testimony. The proof does not assert the old histories were identical, or that A's real-world measurement signatures were authenticated.

The earlier **strict-prefix** relay remains a special case that reconstructs a complete shared causal transcript when both began with identical event origins and ordering. Its stronger history-equality requirement must not be mistaken for the maintainer's epistemic-synchronization premise.

**Concrete control:** A and B each independently perform p0, so their event-owner histories differ but their operational knowledge, physical access and reasoning are identical. A then observes p1 and p2. A's position-anchored delta carries those path/value differences to B in one informed step; B's initial p0 remains B-origin, and the new events retain A-origin. Their entire subsequent deterministic probe frontier matches. The old common-transcript-prefix API correctly refuses this case, which motivated the more faithful version.

**Partial composition:** a second patch can be applied after the first when the basis version and operational end/start positions agree, regardless of differences in earlier event origins. Two sequential patches and a directly transported concatenation of the same lawful new facts give the same operational endpoint. The number of macro-steps, causal provenance prefixes and wire bytes may differ. No arbitrary reordering, concurrent join or change-of-basis commutativity is claimed.

**Compression accounting:** the implementation removes absolute probe names and redundant known outcome values from event payloads. It is not generally shortest-bit code: source tables, interpreter, model, provenance signatures, commitments and per-packet headers must also be counted. Large shared semantic context is not free information.

## 3. Evolving the shared mathematical language

A concept/basis upgrade is a meaning-preserving map from basis B_n to B_(n+1). Our finite experiment verifies a **conservative definitional extension**: starting with known binary probes p and q, a new named probe r has response r(x)=p(x) XOR q(x), with similar AND/NOT constructors. Receiver and sender independently compute the entire new interpretation on all admitted sources and check matching basis fingerprints.

This is useful as a test: the older probe outputs and compatible-source fiber stay unchanged; the common language admits a new epistemically intelligible query; and **no new physical instrument is silently granted**. Definitional language growth itself does not produce new empirical bits. When the old observations already determine p(x) and q(x), the derived r(x) becomes inferable immediately.

A relative path/value patch encoded against the upgraded B_(n+1) is ill-typed to a decoder frozen at B_n. A separately checked concept-upgrade message repairs this, but then the recipient is no longer epistemically stationary. Future work must specify independent empirical probe discoveries and semantic translations that are not simple truth-functional definitions; blindly extending a source table is not a proof of a new physical modality.

## 4. When does a probe's existence carry its result?

This is the most discriminating mathematical issue raised by the user's proof intuition.

For a named probe p:X→Y and current compatible-source class K, define the possible result set p(K)={p(x):x∈K}.

**Exact option-value criterion (PROVED LOCAL):**
\[
\boxed{\text{A's model can infer the result of p without measuring p}
       \quad\iff\quad |p(K)|=1.}
\]

If p(K) is a singleton {y}, the model already entails that p's output is y. The finite lab constructs a proof-shaped certificate for y, bound to the exact recipient source/context and independently rechecks it. This is a **deduction**, not a forged causal event.

If p(K) contains at least two outputs, two candidate true sources have the same current evidence yet disagree on p; the mere mathematical possibility of performing p does not determine its result. Example: X={A,B}, p(A)=A and p(B)=B. Everyone can know p exists while not knowing which output will occur.

**A second, stronger proof-shaped phenomenon: a theorem can identify the *right future experiment* without identifying its result.** Fix a target T:X→Z (e.g., a field invariant or a proposition the observer wants to decide). A probe p is *target-sufficient relative to K* exactly when

\[
\boxed{\forall x,x'\in K:\quad
p(x)=p(x')\ \Longrightarrow\ T(x)=T(x').}
\]

Equivalently, for every possible outcome y of p, the corresponding fiber \(K_y=\{x\in K:p(x)=y\}\) has a single target value \(T(K_y)=\{t_y\}\). The resulting proof produces a **conditional decision table** "if p returns y, conclude T=t_y" for every possible y.

**Proof of necessity and sufficiency:** If p has matching values on two compatible sources with different target outcomes, even obtaining p's answer cannot distinguish those target possibilities; it is insufficient. Conversely, if every p-fiber agrees on T, observing any actual y leaves only a fiber with one possible T. Therefore one completed *physically executable* p observation determines the target, provided p has been epistemically recognized and its instrument is feasible. There is no claim that the observer already knows which target outcome will occur.

This is an important refinement of the user's proposal: **the mere possession of a proof of an experiment's decisiveness can eliminate an entire search for a suitable probe, even when the final empirical value remains unknown.** The proof compresses *investigative path structure*, not arbitrary source information.

**Explicit controls:** A two-source identity probe p(A)=A, p(B)=B is target-sufficient to determine the actual source once it is used, though the source is not identified before measurement. The mathematical parity target over sources 00,01,10,11 is not determined by a left-bit probe alone. A derived XOR/parity probe is target-sufficient for parity, but in the finite conservative basis-upgrade model that mathematical concept is not automatically a physically executable instrument. We reject any "one physical step" conclusion when p is missing from the actual feasible device set.

The exact \`certify_probe_decision\` and \`verify_probe_decision\` functions compute/check all outcome branches against the declared finite source table without actualizing a new outcome. Their success does not independently authenticate the physical instrument, establish the premise K is the entire true source space, or prove a universal cognitive phenomenon.

**The repair of the original idea:** If a "probe" is really a *proof object* of type "for all x compatible with this context, p(x)=y", then the existence of a **valid inhabitant of that value-indexed type** carries the conclusion y. If instead "probe" means a merely *available physical experiment* of type X→Y, availability does not generally supply its source-dependent outcome.

**Source-dependent options:** Availability of an instrument can itself be source-sensitive evidence if it is actually tested or its existence is source-authenticated; then observing the availability is an additional empirical outcome. A declared option that is merely contemplated does not act as that observation. The test suite explicitly separates these.

This is closely related to finite checking of proof certificates (e.g., G. Necula, *Proof-Carrying Code*, POPL 1997, https://doi.org/10.1145/263699.263712) and to dynamic epistemic updates (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dynamic-epistemic/). The shared idea is that a validated proof permits some conclusions without independently re-running all actions that motivated its construction. These literatures do not establish a physical SUCC ontology.

## 5. Why the infinite ladder does not generally go home to frozen B0

A short delta at rung n assumes the receiver already knows basis B_n, its decoder and all requisite previous concepts. Its apparent compactness is **conditional** on that accumulated side information.

These quantifiers do not commute:

\[
(\forall n<\omega)(\exists\ \text{finite }\delta_n)
\quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad
\exists\ \text{finite }m_\omega
 \text{ carrying every arbitrary possible limit to }B_0 .
\]

Fix the base decoder and suppose n bits of the world are arbitrary and not inferable from B0. There are 2^n distinct source prefixes but only 2^n-1 binary strings of length at most n-1. Hence some n-bit source requires at least n transmitted bits in a one-shot, exact, fixed-base code. The bound is about the **worst case**, not every specific sequence. It is a pigeonhole theorem, not a physical claim about infinite information being transported.

An arbitrary complete infinite binary source varies over uncountably many alternatives, whereas all finite binary messages form a countable set; no universal finite exact final message can encode every such source in B0.

**Important exceptions:** a particular infinite structure may have a short generating rule and an independent finite proof of its validity, and so can be communicated intensionally via a shared interpreter. A physically stationary receiver may also accumulate an unbounded series of packets over unbounded time; doing so changes its knowledge. A receiver whose **knowledge and decoder stay frozen** cannot learn from an unrecognized later message. Thus the user's intuition is correct as a *uniform frozen-base transmission obstruction*, not an impossibility theorem about every finitely describable infinite result.

A finite message in basis B_n need not remain intelligible as the same token in B0. Backward transport requires verified translation plus all necessary dictionary/proof dependencies. Equivalently, one must charge the description cost of the earlier shared conceptual ladder.

Adjacent distributed-computation literature distinguishes copying entire replica states from operation- and delta-based updates, with separate causal delivery/merge requirements (P. Almeida, *Approaches to Conflict-free Replicated Data Types*, 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18220). The SUCC research adds epistemic meaning, legal experimental access and proof authority as obligations; the existence of those earlier distributed algorithms is not evidence for our new physical theory.

### A sharper compression target: future-indistinguishable histories

The current operational signature is deliberately **sufficient**, not **minimal**. To define a genuinely shortest continuation-preserving message, one can first classify history states by the distinction that matters to the user:

\[
h\equiv_{\rm future}h'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{every admissible future probe/action path has the same
possible observable results and requested reports from }h,h'.
\]

The equality must count availability of actions itself: if one state offers a possible probe the other cannot perform, they are different even before an outcome is observed. In finite deterministic labeled transition systems, this relation can be calculated through partition refinement, with a properly defined observation/report labeling. Only after establishing the correct quotient can one optimize the communication needed to identify the receiver's new equivalence class.

This resembles the classical **Myhill–Nerode** construction, where input histories are merged exactly when no future suffix distinguishes their acceptance behavior; equivalence classes determine minimal deterministic automaton states. Primary pedagogical reference: Cornell CS4120 DFA minimization notes, https://courses.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs4120/2026sp/notes.html?id=leximpl . Our observer system includes causally constrained probe menus and reported knowledge, so an explicit labeled transition system and corresponding partition-refinement theorem remain to be built before claiming the classical automata result applies unchanged.

The important prediction is that the full chronological observation history can be **strictly larger** than the minimum information B needs to act indistinguishably from A in every later admissible situation. Conversely, present conclusions alone can be **strictly smaller** than the required future-sufficient state. Those two inequalities suggest the correct minimization frontier.
