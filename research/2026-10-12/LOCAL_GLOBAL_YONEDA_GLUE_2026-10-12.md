## 0. The object actually requested

There are two independent motions in the author's proposal:

1. **Arithmetic SUCC/FUCC:** SUCC walks along valid additive steps in a sieve; authentic Gaussian FUCC factors exclude divisibility. In a finite periodic quotient, every arrow must retain its true integer displacement. The mathematical target is finite-stage annihilation of all nonzero escape holonomy, not checking a particular given walk.
2. **Diagonal epistemic tower:** many A/B observers climb lawful stages, independently construct local mathematical *proof objects*, and relay the results diagonally to E. E should construct and verify a global proof through one finite epistemic macro-update, without itself executing every upstream physical observation or being handed the global answer.

**Crucial upgrade over PR #26:** that PR distributed *verification of pieces of a previously supplied global potential*. Here every climber independently computes a potential from only the edges of their own connected patch. Neither climbers nor E receive a pre-fitted global potential. E solves the gluing problem. A negative result generates a closed walk in the **original source graph**, not only a bad packet. Diagram agreement is necessary but not source authentication.

## 1. The typed source and sheaf

Let \(\Gamma=(V,D)\) be a finite directed multigraph (possibly including loops and parallel darts), with label \(w:D\to\mathbb Z^d\). In the Gaussian periodic case, \(V\subseteq(\mathbb Z/Q)^d\), and each actual step satisfies

\[
 v+w(e)\equiv v'\pmod Q.\tag{G0}
\]

All reasoning below takes place in the **free path groupoid**: a dart may be formally traversed backward, with label \(-w(e)\). This is a mathematical inverse in the groupoid. For *physical* directed probes, it is **not** automatically a physically executable inverse; symmetric Gaussian step sets provide that additional property.

A **section** over an edge subgraph \(U\) is a map \(g:V(U)\to\mathbb Z^d\) such that

\[
 g(v')-g(v)=w(e)\quad\text{for each dart }e:v\to v'\text{ of }U.\tag{G1}
\]

Let \(\mathscr S(U)\) be the set of these sections, with restriction maps \(\mathscr S(U)\to\mathscr S(W)\) for \(W\subseteq U\). Every **connected** local patch has a section either generated by exact integration or ruled out by a nonzero local closed walk. Given a section on a connected patch, any other differs by one additive constant. Patches need not have connected pairwise intersections.

A source certificate includes the entire finite graph declaration (vertices, every dart and displacement, source signature), local patch edge IDs, exact local potentials, overlap-transition witnesses, and the outcome. A hash commits to a *declared byte-level source*, not independently authenticating arithmetic origin or physical measurement.

## 2. LG1 — The exact Čech gluing theorem (proved)

Let \(U_1,\ldots,U_m\) be connected subgraphs whose vertices and darts jointly cover all of \(\Gamma\). Suppose every observer \(i\) independently produces a valid local section \(g_i\in\mathscr S(U_i)\). For **every shared vertex** \(v\in U_i\cap U_j\), define an oriented overlap edge \((i,j;v)\) with transition

\[
 \delta_{ij}(v):=g_i(v)-g_j(v).\tag{G2}
\]

Keep a separate edge for each common vertex, even if the same pair of charts intersects in multiple disconnected components. This defines the **overlap multigraph** \(N\). We seek gauge translations \(c_i\in\mathbb Z^d\) with

\[
 g_i(v)+c_i=g_j(v)+c_j
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c_j-c_i=\delta_{ij}(v).\tag{G3}
\]

**Theorem LG1:** The following statements are equivalent:

- **(a)** There is a global source potential \(g:V\to\mathbb Z^d\) satisfying every (G1).
- **(b)** The family of local potentials admits constants \(c_i\) satisfying every G3.
- **(c)** The sum of the oriented transitions \(\delta\) around **every closed walk in the overlap multigraph** is zero.
- **(d)** A finite spanning-forest solver of the overlap equations succeeds on all non-tree constraints.

**Proof.** For (a)→(b), on connected \(U_i\), both \(g\) and \(g_i\) integrate the same dart labels, so their difference is constant; call it \(c_i\). Then G3 holds at every shared vertex. For (b)→(a), the local formulas \(g|_{U_i}=g_i+c_i\) agree at all vertices on intersections. They therefore define a unique global function. Every dart lies in a chart and has its verified local increment, so G1 holds globally. The remaining equivalence is the elementary exactness criterion for a 1-cochain on a graph: potential differences sum to zero around every cycle, and conversely one assigns potentials along a spanning forest then checks non-tree edges. QED.

**Strengthening:** if G0 holds, choose one root in each *source* connected component and translate the resulting potential so that its root has the correct address modulo \(Q\). Then every vertex in that component satisfies \(g(v)\equiv v\pmod Q\). This produces the exact confinement potential accepted by the original SUCC finite voltage-graph verifier, without receiving one as an input. It follows that every lifted component above an observed quotient component is finite. The theorem is for a **fixed graph**; it does NOT produce a confining prime sieve for every step bound.

## 3. LG2 — From a failed overlap loop to a concrete source escape (proved)

The failure is not a vague sheaf-cohomology diagnosis. Suppose a finite oriented overlap cycle

\[
 i_0\overset{v_0}\longrightarrow i_1
 \overset{v_1}\longrightarrow\cdots
 \overset{v_{r-1}}\longrightarrow i_0
\]

has nonzero sum \(\Delta=\sum_k\delta_{i_k i_{k+1}}(v_k)\ne0\). Within each succeeding **connected** chart \(U_{i_{k+1}}\), take an edge path from its preceding overlap vertex \(v_k\) to the next overlap vertex \(v_{k+1}\). Concatenate these paths cyclically. It forms a closed walk in the *original source quotient graph*, with actual displacement

\[
 \sum_k \bigl[g_{i_{k+1}}(v_{k+1})-g_{i_{k+1}}(v_k)\bigr]
 =\sum_k\bigl[g_{i_k}(v_k)-g_{i_{k+1}}(v_k)\bigr]
 =\Delta\ne0.\tag{G4}
\]

Thus **every failed global gluing returns a finite, original-source nonzero-holonomy certificate**. The verifier independently checks the overlap cycle, reconstructs the expanded graph path, checks composability and the exact sum. Repeating the translated path is a valid infinite source escape when the original physical graph licenses the corresponding reversed steps (as the symmetric Gaussian SUCC graph does).

**Receipt completeness correction:** after normalizing the global potential to source residue addresses, E stores the FINAL per-chart gauge constants, not preliminary spanning-forest shifts. The independent verifier recomputes these constants from global and local sections and rejects forged gauge metadata. A globally correct potential cannot be used to launder an untrue patch-transport receipt.

**Mutation:** the directed three-vertex clock \(0\to1\to2\to0\) with three \(+1\) voltages has three perfectly valid one-edge local charts. The three pairwise overlaps each contain one vertex and there is no triple overlap. Their transition cycle sums to \(3\), so no global section exists. Replacing the last dart's voltage by \(-2\) repairs the loop and produces a global section. This is *exactly* why successful local checks alone cannot certify E's global proof.

**Disconnected-overlap mutation:** two charts connecting the same two observed vertices can have distinct actual lifts \(+1\) and \(+4\) modulo \(3\). Their intersection has two vertices, and one transition equation at each. These two overlapping arrows form a 2-cycle with displacement \(3\); testing only one arbitrary representative of the pairwise intersection would falsely accept gluing. The code retains both. The triple-overlap Čech identity at a **single shared point** is tautological, but it does not guarantee trivial cohomology on the *entire cover*.

## 4. LG3 — Representation-theoretic and Yoneda formulation

Let \(\mathcal C=\Pi_1(\Gamma)\) be the free source path groupoid. The displacement labels define a functor \(W:\mathcal C\to B\mathbb Z^d\), preserving ordered path composition, including its **true integer increment**. Let \(F_W:\mathcal C\to\mathrm{Set}\) act on the regular additive torsor \(\mathbb Z^d\): every vertex is assigned \(\mathbb Z^d\), every arrow of voltage \(w\) acts by \(x\mapsto x+w\). A global source potential is exactly a **natural transformation**

\[
 \boxed{\quad\operatorname{Nat}(1_{\mathcal C},F_W)
 \;\cong\;\mathscr S(\Gamma).\quad}\tag{G5}
\]

The naturality square for \(e:v\to v'\) is exactly G1. Generator checks imply naturality for all composed and formally inverted paths. By Yoneda, a local element \(x\in F_W(v)\) is equivalent to an element of \(\operatorname{Nat}(\mathcal C(v,-),F_W)\); **this does not by itself produce a global section**. Global gluing requires the represented local data to be compatible on every overlap and every closed path. The regular torsor action is free, so nonzero source holonomy cannot fix a global section.

This is an application of the **classical Yoneda lemma** and elementary functoriality, not a new proof of Yoneda. It supplies a precise, testable interface between ObsSUCC's existing restricted-context Yoneda calculations and its source-relative arithmetic voltage certificates.

Sources: Emily Riehl, *Category Theory in Context*, §2.3 ([primary author-hosted PDF](https://math.jhu.edu/~eriehl/context.pdf)); Tom Leinster, *Basic Category Theory* ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.09375)).

## 5. LG4 — The nonabelian version; parity can miss curvature (proved)

Let \(G\) be **any group**, with each dart assigned transport \(T_e\in G\). Give every vertex the regular left \(G\)-torsor, and require a section to satisfy \(g(v')=T_e g(v)\). If two local sections \(g_i,g_j\) are independently valid, their right-hand gauges must satisfy, on every shared vertex,

\[
 s_j=t_{ij}(v)s_i,\qquad
 t_{ij}(v):=g_j(v)^{-1}g_i(v).\tag{G6}
\]

**Theorem LG4:** Local sections glue to one global torsor section iff the **ordered product** of transition elements around every cycle in the overlap multigraph is identity. As in LG1, a spanning-forest gauge assignment gives a finite constructive proof; a contradiction yields a nonidentity product and a concrete closed source-groupoid walk whose holonomy is nonidentity (up to change of basepoint conjugacy).

**Proof.** A global section restricts on every connected chart to a local section times a constant right multiplier, since \(g_i(v)^{-1}g(v)\) is constant along each transported edge. This supplies G6. Conversely gauges satisfying G6 make the adjusted local sections agree at every shared vertex, defining a global section that respects all darts. Ordered holonomy identity is exactly the condition for solving \(s_j=t_{ij}s_i\) by a spanning-forest assignment. A failure gives a closed sequence whose nonidentity product cannot equal the telescope of honest gauge differences. The source graph-walk expansion uses the same connected-chart routing construction as G4. QED.

**Exact hostile \(S_3\) witness:** choose noncommuting transpositions \(a=(12)\), \(b=(23)\). Four source edges around a square carry \(a,b,a^{-1},b^{-1}\). Their ordered holonomy is a nonidentity 3-cycle, but its image under the sign homomorphism \(S_3\to\{\pm1\}\) is \(+1\). Every one-edge local chart is valid; the global chart-gluing equation fails. This is a rigorous example of **source-visible nonabelian obstruction invisible to an abelianized observer**. A flat nonabelian control passes; fake source/path/section mutations are rejected.

For precedent, this is finite discrete nonabelian Čech cohomology / principal torsor gluing, not a claimed new topological invention. Unlike a scalar 'knowledge score,' the actual ordered transition group is load-bearing. There is no quantum or physical complex phase asserted.

## 6. LG5 — Proof-support rank and the right resource lower bound

For a graph regarded as an undirected **multigraph of all declared source darts**, with \(m\) darts, \(n\) vertices and \(c\) connected components, the dimension of its integral graph cycle space is

\[
 \boxed{\beta_1=m-n+c.}\tag{G7}
\]

A spanning forest contains \(n-c\) edges. Every other dart produces one independent fundamental cycle. For a free abelian displacement target, a global potential exists iff the displacement sum vanishes on this **basis**. This yields a finite complete certificate of length \(\beta_1\) in the *fundamental-cycle-query* model. Necessity in that model is adversarial: if a verifier fails to interrogate even one independent fundamental cycle, two sources identical on all queried cycles can differ on that missing cotree dart's nonzero displacement. Thus \(\beta_1\) independent cycle queries are required in the worst case **when each oracle query reports one chosen cycle's voltage**.

**Boundary:** this is *not* an information-theoretic lower bound on arbitrary mathematical proof length. A separately proved source law might imply many cycle equations simultaneously. Nor should directed reverse darts be counted as independent *geometric* cycles: retaining both orientations introduces algebraically redundant length-two cycles in the directed-multigraph presentation. The rank is valid for the specified graph presentation and test oracle.

The executable module `cycle_support_budget.py` generates every basis walk, checks nonzero ones against original-source voltages, and explicitly labels the oracle restriction. It supplies a rational resource account for E's single macro update: the macro accepts a proof after upstream work; it has no magical constant-time source-knowledge acquisition.

## 7. LG6 — Many A/B climbers and one verifier E (proved conditional)

Define a finite directed communication network with observers \(A_0,B_0,A_1,B_1,\ldots\) climbing via two-to-two ascent and sending source-labeled **mathematical chart certificates** diagonally downward to E. At each floor, higher chart construction is permitted only when the appropriate earlier certificates have genuinely arrived. All hops, origins, declared source signatures, chart edge sets and causal parent references remain in an append-only log.

**Theorem LG6 (finite tower):** Assume (i) a stable, *declared* authentic mathematical graph; (ii) each chart is computed and mathematically checked using only its locally assigned edges; (iii) all source graph vertices and darts are covered; (iv) the network receipt log is replayable with lawful link directions and prerequisites; (v) E possesses all required receipts and uses the source-matched verifier. Then one finite epistemic **macro-SUCC** at E can construct an exact global potential if the class in LG1 vanishes, or construct a nonzero original-source holonomy witness if it does not. If the graph is a genuine periodic Gaussian sieve, a global potential also proves finite lifted components through the existing periodic lifting theorem.

**Proof.** Causal replay and source/signature checks guarantee the syntactic input belongs to the declared local chart family. Independent local chart verification establishes G1 on each local patch; total coverage establishes the hypotheses of LG1. Apply the finite gluing algorithm; independent verification of its output yields the global potential or LG2's source-walk witness. All upstream arithmetic and transmission steps already occurred, and E's one macro action is only a counted final proof-acceptance event. QED.

This upgrades the prior `voltage_tower.py`, where a globally constructed candidate potential was partitioned and checked. The new `constructive_gluing_tower.py` does **not** possess a global candidate and never calls `construct_confinement_potential`; it constructs local charts at each assigned climber and a global potential **only after E has its data**. The connected chart *partition* is still produced from knowledge of the source graph topology; its construction is not yet fully distributed or physically authenticated.

### Exact Gaussian benchmark, no global candidate input

Use the true conjugate factors above 5 and 13, period \(Q=65\), and all unit-length SUCC steps. The code constructs the source graph directly from authentic Gaussian residue arithmetic. Its 2,304 surviving quotient vertices have 4,356 directed darts and 243 connected components, largest size 580. A graph-topology spanning-tree cover yields **462 independently generated connected charts** (two per nontrivial component, plus isolated-component charts). They create **2,280 pointwise overlap constraints**. The finite independent cycle-basis module sees \(4,356-2,304+243=2,295\) fundamental cycles in the full directed-dart presentation. The two counts need not agree because some cycle equations are checked *inside charts* rather than across overlaps.

A three-floor tower with six A/B climbers constructs the 462 charts, transfers receipts across 14 licensed network transmissions carrying 2,310 chart references, and lets E assemble and independently verify every original dart equation in **one counted E macro update**. The maximum lifted component of the sieve is 580. This is a direct finite arithmetic certificate, not a universal result for every step length; Gaussian primes outside the sieve require the previously implemented finite exceptional-attachment adapter before claiming an actual Gaussian-prime bound.

**The necessary negative control:** the three-edge nonzero winding cycle (period 3) also passes all individual local checks, but E returns **OBSTRUCTED**, with a closed original-source walk of displacement \(\pm3\). Missing relays, false chart owners, changed source digests, forged local potentials, unlicensed edges and repeated macro commits are rejected. A distributed no-go is a useful output; the tower never equates 'something arrived' with 'true proof'.

**Authentic prime-sieve negative control (new holdout).** With rational split primes 5 and 17, period 85, the independent quotient-holonomy engine finds an actual unit-step escaping loop. Construct the 160-vertex / 161-dart connected source subgraph of that genuine sieve from this witnessed walk, and assign one edge per local chart (all 161 charts are individually solvable). The new independent overlap solver returns **OBSTRUCTED**; its separately verified unfolded original-source walk has displacement \((\pm85,0)\). This demonstrates source-valid nontrivial descent, not just a toy torsor cycle. It proves neither that every fine sieve escapes nor that the Gaussian primes themselves have an escaping walk.
