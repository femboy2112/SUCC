## 2. Existing mathematical context

The source-faithful primitive presheaf in section 3 is already a **sheaf** for subgraph-union covers. A sheaf glues a **matching family**, not an arbitrary collection of individually existing local sections. Sheafification therefore cannot remove this example's obstruction: sheafifying an already sheaf-valued primitive system preserves its empty global section set.

This is classical gain/voltage graph mathematics. Zaslavsky's *Biased Graphs. I. Bias, Balance, and Gains*, §5, Lemma 5.3, characterizes balance by switching gains to identity; in an additive group, switching is subtraction of vertex potential differences. The author-hosted scan was inspected at PDF page 13, printed p.44; its proof begins with a spanning tree. This is primary precedent for the zero-period criterion, not independent numerical evidence. [Zaslavsky 1989, author-hosted primary paper](https://people.math.binghamton.edu/zaslav/Tpapers/bg1.jctb1989.pdf).

The locally constant gauge sheaf acts simply transitively on primitive sections whenever they exist. A complete locally soluble cover makes this a torsor. ## 3. Proposed SUCC objects

Let \(G=(V,D)\) be a finite directed multigraph, each dart retaining its unique identity and endpoints. Let \(A=\mathbb Z^d\), \(d\geq1\), and let \(w:D\to A\) be the **actual declared integral increment**, not its residue. Work in formal reversible paths: reversed traversal contributes \(-w(e)\). This does not grant a physical reverse operation.

For an edge subgraph \(U\), define

\[
\mathscr P_w(U)=\{p:V(U)\to A:\ p(h(e))-p(t(e))=w(e)\text{ for every }e\in D(U)\}.
\]

For \(W\subseteq U\), restriction is \(p\mapsto p|_{V(W)}\). Objects are subgraphs, arrows are inclusions, and covering families jointly cover **vertices and dart identities**. Intersections preserve only shared darts and their vertices, plus any shared isolated vertices. The gauge sheaf is

\[
\mathscr L_A(U)=\{a:V(U)\to A:\ a(h(e))=a(t(e))\text{ for every }e\in D(U)\}
\cong A^{\pi_0(U)}.
\]

Let \(U_1,\dots,U_s\) be nonempty connected charts forming a complete cover, and let \(p_i\in\mathscr P_w(U_i)\) be exact local sections. The overlap **multigraph** \(N\) has chart vertices and an arrow \((i,j;v)\) for each shared vertex \(v\), with \(i<j\) and

\[
\delta_{ij}(v)=p_i(v)-p_j(v).
\]

Every shared vertex is retained. On connected components of a genuine intersection, \(\delta_{ij}\) is constant, since both sections satisfy the same shared dart equations. At a triple-shared vertex, \(\delta_{ij}+\delta_{jk}=\delta_{ik}\). Thus these are actual Čech cocycle values for \(\mathscr L_A\); the multigraph stores some redundant equal constraints, not a different sheaf.

Define the **source period subgroup** \(\Lambda_G\leq A\) to be generated by all signed sums \(W(\gamma)\) around closed source walks. Define \(\Lambda_N\leq A\) from all signed sums \(\Delta(\eta)\) around closed overlap walks. Empty walks contribute zero. Graph fundamental cycles generate these groups, so both have finite generating sets.

## 4. Candidate theorem

**Theorem A03-T (PROVED LOCAL; classical finite affine descent).** For every finite multigraph, integer dimension, exact label assignment, complete connected chart cover, and valid local section family as in section 3:

1. \(\boxed{\Lambda_N=\Lambda_G}\). This equality is independent of chart gauges and of the choice of complete locally soluble connected cover.
2. The following are equivalent: \(\mathscr P_w(G)\ne\varnothing\); \(\Lambda_G=0\); \(\Lambda_N=0\); the local sections admit constants \(c_i\in A\) with \(c_j-c_i=\delta_{ij}(v)\) at every shared vertex; the Čech class \([\delta]\) is zero. When nonempty, global primitives form an \(A^{\pi_0(G)}\)-torsor.
3. For every abelian group \(B\) and homomorphism \(q:A\to B\), the projected source and projected overlap have period subgroup \(q(\Lambda_G)=q(\Lambda_N)\). They admit a \(B\)-valued global primitive exactly when \(q(\Lambda_G)=0\).
4. For a finite family \(q_j:A\to B_j\), put \(K=\bigcap_j\ker q_j\). Every projected system glues exactly when \(\Lambda_G\subseteq K\). Individual nonzero periods invisible to the entire menu are exactly \((\Lambda_G\cap K)\setminus\{0\}\). These statements are distinct: hiding one period need not hide the whole system.

**Sharp scalar consequence.** For every nonempty finite menu of modular maps \(q_m:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\), \(m\geq2\), let \(L=\operatorname{lcm}\{m\}\). There exists a complete two-chart source with **two vertices and two darts** whose period subgroup is \(L\mathbb Z\ne0\), while every modular projection in that menu glues. Within sources admitting a complete exact connected chart cover, an obstructed source cannot use fewer vertices, darts, or charts. This is a uniform finite-menu alias theorem, not just a parity triangle.

## 5. Proof attempt — complete proof

**Sheaf and torsor first.** A matching family \(p_i\) defines a unique function on covered vertices, because its values agree on intersections. Every dart belongs to a chart; its equation holds in that chart and hence in the glued function. This proves the sheaf axiom. If \(p,p'\) are two primitives, their difference is constant along every dart and therefore on every connected component. Conversely adding any such componentwise constant preserves every equation. Restriction respects this action. Intersecting the given soluble cover with any subgraph gives a soluble cover of that subgraph, proving local nonemptiness. This proves the claimed torsor properties and the sheafification statement.

**Overlap periods are source periods.** Take a closed overlap walk

\[
i_0\xrightarrow{v_0}i_1\xrightarrow{v_1}\cdots\xrightarrow{v_{r-1}}i_r=i_0.
\]

In each succeeding connected chart \(U_{i_{k+1}}\), choose a formal source path from \(v_k\) to \(v_{k+1}\), with indices cyclic modulo \(r\). Connectedness supplies it. Exact integration gives path increment \(p_{i_{k+1}}(v_{k+1})-p_{i_{k+1}}(v_k)\). Concatenating these paths gives a continuous closed source walk. Its increment is

\[
\sum_k[p_{i_{k+1}}(v_{k+1})-p_{i_{k+1}}(v_k)]
=\sum_k[p_{i_k}(v_k)-p_{i_{k+1}}(v_k)]
=\Delta(\eta).
\]

The middle equality is cyclic reindexing; signs are traversal signs, not discarded orientations. Hence every overlap period is a source period and \(\Lambda_N\subseteq\Lambda_G\). Different paths chosen inside one chart give the same increment, because the section integrates all chart darts exactly.

**Source periods are overlap periods.** Take any closed source walk with vertices \(v_0,\dots,v_r=v_0\). For each traversed dart, choose a covering chart \(i_k\) containing it, including both endpoints. At each junction \(v_k\), switch from the previous dart's chart \(i_{k-1}\) to the next one's chart \(i_k\), using the overlap arrow labeled \(v_k\); omit a switch when the charts coincide. These switches form a closed overlap walk. Local integration and reindexing give

\[
W(\gamma)=\sum_k[p_{i_k}(v_{k+1})-p_{i_k}(v_k)]
=\sum_k[p_{i_{k-1}}(v_k)-p_{i_k}(v_k)]
=\Delta(\eta).
\]

A walk with no switches lies wholly in one chart and has zero period. Thus \(\Lambda_G\subseteq\Lambda_N\), proving part 1. Replacing \(p_i\) by \(p_i+a_i\) adds \(a_i-a_j\) to transitions; these contributions telescope on every overlap loop. Any other admissible cover has the same subgroup by the already proved equality.

**Vanishing is precisely integrability.** A global primitive telescopes to zero on any closed source walk. Conversely assume every closed source walk has zero increment. Choose a root in each connected source component. Assign to \(v\) the increment of a source path from its component root to \(v\). Two paths differ by a closed walk after reversing one, so the assignment is path-independent. Concatenating a path with any dart proves its equation. On each connected \(U_i\), the global primitive minus \(p_i\) is constant \(c_i\), and matching at shared vertices gives \(c_j-c_i=\delta_{ij}(v)\). Conversely such constants yield a matching family \(p_i+c_i\); the sheaf argument glues it globally. In the Čech complex, the last equation is exactly \(\delta=d^0c\); triple-overlap identities already establish that \(\delta\) is a cocycle. This proves part 2 without assuming a nerve theorem or an isomorphism of full homology groups.

**Coefficient maps.** A homomorphism commutes with signed sums and with the two explicit walk constructions. Therefore projected periods generate precisely \(q(\Lambda_G)\). The path-integrability argument works for any abelian coefficient group \(B\): inverses, addition, and zero are all that it uses. Projected connected local primitives are \(q\circ p_i\), so their cover remains valid. This proves part 3. Apply it to each map: all projected systems glue iff every generator of \(\Lambda_G\) belongs to every kernel, equivalently \(\Lambda_G\subseteq K\). A particular period is invisible iff it belongs to \(\Lambda_G\cap K\). This proves part 4.

**Finite-menu witness and minimality.** Use vertices \(u,v\), darts \(a,b:u\to v\), and labels \(w(a)=1\), \(w(b)=1+L\). The charts \(U_a,U_b\) each contain its single dart. Take \(p_a=(0,1)\), \(p_b=(0,1+L)\). Their intersection has the two isolated vertices, with transitions \(0\) at \(u\) and \(-L\) at \(v\). A global primitive would require its single difference \(p(v)-p(u)\) to equal both distinct labels. The closed source walk \(b^{-1}a\) has period \(-L\); the integral source cycle group has rank one, generated by that two-dart loop, so \(\Lambda_G=L\mathbb Z\). Each menu modulus divides \(L\); consequently both labels project to \(1\), giving the projected primitive \((0,1)\). With a single source vertex, every dart is a loop; exact local coverage forces each loop label to be zero. With at most one dart, either it is such a zero loop or it joins two vertices and integrates directly, with other isolated vertices free. With one chart covering the source, its promised local section is global already. Thus two vertices, two darts, and two charts are all minimal in the admitted class. QED.

The subgroup is an obstruction invariant, **not a full classification of marked Čech classes**: opposite marked cycle labels can generate the same subgroup. Equality of period images also does not identify the entire source and overlap cycle spaces; overlap graphs may contain redundant zero-period cycles.

## 8. Remaining obstruction

For the admitted finite affine source, nothing remains unresolved about the global-section decision: a nonzero period is a complete obstruction. What remains unresolved outside this domain is how actual causal observations are certified to refer to the **same stable original source**, especially under destructive instruments, and how those observations acquire this affine primitive semantics. A digest alone does not establish that map.

The theorem also distinguishes two limited observations. Some nonzero periods can be hidden while another is detected; a system receives a wholly false global acceptance only when **all** periods lie in the menu kernel. A further quotient may change the mathematical object while leaving its displayed data consistent. The next discriminating probe for the 1/7 witness is the original integer label difference or mod 5; it separates this witness immediately, but adding any fixed finite modular menu still leaves an LCM alias elsewhere in the unrestricted source class.

## 9. Cross-domain connection with actual maps

The coefficient-loss map is an explicit natural transformation of source-relative primitive systems:

\[
\mathscr P_w(U)\longrightarrow\mathscr P_{q\circ w}(U),\qquad p\longmapsto q\circ p.
\]

For every \(W\subseteq U\), restricting then applying \(q\) equals applying \(q\) then restricting. On darts, \(q(p(h)-p(t))=q(w(e))\); on overlaps, \(q(\delta_{ij})=q(p_i)-q(p_j)\); on closed walks, \(W_q=q\circ W\). These are actual commuting equations. The transformation need not be surjective on global sections: the 1/7 integral source has no global section, while its mod-2 source has one.

At the path-groupoid level, the functor sends a vertex to the additive torsor \(A\), and a dart to translation by \(w(e)\). A global primitive is a natural section of that torsor functor. A coefficient homomorphism sends translation by \(w(e)\) to translation by \(q(w(e))\), respecting composition and inverses. Nonzero regular-translation holonomy has no fixed point; quotient transport can kill it. This gives the precise relation to information loss, without identifying arbitrary probabilistic supports with this torsor.
