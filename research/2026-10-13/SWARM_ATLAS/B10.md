## 3. Proposed SUCC objects

Let (S) be Hausdorff, (I) an arbitrary probe index set, and (A_i) finite
discrete report alphabets. Assume each (q_i:S\to A_i) continuous; define

\[
\Phi(s)=(q_i(s))_{i\in I},\quad Y=\prod_i A_i,\quad
F_E(y)=\{s:\Phi(s)|_E=y|_E\}
\]

for finite (E\subseteq I). Specify a **finite-valued** source cost
(c:S\to[0,\infty)), with compact sublevels (K_R=\{c\le R\}) for every
finite (R\ge0). This is an audited hypothesis, not a consequence of naming a
source physical. Put (m_E(y)=\inf_{s\in F_E(y)}c(s)), with empty infimum
(+\infty), and (L(y)=\sup_{E\text{ finite}}m_E(y)).

## 4. Candidate theorem

**SWM-B10-T1, PROVED LOCAL, classical compactness corollary (paper proof).**
Under exactly these hypotheses, for every (y\in Y),

\[
\boxed{\inf_{\Phi(s)=y}c(s)=L(y).}
\]

When (L(y)<\infty), the left infimum is attained. Consequently

\[
y\in\Phi(S)\iff L(y)<\infty,
\]

and the **exact closed-image criterion** is

\[
\Phi(S)\text{ closed}\iff
\forall y\in\overline{\Phi(S)}\ \exists R_y<\infty\
\forall E\text{ finite}\ \exists s_E\in F_E(y):c(s_E)\le R_y.
\]

The bound is uniform over probes for one profile; it may vary between profiles.
The weaker order (\forall E\exists R_E\exists s_E) does not suffice.

## 5. Proof attempt — completed

Every global realizer belongs to every finite fiber, so its cost is at least
(L(y)). If (L(y)=\infty), finite-valued cost rules out a global realizer,
proving equality with the empty infimum.

Suppose (L=L(y)<\infty). In compact (K_{L+1}), consider all closed sets

\[
F_E(y)\cap K_{L+\varepsilon},\qquad
E\subseteq I\text{ finite},\quad 0<\varepsilon\le1.
\]

They are closed: finite fibers are closed by continuity, and compact sublevels
are closed in Hausdorff (S). For any finite collection, combine the probe
sets into (E_*) and take the least positive (\varepsilon_*). Since
(m_{E_*}(y)\le L<L+\varepsilon_*), the defining infimum supplies a witness
of cost below (L+\varepsilon_*). Thus the collection has the finite
intersection property. Compactness yields one (s) in their total intersection:
it matches every probe and has (c(s)\le L). This proves attainment and equality.

Finite cylinders give
(y\in\overline{\Phi(S)}\iff\forall E\ F_E(y)\ne\varnothing).
Applying the proved equivalence to each such (y) proves the criterion.
No executed infinite observation or computable extraction follows from this
classical existence proof.

## 6. Crucible

**T2, REFUTED generalization:** Polish + locally compact + Baire + sigma compact
does not force closed trace image. Take discrete (S=\mathbb N_{\ge1}),
(U(k)=\max(1,k-1)), (q(k)=\mathbf1_{k=1}). Then

\[
q(U^{n-1}(k))=\mathbf1_{k\le n},\qquad n\ge1.
\]

This source is countable and complete under the discrete metric, hence Polish;
singletons are compact neighborhoods. Every dense open set is the whole space,
so it is Baire. Compact subsets are finite. The zero thread has finite fiber
(\{k>h\}), but no global realizer: probe (n=k) reports one.
With (c(k)=k), (m_h(0)=h+1\to\infty). For any audited cap (R\in\mathbb N),
one probe (n=R) refutes every source in (K_R). These clopen fibers are not
dense, so the [Baire dense-open theorem](https://web.pdx.edu/~erdman/CRA/COMPANION_ANALYSIS_pdf.pdf)
does not apply.

The same phantom lifts continuously to the standard Polish Baire space
(\mathbb N_{\ge1}^{\mathbb N}) by using its first coordinate. No finite-valued
compact-sublevel cost exists on that entire space: for any proposed compact
cover (C_j), coordinate (j)'s projection is finite; choose (x_j) outside
it. Then (x\notin\bigcup_jC_j), contradicting sigma compactness. A mere
first-coordinate bound leaves all other coordinates uncontrolled.
