## Original question and existing context

Which conditions ensure that an ideal compatible future profile is realized by
one original source, beyond the finite-source and compact-source cases?
VOYAGER V2a already gives the exact condition: closed trace image. This mission
seeks a source-typed completeness criterion and tests weaker proposed substitutes.
The background is standard complete-subspace and compact-image topology.
Kechris, *Classical Descriptive Set Theory* (1995), publisher record and chapter
inventory: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-4190-4 . This is prior-art context,
not a claim that the particular SUCC criterion has independent priority.

## Objects and quantified theorem

Let W be a countable probe family with a **complete** enumeration w_0,w_1,...,
O a finite discrete alphabet, S any nonempty declared source class, and
Phi(s)(n)=q(U_{w_n}(s)). Set s~t iff Phi(s)=Phi(t). On Q=S/~ define

    d([s],[t]) = 0                         if Phi(s)=Phi(t),
              = 2^(-min{n:Phi(s)(n)!=Phi(t)(n)}) otherwise.

The alphabet need only be discrete and complete for the metric equivalence;
finiteness adds compactness. The profile metric on O^N is complete. Then the
following are equivalent:

1. Every profile z such that each finite coordinate set is matched by some s
   is matched at all coordinates by one s.
2. Phi(S) is closed in the profile product space.
3. The observational quotient metric space (Q,d) is complete.

Quantifiers in (1) are
`forall z, (forall finite F, exists s, forall n in F, Phi(s)(n)=z(n)) ->
exists s, forall n, Phi(s)(n)=z(n)`.
No original source topology is assumed for the equivalence.

## Proof

A finite-coordinate cylinder is a product-topology neighborhood basis. Thus
finite compatibility is precisely z in closure(Phi(S)), proving (1)<->(2).
The map [s] -> Phi(s) is well-defined, injective, onto Phi(S), and an isometry
by the definition of d. The ambient stream space is complete: in a Cauchy
sequence every fixed coordinate eventually stabilizes, giving a coordinatewise
limit; agreement in the first h coordinates proves metric convergence.
A closed subspace of a complete metric space is complete. Conversely, if a
complete subspace Y contains a convergent ambient sequence y_n->z, then (y_n)
is Cauchy in Y; completeness gives y in Y, and uniqueness of metric limits gives
y=z. For every closure point z choose y_n agreeing in the first n coordinates,
so completeness implies closedness. This proves (2)<->(3).

**Additional sufficient condition.** If S carries a complete metric d_S, Phi is
continuous, injective, and `d_S(s,t)<=C*d(Phi(s),Phi(t))` for a fixed finite C,
then Phi(S) is closed. Lift a Cauchy image sequence, use the displayed bound,
complete S, and continuity. Completeness of an unrelated source metric alone
is insufficient; the quantitative inverse condition is load-bearing.

## Crucible and minimal countermodel

Take S to be *all finite-support binary streams* with the discrete metric,
q=head, U=left shift. This countable discrete space is complete, Polish,
locally compact, and Baire; every function from it is continuous. It is closed
under U. Phi is the inclusion into Cantor space. Every finite binary transcript
has a finite-support S witness, but the all-one profile is absent. Hence all
four source regularity labels and even source-metric completeness fail to
force actualization. The observational quotient metric is incomplete.

`PartialEvidence`, `patch_tail(evidence,(0,))` implement exact witnesses at
arbitrary scattered coordinates. Replacing the source with *all* binary
streams is the compact positive control: Phi is identity and every profile
already belongs to S. Finite complete Moore sources are a second positive
control. Removing complete probe enumeration can also identify extra states;
then the theorem concerns only that smaller observational quotient.

## Obstruction, bridge, and hole contract

The missing source condition is observational completeness, not a claim that
"physical", "Polish", or "Baire" means globally complete. The map from B12's
partial information ideal is union followed by total-profile embedding;
actualization is the additional requirement that this profile lie in Phi(S).
The map to B15 is the final-trace quotient Q; d metrizes precisely its finite
behavioral approximations. The next falsifier is a concrete proposed inverse
regularity bound that fails on two arbitrarily close traces with separated
source states. No probability or physical preparation certificate is supplied.
