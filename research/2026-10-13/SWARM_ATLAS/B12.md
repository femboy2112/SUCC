## 3. Proposed SUCC objects

Let $S\ne\varnothing$, $Q$ be any set of authorized original-state query contexts, and $O\ne\varnothing$ a **finite discrete** alphabet. Declare readouts $\Phi:S\to O^Q$, $C=\Phi(S)$. A record is a partial function $r:D\to O$, $D\subseteq Q$, ordered by graph inclusion. Define

\[
R(C)=\{r:\exists c\in C,\ r\subseteq c\},\qquad
V(r)=\{s:r\subseteq\Phi(s)\}.
\]

These are possible evidence descriptions, not permissions to perform every context. More information shrinks $V$. Graphs omit ownership/causal receipts; those remain separate required metadata.

## 4. Candidate theorem

**B12-T1 — PROVED LOCAL, classical specialization.** For every such contract, the following are equivalent:

1. $C$ is closed in the product topology of $O^Q$.
2. Every partial $r$ whose finite restrictions lie in $R(C)$ lies in $R(C)$.
3. $R(C)$ is closed under nonempty directed unions.
4. $R(C)$ is a dcpo.

When these hold, joins are unions; finite records are exactly the compact elements; $R(C)$ is pointed, algebraic and bounded-complete. Its Scott topology has basis $\uparrow f$ for finite $f\in R(C)$. Source realization remains existential; source identity and executable access do not follow.

## 5. Proof attempt

**Complete proof.** (1⇒2): finite alphabet compactness makes closed $C$ compact. The closed cylinders $\{c\in C:c(q)=r(q)\}$, $q\in D$, have the finite-intersection property, hence intersect. (2⇒3): directed graphs agree on overlaps; every finite subset of their union lies in one member, hence is realizable. (3⇒4): union is the least upper bound. (4⇒1): if $z\in\overline C$, all finite restrictions of total $z$ belong to $R(C)$ and form a directed family. Its supremum must extend their union $z$, hence equal $z$; thus $z\in C$.

For any directed family with an upper bound in $R(C)$, its union is already realizable by that bound's source. A finite $f\subseteq\bigcup D$ lies in some member by directedness, proving compactness. Every record is the directed union of its finite restrictions; if a record is compact, applying compactness to that family makes it finite. Any bounded family has realizable union, proving bounded completeness. Basic upsets are Scott-open by compactness; Scott-inaccessibility applied to finite restrictions shows every open is their union.

**B12-T2 — PROVED LOCAL / CLASSICAL.** For any token set $T$ and rules $A\Rightarrow b$ with finite $A\subseteq T$, $b\in T$, let $F(X)=X\cup\{b:\exists A\subseteq X, A\Rightarrow b\}$. Then $F$ preserves directed unions: every finite enabled premise lies in one member. Its least closure above $X$, $H(X)=\bigcup_{n<\omega}F^n(X)$, is a fixed point by continuity and least by induction. Each derived token has a finite proof tree, hence finite input support, so $H$ also preserves directed unions. If source truths $T_s\subseteq T$ satisfy $A\subseteq T_s\Rightarrow b\in T_s$ for every rule, induction gives $X\subseteq T_s\Rightarrow H(X)\subseteq T_s$. Inferred tokens never become actual observation events.

## 6. Crucible

**Nonclosed source:** eventually-zero binary streams realize every finite restriction of the all-ones record, using finite-support witnesses, but no original stream realizes its union. Thus their $R(C)$ lacks a supremum. This independently reconstructs VOYAGER's countermodel in information order.

**Source substitution:** start `IdealJetFrame` from polynomial zero; `succ(x)` accepts the compatible replacement polynomial $x$ and returns jet $(0,1)$. The record has an original-class realizer, but not the actual initial zero source. Existential compatibility cannot authenticate continuity of physical provenance. This is a limitation, not a claimed module defect.

**Alphabet mutation:** for $O=\mathbb N$, set $c_m(0)=m$, $c_m(n)=1$ for $1\le n\le m$, and zero afterward. $C=\{c_m\}$ is closed: coordinate zero fixes $m$, then a mismatching coordinate separates any outsider. Yet partial records $r_h(n)=1$, $1\le n\le h$, are individually realizable and their union has no realizer. Finite alphabet compactness is load-bearing.

**Infinitary mutation:** adding seal only when all naturals are present gives $F(\bigcup_n[0,n))=\mathbb N\cup\{seal\}\ne\bigcup_nF([0,n))=\mathbb N$. Monotonicity alone licenses no finite-premise continuity theorem.

## 9. Cross-domain connection with actual maps

Let $B$ be the finite records in $R(C)$. Its ideal completion is exactly the set $E(C)$ of finitely realizable partial records: $I\mapsto\bigcup I$, with inverse $r\mapsto\{f\in B:f\subseteq r\}$. Directedness prevents conflicts and puts every finite union subset in one member; downward closure gives the inverse identity. This works even when $C$ is nonclosed. Then $E(C)$ contains formally consistent partial evidence unsupported by any single original source; B12-T1 states exactly when $E(C)=R(C)$.

For a declared context translation $t:J\to Q$, let $C_t=\{c\circ t:c\in C\}$ and
\[
\pi_t(r)(j)=r(t(j))\quad(t(j)\in\operatorname{dom}r).
\]
If $C$ is closed, $C_t$ is compact/closed, so both record domains are dcpos. Coordinatewise, $\pi_t(\bigcup D)=\bigcup\pi_t(D)$; thus this map is Scott-continuous and $\pi_t\Phi(s)=\Phi_J(s)$. It obeys $\pi_u\pi_t=\pi_{t\circ u}$. Translation preserves the same supplied source through actual maps; source substitution needs a separately attested commuting map.
