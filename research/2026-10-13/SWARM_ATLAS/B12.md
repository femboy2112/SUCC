## Original question and existing mathematical context

Can partial evidence have legitimate ideal limits without silently inventing
a complete source? Classical domain theory supplies the appropriate object.
Primary author/publication record: Abramsky and Jung, *Domain Theory*, Handbook
of Logic in Computer Science, Oxford University Press, 1994, pages 1–168:
https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/publications/publication1830-abstract.html .
The author's full manuscript link is
https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/samson.abramsky/handbook.pdf ; retrieval timed out,
so no particular page theorem is claimed read. The arguments below are
self-contained standard flat-domain proofs.

## Objects and candidate theorem

Fix a probe address set W and a discrete report alphabet O. Let D be all
partial maps W ⇀ O, ordered by extension. Its finite-support elements K are
finite evidence reports. Two reports are compatible iff they assign identical
values wherever both are defined. Directed means nonempty, and every two
members have a common upper bound *inside the family*.

**Theorem.** D is an algebraic directed-complete partial order. A directed
family has supremum given by union. Its compact elements are precisely the
finite-support maps. Every nonempty downward-closed directed ideal I of K
is exactly the family of finite restrictions of its union partial map.
The coordinate-shift update on W=N, `U(p)(n)=p(n+1)` wherever defined, is
Scott-continuous and preserves directed suprema.

## Proof

For a directed family, two assignments to a shared address agree because
some common upper bound extends both. Their union is therefore a function,
extends each member, and is below every common upper bound. If finite p lies
below a directed supremum, each of its finitely many addresses is supplied
by one member; directedness combines these members at a common finite step.
Thus p is compact. Conversely an infinite-support p is the union of its
directed finite restrictions but is below none of them; p is not compact.
Every p is that directed union, giving algebraicity.

For an ideal I, any finite restriction q of union(I) obtains each address from
some member of I. Directedness combines finitely many such members, and
downward closure puts q in I. Every member of I is obviously a finite
restriction of its union, proving the representation and its uniqueness.
Shift is monotone and commutes with unions address by address, hence is
Scott-continuous. General fixed reindexing maps have the same proof.

## Completion boundary and crucible

A directed chain need not cover W: observing only even addresses has a
partial ideal limit whose odd addresses remain undefined. Even total union
need not correspond to one original source: every initial all-one prefix has
an eventual-zero witness but their union is all-one. This is B10's nonclosed
source image; the domain construction did not manufacture a source state.

The subset of **total** maps is not Scott-open when W is infinite and O is
nonempty: a total map lies in it and is the directed supremum of its finite
restrictions, none total. The same obstruction kills a universal finite
certificate for totality in this evidence-only domain.

Individual source-compatible reports need not admit a source-compatible join:
source profiles are exactly (0,1) and (1,0). The evidence {0↦0} and {1↦0}
is separately realizable and their ordinary partial-map join is consistent,
but the combined evidence (0,0) has no source witness. Code-level conflict
checking is consequently weaker than source-relative admissibility.
Our `join` tests only information consistency and makes no source claim.

Executable controls test join/shift commutation, nonmonotone conflicting
reports, finite/cofinite/sparse completions of arbitrary scattered evidence,
and sorted unique address validation. There is no default report at an
unobserved address in `PartialEvidence`; `patch_tail` returns an explicitly
hypothetical `LassoStream`, a different type.

## Remaining obstruction and lawful bridge

Domain-theoretic continuity handles **fixed** transport maps. A probe update
that changes its source/basis or retrospectively retracts a report needs
provenance-indexed domains and a lawful comparison, not ordinary extension.
Map finite causal receipts to a partial report only after fixing source and
basis and rejecting conflicting assignments; union is then an information
limit. Map a **total** ideal union to B09's profile, then apply B10's closed-image
criterion separately. These actual maps preserve neither original source
existence nor physical performance without those extra premises.
Next discriminator: a source-changing report pair with the same address and
different values should be typed into different source contexts, not joined.
