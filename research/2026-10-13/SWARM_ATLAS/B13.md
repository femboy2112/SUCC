## 5. Proof attempt

Take any finite union proof. Each syntax declaration, axiom/evidence leaf and
used rule instance occurs at some stage. There are finitely many assets, so their
birth indices have a maximum s. Nestedness places all assets in `C_s`. Induct
over the proof tree: leaves remain licensed, and each internal inference retains
its identical premises, rule and conclusion. Thus the whole proof transports.
Conversely, inclusion transports every finite-stage proof to the union. This
proves both directions without soundness, compactness, or performed infinity.

For the guard, a union contradiction would transport to a consistent finite
stage. Dovetailing the uniform stage enumerators enumerates the union axioms.
Here is the conventional second-incompleteness argument. Write `Pr` for T's
provability predicate and choose `T |- G <-> not Pr(G)`. Necessitation and
internalized modus ponens yield `Pr(G) -> Pr(not Pr(G))`; positive introspection
yields `Pr(G) -> Pr(Pr(G))`. Formalized propositional contradiction therefore
gives `Pr(G) -> Pr(false)`. Consequently `Con(T) -> not Pr(G) -> G` is provable.
If T proved Con(T), it would prove G, hence both Pr(G) by necessitation and its
negation by the fixed point, contradicting consistency. These coding hypotheses
are load-bearing; the finite monitor does not implement this proof system.
