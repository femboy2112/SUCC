# C20 — Blackwell experimental sufficiency and its carrier boundary

## 1. Original question

When does one finite experiment suffice to simulate another for an observer
addressing the **same original source**, and does that suffice to reuse policies
on a mutable carrier? Steelman: sufficiency means exact reproduction of every
finite randomized decision law, with no hidden-source input to the decoder.
The classical readout theorem survives; the extension to state-changing,
action-preserving histories fails. This dossier makes both statements explicit.

## 2. Existing mathematical context

Blackwell compares experiments indexed by common possible distributions and
their attainable risks in [Comparison of Experiments (1951), pp. 93–102](https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/nanna/record/112749/files/math_s2_article-08.pdf?registerDownload=1&version=1&withMetadata=0&withWatermark=0).
The finite decision/garbling equivalence belongs to classical comparison theory;
see also [Equivalent Comparisons of Experiments (1953), DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177729032).
The 1951 primary indexed excerpt was accessible; direct archive/PDF retrieval
returned 403 and the 1953 full text was inaccessible here.
[Fritz–Gonda–Perrone–Rischel (2023), §5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2010.07416v3#S5.SS1)
gives actual garbling maps and explicitly separates one-experiment comparison
from a joint implementation. These are literature support, not a calibration of
SUCC's sources. No scholarly novelty is claimed.

## 3. Proposed SUCC objects

Let finite nonempty \(\Theta,Y,Z\) be declared original-source and report sets.
Stipulate rational row-stochastic channels \(E:\Theta\to Y\),
\(F:\Theta\to Z\). A garbling \(K:Y\to Z\) must be source-independent:
\(F_{\theta z}=\sum_yE_{\theta y}K_{yz}\), **for every** \(\theta,z\).
All randomness is classical. A source's support alone supplies no likelihoods.

A physical-model instrument has the richer type
\(I_a:C\to Y_a\times C\), with an explicitly initialized carrier
\(i:\Theta\to C\); its readout marginal discards the next carrier. The immutable
address \(\theta\) is bookkeeping for the target, never observable input to a
policy. Laws and preparation are mathematical premises, not empirical facts.

## 4. Candidate theorem

**CLASSICAL, reconstructed finite rational form.** For every such \(E,F\), the
following are equivalent:

1. Some rational stochastic \(K\) satisfies \(F=EK\).
2. For every finite nonempty decision set \(A\) and rational stochastic
   \(D:Z\to A\), some rational stochastic \(L:Y\to A\) satisfies \(EL=FD\).
3. For every rational prior \(\pi\) on \(\Theta\) and rational reward table
   \(u:\Theta\times A\to\mathbb Q\), the maximum expected reward using \(E\)
   is at least that using \(F\), over randomized decisions.

Statement 2 concerns equality of source-conditional laws, not a chosen score.
The equivalent loss formulation reverses the optimization inequality.

## 5. Proof attempt

\(1\Rightarrow2\): take \(L=KD\). Nonnegativity, unit row sums and associativity
give \(EL=EKD=FD\). \(2\Rightarrow1\): choose \(A=Z,D=\mathrm{id}_Z\).
Composition also proves \(1\Rightarrow3\).

For \(3\Rightarrow1\), let \(P=\{EG:G:Y\to Z\text{ stochastic}\}\).
It is the convex hull of finitely many matrices \(EG\) with deterministic
\(G\). If \(F\notin P\), finite-dimensional strict separation gives
\(\langle H,F\rangle>\max_{M\in P}\langle H,M\rangle\).
Because the gap is strict and the vertex list finite, approximate \(H\) by a
rational matrix without closing the gap. Take uniform
\(\pi_\theta=1/|\Theta|\), \(A=Z\), and
\(u(\theta,z)=|\Theta|H_{\theta z}\). Identity decisions under \(F\) attain
\(\langle H,F\rangle\); every \(E\)-decision lies in \(P\), contradicting 3.
If a real garbling exists, its feasible set is a nonempty bounded rational
polytope and has a rational vertex. Thus the rational requirement is preserved.
This is a mathematical derivation using classical separation/polytope facts;
the probe below does not prove those universal facts.

## 6. Crucible

Positive map:
\[
E=\begin{pmatrix}3/4&1/4\\1/4&3/4\end{pmatrix},\quad
K=\begin{pmatrix}2/3&1/3\\1/3&2/3\end{pmatrix},\quad
EK=F=\begin{pmatrix}7/12&5/12\\5/12&7/12\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both channels have identical full supports, yet reverse garbling is impossible:
their row total variations are \(1/2\) and \(1/6\), and stochastic maps contract
total variation. Equal supports therefore cannot establish equal informativeness.

Noncomparability: \(\Theta=\{00,01,10,11\}\), \(E(a,b)=a\), \(F(a,b)=b\).
Equal E-rows at 00/01 would have to become unequal F-rows; the reverse fails at
00/10. Under the stipulated uniform prior, guessing a gives values \(1,1/2\);
guessing b gives \(1/2,1\). These explicit decision witnesses oppose each order.

Minimal temporal control: \(C=\Theta=\{0,1\}\), initialized at \(c=\theta\).
Both machines have always-enabled named actions a,b. Keeping gives
\(I_a(c)=\delta_{(*,c)}\); erasing gives \(I_a(c)=\delta_{(*,0)}\).
Both give \(I_b(c)=\delta_{(c,c)}\). Every initial single-action readout agrees,
but word ab produces \((*,\theta)\) versus \((*,0)\). Erased histories have equal
rows for both sources; no source-independent postprocessor can recover unequal
kept rows. Even randomization and remembered public outputs cannot repair this.

The simulator contract preserves action order and forbids extra probes, hidden
source access and reset. Reordering b before a changes that contract. Two source
values and horizon two are minimal for this hidden-future distinction; no
minimality claim is made for the number of action names. Stipulated source-faithful
re-preparation after a restores the kept histories, and is a positive control.

## 7. Verdict

**SURVIVES / CLASSICAL:** common-original-source readout garbling exactly
characterizes decision-law simulation and unrestricted finite reward dominance.
**FAILS / REFUTED:** initial readout dominance certifies action-preserving
carrier/history simulation or every reusable policy without reset. The ab
countercontrol is the receipt; a fixed word already defeats the universal
adaptive-policy claim. **TESTED MODEL:** exact finite controls pass.

## 8. Remaining obstruction

Neither support declarations nor stochastic tables authenticate a physical
source or calibrate noise. Next falsification: supply a proposed stochastic
carrier simulation and check its initial-source, enabledness, output and
next-carrier squares on every reachable state. A successful one-step proof must
then survive the ab control. Resource costs, approximate dominance, correlated
preparations and unbounded protocols remain outside this result.

## 9. Cross-domain connection with actual maps

requires \(h(T^R_a(c))=T^K_a(h(c))\) and matching outcomes/menus.
Taking \(h=\mathrm{id}\) in the countercontrol gives \(0\ne1\) at c=1.
