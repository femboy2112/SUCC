# SWM-C21 — Exact finite-horizon original-target policies without resets

## 1. Original question
How can stochastic adaptive policies optimize finite observation cost while respecting original/current separation, destructive actions and the actual action menu?

## 2. Existing mathematical context
R. D. Smallwood and E. J. Sondik, *The Optimal Control of Partially Observable Markov Processes over a Finite Horizon*, Operations Research 21 (1973), 1071–1088, [primary publisher](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.21.5.1071). Finite-horizon belief-state dynamic programming is classical. Repository `adaptive_proof_policy.py`, originating commit `5483f077745cdf31f363ef0da6b96d214188503b`, solves the deterministic AND/OR finite reachability game; it is unmodified.

## 3. Proposed SUCC objects
A normalized rational J(o,x|h), finite original target t:O→T, finite total instrument menu with declared carrier spaces, nonnegative rational costs and a fixed finite horizon n. Policies choose actions using public history only. Terminal certification requires all positive-mass original labels to share t; omitted zero-prior labels are outside this probabilistic certificate. No reset or free source-specific action-menu observation is added.

## 4. Candidate theorem
For every finite such model and horizon n, a minimum-expected-cost deterministic adaptive policy certifying the original target on **every positive-probability branch by n** exists whenever any such policy exists. Its value is

\[
V_0(J)=0\text{ on terminal beliefs, otherwise }+\infty;
\quad V_n(J)=\min_a\{c(a)+\sum_r p(r|J,a)V_{n-1}(J_{a,r})\}
\]

on nonterminal beliefs, with impossible outcomes omitted and unavailable carrier types excluded. A chosen action is feasible only if all its positive branches have finite continuation value. The code uses `None` for +∞; budget failure raises an exception. Randomization over public actions cannot improve this minimum.

## 5. Proof attempt
Induct on n. Terminal beliefs require zero further cost. At a nonterminal belief every deterministic root action has expected cost c(a) plus the weighted costs of its conditional continuation policies. The induction hypothesis optimizes each branch, independently because their records differ; there are finitely many root actions and each finite-depth outcome tree is finite. Taking their minimum proves attainment and optimality. A randomized root action is a convex combination of deterministic root costs and cannot beat their minimum; induction removes randomization at subsequent nodes. This result does not assert a finite bound or optimality for unrestricted infinite-horizon policies.

**All-horizon no-reset obstruction:** Let destructive A report a and erase b; destructive B report b and erase a. If the first action is A, each observed a admits originals (a,0),(a,1) with opposite parity and identical current state. Future histories have identical conditional laws by induction on instruments. The B-first case is symmetric. Any adaptive continuation therefore fails original parity certification; root randomization also fails on whichever action is selected. This proves the impossibility for all finite terminating policies, beyond the finite test horizons.

## 6. Crucible
Exact controls: destructive parity infeasible at horizons 0,1,2,4,7; preserving A then B has cost 2. A conditional reversible Q reports a and replaces b by a XOR b; choose R0 or R1 from Q's observed a to read parity. The adaptive two-step policy succeeds, while an independent exhaustive replay of all nine fixed two-action words fails.

Expected cost differs from worst-case rank: priors (1/2,1/4,1/4), targets (0,0,1), a cost-1 probe isolates state 0 and a cost-3 probe reads all states. The two-step optimum is **5/2**, while a one-step optimum costs 3. Full-support noisy readouts retain both target labels after every finite history, so no finite zero-error certificate exists despite possible statistical concentration.

## 7. Verdict
**PROVED LOCAL**, with classical finite-horizon control attribution, a general all-finite-policy no-reset proof and exact finite tests. Probabilistic positive-support certainty is not source-universal deductive proof on zero-prior or omitted alternatives. No Lean check.

## 8. Remaining obstruction
Almost-sure eventual termination can have no bounded horizon and is not decided by a finite-horizon failure. Worst-case adversarial source completeness needs explicit nonzero support or the deterministic source-universal solver. Partial enabled menus require an added common-availability contract; this implementation only uses total kernels.

## 9. Cross-domain connection
Dirac instruments embed the existing deterministic solver into the stochastic law model; use a strictly positive prior on every admitted original state to recover its zero-error source domain. C20 shows why a one-step Blackwell relation does not transport policies under destructive backaction. The J update from C17 is the exact state sufficient for the Bellman recursion.
