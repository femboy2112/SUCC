## 2. Existing mathematical context

Strong planning guarantees termination against nondeterminism; strong cyclic planning additionally uses execution assumptions. This distinction is classical, as documented by [Cimatti–Pistore–Roveri–Traverso](https://iris.unitn.it/handle/11572/74586). [Brihaye–Geeraerts–Haddad–Monmege](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.5030) study min-cost reachability, explicitly charge nonreaching plays infinity, and recover the classical reachability attractor. Their arbitrary-weight games require additional strategy qualifications. The source/current bookkeeping below specializes classical planning; repository novelty establishes no academic priority.

## 3. Proposed SUCC objects

Let finite nonempty sets $S,X,A,O$ denote original sources, current hidden carrier states, actions, and outcomes. Fix $t:S\to L$, arbitrary nonempty $B_0\subseteq S\times X$, stationary enabled sets $E_a\subseteq S\times X$, and stationary relations

$$
R_a\subseteq S\times X\times O\times X.
$$

Every enabled pair has at least one successor. Originals never change: $(s,x)\mapsto(s,x')$. Any persistent latent calibration/source mode must be included in these coordinates; future restrictions cannot depend on omitted history. A public-history policy may choose $a$ only when $B\subseteq E_a$. Menus and actual costs are not extra observations. Only $(a,o)$ is recorded, and

$$
U_{a,o}(B)=\{(s,x'):\exists x,\ (s,x)\in B,\ (s,x,o,x')\in R_a\}.
$$

Terminal means $t$ is constant on the original projection of $B$. Costs $k(s,x,a,o,x')$ are finite, stationary, nonnegative real numbers; they may remain private and correlated.

## 4. Candidate theorem

**C21a — PROVED LOCAL, classical structure.** On the finite reachable belief set $\mathcal B$, let $W_0$ be terminals and

$$
W_{n+1}=W_n\cup\{B:\exists a\text{ safe},\
\forall o,\ U_{a,o}(B)\ne\varnothing\Rightarrow U_{a,o}(B)\in W_n\}.
$$

There exists a deterministic public-history policy terminating soundly on every compatible hidden execution iff $B_0\in W_*$. First-entry rank is the minimum worst-case number of actions. A rank-decreasing belief policy suffices.

**C21b — PROVED LOCAL.** If $B_0\in W_*$, the minimum worst-case total *actual* cost among guaranteed terminating deterministic public-history policies is attained by a finite policy tree.

## 5. Proof attempt

For C21a, induction identifies $W_n$ exactly with policies of depth at most $n$: choose a safe first action and winning continuations for every nonempty outcome branch. Conversely, any bounded policy supplies that same decomposition. A policy terminating on every execution has a finite public tree: otherwise finite branching gives an infinite outcome branch by König's lemma. Every prefix is compatible with a hidden path; finite hidden branching gives one consistent infinite hidden execution with a single fixed original source. Thus no adversary secretly switches originals. Rank witnesses prove sufficiency; outside the fixed point, every safe action has an outside branch, proving necessity.

For C21b, a rank policy supplies a finite upper bound $C$. The transition cost alphabet $K$ is finite. If positive costs exist, put $\delta=\min(K\setminus\{0\})>0$. Any path of cost at most $C$ has at most $\lfloor C/\delta\rfloor$ positive-cost transitions. Consequently the set of possible sums at most $C$ is finite, including when zeros repeat arbitrarily. If all costs vanish it is $\{0\}$. Every finite policy has finitely many compatible hidden paths, so its worst cost is one such sum. The nonempty set of achievable policy values at most $C$ therefore has a minimum, achieved by an actual finite policy. Nontermination receives $+\infty$, even at zero cost.

For the stronger public-cost specialization $k=c(B,a,o)$, erase repeated beliefs in finite policy trees: replace the earlier occurrence's subtree by the later subtree. Stationarity preserves admissibility; the removed public prefix has nonnegative cost, so worst cost cannot increase. Repetition-free paths have depth below $|\mathcal B|$. Finite-horizon dynamic programming therefore attains the public-cost optimum. This argument does not authorize the same erasure with private accumulated-cost correlations.

## 6. Crucible

Exact receipts retain these hostile controls:

* Destructive two-bit parity loses; nondestructive, joint, and conditional adaptive controls have ranks 2, 1, and 2.
* Correlated prior originals $\{00,11\}$ certify parity at rank 0; full-source destruction still loses. Original/current uncertainty can contain multiple currents per original; collapsing them to a diagonal is invalid.
* A zero-cost wait and a read costing $1/3$ have proper value $1/3$. The scalar Bellman equation $v=\min(v,1/3)$ also accepts zero, which certifies no completion. Adversarial perpetual retry loses despite an available successful outcome.
* Forced actions A then B have hidden costs $(9,0)$ for one original and $(0,9)$ for another. Both actual totals are 9; separate edge maxima incorrectly produce 18.
* Shortest completion costs 5 while a two-action completion costs 1: minimum rank is not minimum cost.

Dropping hypotheses is sharp: infinitely many revealing actions costing $1/n$, or time-dependent revealing cost after $n$ waits, have unattained infimum zero. A negative-cost loop followed by revelation has no finite minimum.

## 8. Remaining obstruction

Ordinary beliefs suffice for winning and minimum depth. Private-cost optimization additionally needs accumulated possibility costs; a stationary optimum on ordinary beliefs is not established here. Arbitrary real costs yield mathematical attainment, not an executable comparison oracle. Rational tables admit the exact construction below. Finite search does not promote source-table fidelity.

## 9. Cross-domain connection with actual maps

The reduction to a turn-based reachability game is explicit: controller node $B$ chooses safe $a$, adversary node $(B,a)$ chooses a nonempty $o$, then reaches $U_{a,o}(B)$. Terminal predicates become the game's target set.

For hidden costs, lift public history to $d_B:S\times X\rightharpoonup\mathbb R_{\ge0}$, the maximum accumulated cost of compatible paths ending at each pair. Initially $d=0$; transport is

$$
d'(s,x')=\max_{x:(s,x)\in B,\ (s,x,o,x')\in R_a}
             [d(s,x)+k(s,x,a,o,x')].
$$

The controller computes the whole map from public history; it never observes which pair or actual cost occurred. At threshold $C$, reject any action with an overflowing compatible branch; never discard over-budget worlds. Entries come from finitely many sums, giving a finite budget-lift game. Its winning terminal policies are exactly original policies costing at most $C$. Minimize over candidate sums.
