# C23 — Conditional evidence wealth on causal public histories

## 1. Original question

Can finite observers accumulate source-relative evidence while choosing probes and stopping adaptively, including destructive interventions? Steelman: obtain calibrated error control for a declared probabilistic law, while preserving the `observation → history → semantics → proof` boundaries. This is a probe-gap concerning conditioning and provenance, not a missing universal truth oracle.

## 2. Existing mathematical context

Likelihood-ratio test martingales and Ville bounds are CLASSICAL; see [Shafer–Shen–Vereshchagin–Vovk (2011), §3–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0912.4269). [Ramdas–Grünwald–Vovk–Shafer (2023)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.01948) surveys anytime-valid inference. An e-process is adapted, nonnegative and has expectation at most one at every stopping time under each null law; it need not itself be a supermartingale ([Ramdas–Ruf–Larsson–Koolen, §1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.00630)). Repository novelty establishes no academic novelty.

## 3. Proposed SUCC objects

Fix finite source/state/action/outcome sets, public filtration \(\mathcal F_t=\sigma(A_1,Y_1,\ldots,A_t,Y_t)\), declared source/version/basis, and null class \(\mathcal P_0\). A policy chooses admissible \(A_{t+1}\) from public history; common randomized policies include their public randomization and use the same policy law under both hypotheses. Hidden-state access is excluded.

For destructive instruments retain original source \(S\), current state \(X_t\), hypothesis-specific filter \(b_{i,t}(s,x)\), and normalized instrument kernel \(J_{i,a}(y,x'\mid s,x)\). The source-current law is

\[
p_i(y\mid h,a)=\sum_{s,x,x'}b_{i,t}(s,x)J_{i,a}(y,x'\mid s,x),\qquad
b_{i,t+1}(s,x')=\frac{\sum_x b_{i,t}(s,x)J_{i,a}(y,x'\mid s,x)}{p_i(y\mid h,a)}.
\]

The filter exists on positive-probability histories; unreachable histories require no arbitrary division. Actions must be admissible on every supported current state. Stored source history remains separate from the changed carrier.

## 4. Candidate theorem

**CLASSICAL, scoped theorem.** For every \(P\in\mathcal P_0\), assume adapted nonnegative factors \(e_{t+1}\) satisfy
\(E_P[e_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t]\le1\) almost surely. Then \(M_0=1\), \(M_t=\prod_{j\le t}e_j\) is a test supermartingale, and for every \(0<\alpha<1\),

\[
P\{\sup_{t\ge0}M_t\ge1/\alpha\}\le\alpha.
\]

For a simple null, use \(e_{t+1}=p_1(Y_{t+1}\mid h,a)/p_0(Y_{t+1}\mid h,a)\) on null support. If \(p_1\ll p_0\) at each reachable history, conditional mean equals one. Otherwise its supported mean is at most one. Multiplication requires conditional validity; marginal e-values alone are insufficient ([Vovk–Wang, sequential merging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.06382)). Independence is sufficient in appropriate models, never the necessary premise. A simple-null ratio does not automatically test a composite null.

## 5. Proof attempt

Pull the measurable \(M_t\) outside conditional expectation:
\(E_P[M_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t]=M_tE_P[e_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t]\le M_t\).
Induction gives integrability. For first threshold hit \(T\), bounded stopping at \(T\wedge n\) yields
\(1\ge E_P[M_{T\wedge n}]\ge\alpha^{-1}P(T\le n)\).
Monotone limits give the ever-hit bound; applying this to thresholds below \(1/\alpha\) and taking their limit covers a supremum that is never attained. Nonnegativity and Fatou give \(E_P[M_\tau]\le1\) for unbounded stopping, taking \(M_\infty\) as the almost-sure supermartingale limit when needed. Unbounded stopping does not guarantee equality; uniform integrability or another valid equality hypothesis is additional.

## 6. Crucible

**REFUTED: blind products remain calibrated.** One latent bit has \(P_0(yes)=1/2\), \(P_1(yes)=3/4\); five reports copy it. Naive wealth on yes is \((3/2)^5=243/32\), crossing threshold five with null probability \(1/2>\alpha=1/5\). Its null mean is \(61/16\). Correct subsequent conditional laws are deterministic and identical, so every further factor is one and wealth remains \(3/2\). Fresh independent trials instead cross with exact probability \(1/16\).

A partially correlated joint control gives the second yes factor \(10/9\), while marginal reuse has conditional mean \(5/4>1\). Reading then resetting a binary carrier makes the next read deterministic, even when public outcomes choose between ordinary/inverted readouts; stale original sensor laws give mean \(3/2>1\), whereas the current-state factor is one. A separate native adaptive tree changes probes after a no outcome and checks the bound exactly.

**Zero support:** \(p_0=p_1=(1,0)\) permits factor one on its supported outcome. For \(p_0=(1,0),p_1=(1/2,1/2)\), supported factor mean is \(1/2\). A null-zero observed outcome triggers a null-support contradiction/contract review, not numerical division. The native `lr_step` conservatively rejects both examples because it requires full support; this dossier preserves that code.

## 8. Remaining obstruction

Correct tables do not authenticate the outside source, instrument or hypothesis class. A crossing controls false rejection under the specified law; it neither assigns posterior probability to that law nor proves an alternative true. Inverting calibrated tests can give simultaneous coverage for declared parameters, still conditional on law validity. Finite noisy full-support histories leave rival possibilities; universal mathematical proof requires independently justified axioms and derivation, not a confidence threshold.

## 9. Cross-domain connection with actual maps

For a common stochastic reporting channel \(K(z\mid h)\), finite laws satisfy \(P^K(z)=\sum_hP(h)K(z\mid h)\), similarly \(Q^K\). When \(Q\ll P\),

\[
\frac{Q^K(z)}{P^K(z)}=E_P[L(H)\mid Z=z],\qquad L=Q/P.
\]

Jensen gives \(E_P f(E_P[L\mid Z])\le E_P f(L)\) for convex \(f\), hence f-divergence contraction. Coarsening averages evidence; it need not decrease wealth on every realized branch. Forgetting \(L=(3/2,1/2)\) produces one, raising the low branch while reducing second moment from \(5/4\) to one. For nested public subfiltrations \(\mathcal G_t\subseteq\mathcal F_t\), optional projection \(\widetilde M_t=E_P[M_t\mid\mathcal G_t]\) remains a supermartingale by the tower law. Replacing the intervention policy is a different experiment, requiring recalibrated kernels.
