## 2. Existing mathematical context

Koopman constructions; exact dictionary evolution need not recover every source
observable. See Brunton, Brunton, Proctor and Kutz,

## 3. Proposed SUCC objects

A declared source is a measurable space $X$ with map $T:X\to X$. An observation is
$q:X\twoheadrightarrow Y$. A deterministic factor is $S:Y\to Y$ satisfying
$qT=Sq$. Define $U_Tf=f\circ T$, $Jg=g\circ q$, and
$\mathcal A_q=J B_b(Y)$, the full bounded measurable pullback algebra.
Its elements are available model observables; membership does not certify
physical measurement access. A linear dictionary $V$ is a separate object.
For a specified probability $\mu$, put $\nu=q_*\mu$ and
$H_q=J L^2(Y,\nu)$; its bounded core is an algebra, although $L^2$ itself
need not be closed under multiplication.

## 4. Candidate theorem

**D27.1 — CLASSICAL / PROVED LOCAL, conditional.** For every measurable $T$
and measurable surjection $q$ to a nonempty standard Borel $Y$,

\[
\exists\text{ measurable }S:\ qT=Sq
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad U_T\mathcal A_q\subseteq\mathcal A_q.
\]

**D27.2.** For compact Hausdorff $X,Y$, continuous onto $q$, and continuous
$T$, the same equivalence holds with continuous $S$ and $JC(Y)$.

**D27.3.** For standard Borel probability $X$, standard Borel $Y$, measurable
$q$, and nonsingular $T$, assume
$d(T_*\mu)/d\mu\in L^\infty(\mu)$. Then $U_T$ is bounded on $L^2(\mu)$,
and $U_TH_q\subseteq H_q$ iff some measurable $S$ satisfies $qT=Sq$
**$\mu$-almost everywhere**. Here $S$ is identified only $\nu$-almost
everywhere. Invariance means containment, not surjectivity onto the subspace.

## 5. Proof attempt

The forward direction is the identity $U_TJ=JU_S$. For D27.1 choose a
Borel embedding $b:Y\to[0,1]$ with Borel image. Invariance supplies bounded
measurable $h$ with $b(qTx)=h(qx)$. Surjectivity places every $h(y)$ in
$b(Y)$; set $S=b^{-1}h$. This proves equality and measurability.

For D27.2, continuous functions separate points. Invariance therefore forces
$qTx=qTx'$ whenever $qx=qx'$. This defines $S$, and the quotient-map
property of continuous compact-to-Hausdorff surjections makes $S$ continuous.

For D27.3, $J$ is an isometry with closed range, and
$\|U_Tf\|_2^2=\int|f|^2d(T_*\mu)\le\|d(T_*\mu)/d\mu\|_\infty\|f\|_2^2$.
Apply invariance to $b\circ q\in H_q$, obtaining a Borel representative
$h\in L^2(\nu)$. Define $S=b^{-1}h$ where $h\in b(Y)$, and any fixed
value elsewhere. Equality holds almost everywhere. The factor identity and
isometry of $J$ imply nonsingularity of $S$ and boundedness of $U_S$.
Conversely the same identity places every pulled-back $L^2$ observable in $H_q$.

Writing $L_T=U_T^*$, $C=J^*$, adjoints give **$CL_T=L_SC$**.
This does not assert $L_TJ=JL_S$. Nonsingularity alone defines the
$L^1/L^\infty$ transfer duality; our density bound additionally permits
bounded Hilbert adjoints. Measure preservation is the separate condition
$T_*\mu=\mu$, equivalently $L_T1=1$.
