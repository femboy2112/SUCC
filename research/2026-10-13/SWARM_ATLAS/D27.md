# SWM-D27 — Exact observational factors, Koopman closure, and quantified failure

Frozen parent `5eb09e7cba36ec01b8f9d6834b78b5766fd93935`; owner dynamics cohort D.

## 1. Original question

When does a source-derived lossy readout support an exact autonomous evolution,
and how much prediction error is certified when the invariant-algebra condition
fails? This is the cohort's load-bearing theorem target.

## 3. Proposed SUCC objects

Deterministic source: arbitrary set S, total stationary `F:S→S`, and fixed
surjective report `q:S→O`. Observer algebra
`A_q={g q:g:O→ℝ}` and Koopman pullback `U_F f=f F`.

Stochastic source: a **complete declared finite** set S, stationary row kernel
P, deterministic q, observation alphabet O=q(S), projected row
`R_s(o)=Σ_{t:q(t)=o} P(s,t)`. The operator `U_P f(s)=Σ_t P(s,t)f(t)` is a
Markov observable operator. No stationary source distribution is required.

## 4. Candidate theorem

**Deterministic equivalence.** The following are equivalent:

1. A unique `G:O→O` obeys `qF=Gq`.
2. `ker(q)` is an F-congruence: equal current reports imply equal next reports.
3. `U_F(A_q)⊆A_q`.

**Stochastic equivalence.** The following are equivalent:

1. All `R_s` are equal within every q fiber.
2. A unique observed kernel K obeys `U_P q*=q* U_K` on all functions on O.
3. `U_P(A_q)⊆A_q`.
4. For every initial source law, q(X_n) is a homogeneous Markov chain with the
   **same** kernel K and initial law q_*law(X_0).

**Quantified repair.** For any supplied kernel K on O define
`δ=max_s TV(R_s,K(q(s),·))`. Starting its chain from q_*law(X_0), for every
integer n≥0 the source-observed and candidate n-step marginals differ by at
most `min(1,nδ)`. Their entire report-history distributions through n satisfy
the same bound. All quantifiers concern this complete stationary source table.

## 5. Proof attempt

For deterministic 2⇒1, define `G(q(s))=q(Fs)`; congruence makes this independent
of the representative, and surjectivity makes it unique. 1⇒3 follows from
`U_F(gq)=gGq`. For 3⇒2, if equal q-values had distinct next values, the indicator
of one of those values would pull back to an observable nonconstant on that
fiber, contradiction. Thus all three are equivalent on arbitrary sets.

For the stochastic case, `U_P(1_{q=o})(s)=R_s(o)`. The coordinate indicators
form a basis of functions on finite O. Therefore invariant-algebra closure is
equivalent to constant projected rows; define K by those common values to obtain
the intertwiner. If the rows agree, conditional on any visible history ending
at o, the hidden-state law is supported on q^-1(o), and every hidden member has
the same next-report law K(o,·). Its mixture has that law, proving 4. Conversely
use point-mass initial source laws at two members of each fiber and their
one-step report laws to recover equal R_s. This last quantifier must mean the
same K for all initial laws: weaker initial-law-dependent Markov statements
are not equivalent to strong lumpability.

For the repair, condition on any actual visible history. Its next-report law
is a convex mixture of R_s within the final fiber, so its TV distance to the
candidate K row is ≤δ by convexity. Couple the actual and candidate histories
so they start with the same report and, while equal, use a maximal coupling
of these next-report laws. Conditional probability of first disagreement at
each step is ≤δ. The union bound yields probability of any disagreement by n
≤nδ, capped at one. Total variation between path laws is at most that coupling
failure probability. Projection onto the last coordinate cannot increase TV,
so the marginal bound follows. Alternatively marginal triangle inequality
and Markov-kernel contraction induct on n. No positive noise estimate is
assumed to make an exact invariant factor.

**Perron–Frobenius dual:** letting `C=q_*` aggregate source column/row laws,
the same identity reads `C(P_*ν)=K_*(Cν)` for every ν. It is the dual of the
observable intertwiner, not a separate physical evidence family.

## 6. Crucible

Minimal nonconstant deterministic countermodel: S={0,1,2}, F=(0,2,2),
q=(0,0,1). States 0 and 1 have equal current report and distinct next reports.
No nonconstant q on a two-state source can fail: its fibers are singleton.
All 4,330 binary-observed deterministic tables with 1≤N≤4 independently test
the indicator criterion against the factor certificate.

Measurement-noise countermodel: source=(hidden fixed bit, current report),
error 1/4 freshly resampled each step; four states, q extracts the report.
The two hidden sources with report zero have next laws `(3/4,1/4)` and
`(1/4,3/4)`. Strong lumpability fails, and the explicitly chosen uniform-fiber
reference kernel has δ=1/4. At error 1/2 the report carries no hidden bit and
closure holds; this is the calibrated null. A three-state phase transition
mutation has δ=1/2. The default reference average is not claimed minimax.

An exhaustive 512-table rational family gives 7,680 marginal/horizon checks.
A distinct direct source-path enumerator checks the stronger path bound in
the latent source through five steps. These finite controls corroborate the
implementation; the universal finite-kernel theorem rests on the proof above.

## 8. Remaining obstruction

Unknown hidden-state completeness, time-dependent dynamics, destructive probes,
and uncalibrated measurement noise do not satisfy the supplied source contract.
Smallest new theorem target: policy-indexed kernels with an explicit per-history
defect `δ(h,a)` and a validated original-state/source-history identity. A finite
table alone cannot establish those source access facts.
