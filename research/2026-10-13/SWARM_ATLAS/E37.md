## 1. Original question
Which SUCC ladder identities are exactly true at finite cutoff, which require an analytic limit, and does the recurrence identify the Archimedean completion uniquely?

## 3. Proposed SUCC objects
Finite successor multipliers 1,...,N and genuine prime valuations of N!; G_N(z)=N! N^z/[z(z+1)...(z+N)]. Exact code handles positive integer z; the limiting analytic object is Gamma on Re z>0 with locally uniform convergence of Euler approximants, then separately imported meromorphic continuation. The zeta completion is Lambda(s)=pi^(-s/2)Gamma(s/2)zeta(s), conductor 1, and xi(s)=s(s-1)Lambda(s)/2.

## 4. Candidate theorem
For positive z and N>=2,
G_N(z+1)/G_N(z)=Nz/(N+z+1),
z-G_N(z+1)/G_N(z)=z(z+1)/(N+z+1)>0.
Consequently no finite cutoff obeys the exact Gamma recurrence. Recurrence and all positive integer Gamma values alone fail to identify Gamma: Gamma_e(z)=Gamma(z)exp(e sin(2pi z)) agrees at positive integers and obeys the recurrence for every real e, but differs off integers when e!=0.
For integer m>=1 the completed Lambda has a finite nonzero value at -2m; this statement uses the classical meromorphic functional equation, not a finite-cutoff extrapolation.

## 5. Proof attempt — complete identities and countermodel
Cancel numerator and denominator products to obtain the displayed cutoff ratio, then subtract it from z. The boundary defect tends to zero for fixed z, but this calculation alone gives neither locally uniform convergence nor analytic continuation. Periodicity of sin(2pi z) gives Gamma_e(z+1)=z Gamma_e(z); vanishing at integer z gives agreement with factorial values. At z=1/4 and e!=0 the factor exp(e) is different, proving nonuniqueness. Bohr–Mollerup repairs uniqueness by adding positivity, normalization and global log-convexity on (0,infinity); recurrence alone omits its load-bearing hypothesis.
The imported Gamma continuation has simple poles at z=-m. Applying the imported functional equation yields Lambda(-2m)=Lambda(1+2m), where every factor on the right is finite positive. Thus the Gamma pole and trivial zeta zero cancel and the completion is nonzero there. This does not read a nontrivial zero into the construction. The poles at s=0,1 are separately removed by s(s-1)/2.

## 6. Crucible

Existing periodic-gauge controls remain primary local negative examples. Pole-domain points are never passed to the positive-integer cutoff routine. No numerical continuation or negative spectral value is treated as a theorem.

The meromorphic functional equation is classical input; no zero-free region or global nonvanishing is inferred. The missing RH-facing map must also transport the completed pole/Gamma terms and legitimate test space; vanishing cutoff defects alone do not prove its sign.
