## 4. Candidate theorem
For each N>=6, no such universal covariance-only adapter exists. In fact, source a(n)=1 and mutant a'(n)=1 except a'(6)=-1 give identical covariances for EVERY feature family depending only on n and the same squared weights, while b_a(6)=0 and b_a'(6)=-2. This blocks source authentication at an upstream coefficient level; it does not show that a particular completed Weil form is negative or disprove an adapter retaining source phases.

## 5. Proof attempt — complete no-go
For every n, |a(n)|^2=|a'(n)|^2=1. Thus normalized weights agree termwise; every covariance and Gram depending only on these weights and common features is identical. From the coefficient-level Dirichlet-log formula b(6)=a(6)-a(2)a(3), the two values are 0 and -2. If a classifier F(C) returned the correct vanishing verdict for both sources, equality C_a=C_a' would force equal outputs, contradicting the different verdicts. This is a factorization obstruction: the Euler predicate is not constant on fibers of the covariance map.
The covariance is PSD by sum_n probability_n (X(n)-mean)(X(n)-mean)^T. For features (v2,v3), it is actually positive definite once the source includes n=1,2,3 with positive weights: no nonzero linear functional can be constant on (0,0),(1,0),(0,1). Hence the failure is not caused by a degenerate zero covariance.
