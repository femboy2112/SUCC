# SWM-X05 — Instrument simulation transports finite-horizon original-target policies

## 1. Original question

Which extra mathematical data turn C20's one-step Blackwell simulation into a valid comparison of C21's source-changing adaptive policies? Can a stochastic current-carrier map do this without granting the observer a hidden-state oracle?

## 2. Existing mathematical context and provenance

Independent second-generation assignment at root snapshot `508f16881a4b145dffa13d0d3d336d1abcbda98c`. Audited C17/C20/C21 and F48, including the actual rational garbling and Bellman implementations. The finite-kernel operations are classical. Background: Blackwell, [Equivalent Comparisons of Experiments](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177729032), 1953; Fritz, [A synthetic approach to Markov kernels, conditional independence and theorems on sufficient statistics](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.07021v8), 2020. The latter's author abstract was inspected; neither source is used as an unproved policy-transport lemma. The theorem below is derived directly. Academic priority is **UNVERIFIED**.

## 3. Common ambient objects, domains and actual maps

All sets are finite. Fix the same original labels O and target t:O→Z on both sides. Actual current carrier X and virtual carrier Y have initial joint laws J_X on O×X and J_Y on O×Y. A stochastic kernel T:X→Δ(Y) satisfies

\[
J_Y(o,y)=\sum_xJ_X(o,x)T(y\mid x).
\]

There is a common finite action-label set A. Actual action a is enabled on D_a^X⊆X and has joint report/current kernel I_a(α,x'|x), α∈R_a; virtual action a is enabled on D_a^Y⊆Y and has L_a(β,y'|y), β∈S_a. Rows at enabled states normalize. There is a **report-only** stochastic garbling G_a(β|α). It has no original/current-state input. Actual and virtual action costs are nonnegative constants c_X(a),c_Y(a). Costs of implementing G are included in c_X; independent finite randomization is an authorized controller resource.

A policy uses its own recorded reports and chooses only actions enabled at every state of its current positive-support belief. It stops with t certified only when all positive-probability originals share that t-value. Actual implementation retains its true α reports and its generated β reports, while action selection follows the virtual β-history. A common source tag alone does not establish the initial joint-law identity.

## 4. Candidate theorem and quantified contract

**One-way finite-horizon simulation theorem.** Suppose, for every a and x such that T(D_a^Y|x)=1,

1. x∈D_a^X (availability lifting);
2. for every β,y', the joint square holds:

\[
\sum_{\alpha,x'} I_a(\alpha,x'\mid x)G_a(\beta\mid\alpha)T(y'\mid x')
=\sum_yT(y\mid x)L_a(\beta,y'\mid y);
\tag{*}
\]

3. c_X(a)≤c_Y(a).

Then every robust virtual policy which certifies t on every positive-probability branch by finite horizon n has an actual randomized implementation which certifies the **same original target** by n and has no greater expected cost. The original/β-history law is exactly preserved, and every corresponding β-history has the same original posterior. If costs are equal, each transported policy has equal expected cost.

For the finite total-kernel setting of C21, its deterministic optimum therefore satisfies V_X(n)≤V_Y(n), with +∞ for infeasibility, even when G is stochastic. Equality of optimal values is not asserted by this one-way theorem; reverse simulation gives the reverse inequality. For partial menus the same inequality follows from finite-tree derandomization under the robust availability rule just defined.

## 5. Proof attempt — unnormalized path invariant

Let μ_X^h(o,x) be the actual unnormalized law of original/current state and the simulated β-history h; actual α-history and controller coins are summed out. Let μ_Y^h be the virtual law. Inductively maintain

\[
\mu_Y^h(o,y)=\sum_x\mu_X^h(o,x)T(y\mid x).\tag{I}
\]

It holds initially by hypothesis. At a positive history h, if virtual action a is robustly enabled, every y of positive μ_Y^h marginal lies in D_a^Y. Nonnegativity in (I) implies that any x with positive actual marginal satisfies T(D_a^Y|x)=1, so availability lifting licenses a on every actual alternative. Conditioning on extra actual α/coin information only removes alternatives, so the actual action remains robustly licensed.

Multiply (*) by μ_X^h(o,x) and sum over x. The left side is the actual next joint law followed by T; the right side is the virtual next joint law. This proves (I) at each extension hβ. Summing over y shows equality of original/history weights, hence of history probabilities and all positive-history original posteriors. No division at zero-probability branches occurs. If a virtual terminal history has constant t, every finer actual record inside it also has that constant t. Thus certification transports. Summing action costs against the equal decision-history probabilities proves the expected-cost inequality.

The compiler executes only the actual authorized action and G_a on the observed α. It **does not sample T(y|x), inspect x, or execute an action selected from a secretly sampled y**. T is the algebraic intertwiner used in the proof. This distinction is essential for a stochastic carrier map: knowing a kernel mathematically is not an ability to observe its hidden input.

For deterministic optimum comparison, a finite-horizon randomized controller has a finite tree with independent finite random choices. At every reached random-choice node, each positive-probability choice must have a feasible continuation (otherwise it contributes positive certification failure). Its expected continuation cost is a convex average. Select a feasible branch with no larger cost and continue backwards. Finite induction removes all random choices, preserves licensed actions, and does not increase expected cost. This is C21's derandomization argument applied to the compiled tree, whose internal memory includes β. A deterministic controller can reconstruct that memory from its actual history and its now-fixed choices. This does not establish an unbounded-horizon selection theorem.

## 6. Crucible and independent exact receipts

**Smallest original-source counterexample to readout-only transport.** O=X={0,1}, prior (1/2,1/2), target t(o)=o. A first instrument emits the single report `*`. Good preserves the bit; Bad resets both inputs to current 0. Their first-step experiments are identical. Afterwards the only useful continuation reads the current bit without changing it. Good certifies in one continuation step. Bad cannot certify at any finite horizon: originals 0 and 1 have identical current 0 and identical future history laws but different targets. At least two original labels are necessary for a nonconstant target, so the original-cardinality claim is minimal. This improves the size of C20's countermodel, without claiming a nonconstant first report; C20's four-state example supplies that stronger feature.

**Positive stochastic T control.** Actual X is a visible bit. Virtual Y consists of that bit and a fair hidden bit; T appends the independent hidden bit. Two actions preserve or flip the visible bit while the virtual action refreshes its hidden bit. One report channel is noisy (3/4 correct), the other exact. A report-dependent policy is replayed to depth 4 with an unequal original prior. Independent unnormalized propagation verifies (I) and every original-history marginal. Removing an enabled actual state, raising actual cost above its bound, and resetting virtual backaction each breaks the appropriate contract. This is a genuinely stochastic T, not a disguised deterministic quotient.

**C20 audit.** All 625 pairs of two-source/binary-report experiments with probabilities in {0,1/4,1/2,3/4,1} are compared with a separate closed-form two-variable feasibility solution, including rank-deficient experiments. Each returned channel is recomposed without calling the subject's verification helper. Budget exhaustion is separately required to raise.

**C21 audit.** All 256 ordered pairs of deterministic two-state, binary-report actions, two positive priors, horizons 0–2, and unequal action costs are checked by enumerating complete decision trees and replaying original paths. Two stochastic menus add full-support-noise controls. Total: 1548 independent solver comparisons; every depth-2 model has 202 complete candidate trees including early target guesses. The independent oracle performs no normalized-belief update and no Bellman minimization.

## 8. Remaining obstruction and next falsification

This is a sufficient simulation contract, not a necessary characterization of all policy-value comparisons. More general report/state-correlated simulators can require a realizable transducer with its own memory; merely writing H(β,y'|α,x') gives an impermissible state oracle if x' is not observed and the kernel is not physically implemented. A shared missing latent variable can invalidate the declared Markov laws. Infinite-horizon optimality, approximation/error budgets, state-dependent cost couplings, and unlicensed randomness are outside the theorem.

Next falsification: construct a finite pair with V_X≤V_Y for every target/prior/horizon yet no such common T and report-only G; that would delineate the strictness of the sufficient contract. Conversely, any accepted square with a mismatched original/history law falsifies the induction or implementation. Randomness must be a declared controller resource; exact stochastic garbling is not available to a controller forbidden to randomize.

## 9. Cross-domain dependency cut

The bridge is the actual equation (*) and invariant (I), not a similarity between Blackwell and policy terminology. C20 supplies G; C17 supplies the original/current joint law; the T square supplies the missing continuation relation; F48 supplies the availability and original-target requirements. For a deterministic quotient T=δ_q and unchanged reports G=identity, (*) becomes joint outcome/update descent, and F48's quotient induction is the Dirac special case. A deterministic surjective quotient preserves all histories under matching availability; this stochastic one-way theorem yields simulation and value domination, with no unwarranted isomorphism claim.
