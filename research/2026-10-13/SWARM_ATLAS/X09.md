## 5. Proof attempt — complete paper derivation

Cauchy's integral formula makes each coefficient evaluation continuous in H. Taylor representation and the identity theorem make T injective because Ω is connected. J(K_R) is compact in Hausdorff H×L. The continuous injective map T×id from this compact image into Hausdorff V×L is therefore a homeomorphism onto its compact, closed image. The disk supremum seminorm is continuous on H, hence bounded on A(K_R); Cauchy's estimate gives the coefficient bound. A compact subset of discrete L is finite.

Every global receipt belongs to every precision fiber, so its cost bounds 𝔐. Suppose 𝔐=M<∞. Inside compact K_(M+1), take all closed sets

\[
F_{N,\epsilon}(\alpha,l)\cap K_{M+\delta},
\quad N\ge0,\quad0<\epsilon,\delta\le1.
\]

Finite intersections have a witness: use the largest N, smallest ε and smallest δ; the fiber infimum is ≤M, so some witness has cost <M+δ. Compactness gives one e in the entire intersection. Shrinking ε forces every coefficient equality, and shrinking δ gives c(e)≤M. Every global cost is ≥M, proving equality and attainment. If M=∞, any global receipt's finite cost would contradict it. Exact fibers sit inside precision fibers, so 𝔐 is bounded above by the supremum of exact-fiber infima; every global receipt bounds that supremum from above. The attained equality, and the same contradiction when M=∞, prove the entire chain.

This proof uses ordinary closed complex balls and precision quantifiers. It does not apply B09's discrete exact-cylinder closure theorem to complex coefficients. A positive Taylor radius alone supplies neither extension throughout Ω nor a supported receipt.
