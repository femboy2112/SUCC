## Independent reconstruction of the author's theorem

Choose root-to-vertex bijections \(U_v\). A root law \(\nu\) defines a parallel
section by \(\nu_v=(U_v)_*\nu\) exactly when it is fixed by every root holonomy
permutation: two paths differ by holonomy, so this is precisely path independence.
This is componentwise if the graph is disconnected. An invariant law has equal
mass at all points of each orbit. Its orbit masses \(w_i\) therefore recover it
uniquely as \(Jw\), where \(Jw(x)=w_i/|O_i|\). Conversely these laws are invariant.
The uniform law supplies a rational law section even when every point moves.
Diracs commute with bijections, and \(h_*\delta_x=\delta_x\) iff \(hx=x\).
The same reasoning for pulled-back holonomy proves the atomic cover criterion;
no numerical A04 cover minimum was independently recalculated here.

Use matrices \(T(y,x)\) acting on column laws. For each reported update, covariance
\(T(hy,hx)=T(y,x)\) makes
\(\bar T(j,i)=\sum_{y\in O_j}T(y,x)\), \(x\in O_i\), representative-independent.
Grouping a finite sum proves \(RT=\bar TR\); evaluating on Diracs establishes
uniqueness. Covariance also sends invariant measures to invariant measures, whence
\(TJ=J\bar T\). Summing over reports and output orbits gives complete input
normalization of the quotient instrument. Individual reported maps may lose
mass; they are not individually stochastic matrices.

Writing \(\ell_T(\nu)=|T\nu|\), mass preservation by \(R,J\) proves common
likelihoods. Divide the squares by their common positive likelihood to obtain
\(R B_T=B_{\bar T}R\) and \(B_TJ=JB_{\bar T}\), with
\(B_T(\nu)=T\nu/\ell_T(\nu)\). Ordered products prove the finite-history result.
Public-history policies and independent seeds preserve the same contract;
private state-dependent policy choices require checking the resulting kernels.

For \(h=(\alpha,\beta)\), the joint matrix has
\(T_o((s',q'),(s,q))=\mathbf1_{s'=s}K(o,q'|s,q)\).
Evaluating commutation on every Dirac gives exactly X02's pointwise kernel

## Reviewer-derived sharpening: exact Reynolds criterion

**Independent lemma R-D25-X02, CLASSICAL / PROVED LOCAL.** This sharpening belongs
to the reviewer, not silently to the original author. For any nonnegative
rational matrix \(T:C(X)\to C(X)\), define \(\Pi=JR\), the orbit-uniform
averaging projection. Then the following are equivalent:

\[
T\Pi=\Pi T
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists\bar T\ge0:\ RT=\bar TR\ \text{and}\ TJ=J\bar T.
\]

The quotient is necessarily \(\bar T=RTJ\). Indeed, assuming commutation,
\[
RT=R\Pi T=RT\Pi=(RTJ)R,\qquad
TJ=T\Pi J=\Pi TJ=J(RTJ).
\]
Conversely the two squares give
\(T\Pi=TJR=J\bar TR=JRT=\Pi T\).
Uniqueness follows by composing the first square with \(J\) and using \(RJ=1\).
Nonnegativity follows directly from \(RTJ\). This proof needs only a finite
partition and its uniform lift, not original/current product structure.

Since \(\Pi=|H|^{-1}\sum_hh_*\), full H-covariance implies Reynolds commutation.
It is strictly stronger: a three-cycle commutes with uniform averaging on a
three-point S3-orbit but fails to commute with transpositions. Thus exact
all-prior orbit filtering *and* uniform lifting need not imply full naturality.
The positive-likelihood and chronological proofs above apply under this weaker
per-report condition. This does not replace holonomy covariance when actual
edge naturality is required.

2. **Lumpability alone is insufficient.** Resetting three current states to
   state zero has \(RT=R\) for the one-orbit quotient, yet maps the uniform
   prior to a Dirac. It fails \(TJ=J\bar T\) and Reynolds commutation. The
   three-cycle passes both squares and Reynolds commutation but fails S3

3. **Likelihood averaging does not repair the declared instrument.** Effects
   \((1,1/2,1/4)\) turn a uniform prior into \((4/7,2/7,1/7)\).
   Averaging afterward returns uniformity, but Dirac event likelihoods remain
   \((1,1/2,1/4)\); no all-prior one-orbit law square follows. A separate
   two-state reveal instrument has a covariant unconditioned sum, but its
   individual report update is not covariant and creates a non-invariant Dirac.
   Checking only the summed kernel is therefore insufficient.
4. **Joint orbits are not arbitrary products.** Under simultaneous bit-flip,
   orbits are \(\{00,11\}\) and \(\{01,10\}\). If a product map \(f\times g\)
   identifies 00 with 11, it forces \(f(0)=f(1)\) and \(g(0)=g(1)\), thus
   identifies every state and cannot retain parity. Full-support invariant
   priors \((3,1,1,3)/8\) and \((1,3,3,1)/8\) have identical two marginals
   but report-zero likelihoods \(3/4\) and \(1/4\). The joint orbit square
   survives; the collapsed product square fails already on Diracs 00 and 01.
5. **Zero normalization fails.** An impossible reported update sends every law
   to zero; the cone update is total, while normalization is undefined. Keeping
   zero gives the unique cone-valued lift of the word. Removing it or requiring
   normalized lifts over every declared report word breaks totality.
6. **Unrelated instruments are not frame transport.** On an identity edge,
   a root identity update and a leaf reset update fail the edge square despite
   trivial root holonomy. This stronger alternative is refuted; X02's explicitly
   transported family is unaffected.
