## 2. Conditional expectations, intersections and joins

Fix any positive integer **N**. Put `H_N=C^(Z/N)` with inner product
`<f,h>=N^-1 sum_x conjugate(f(x))h(x)`. For every positive `m|N`, let
`A_m={f: f(x) depends only on x mod m}`. Its dimension is m, and its orthogonal
conditional expectation is

`(E_m f)(x)=(m/N) sum_{y=x mod m} f(y)`.

**M1 (paper proof):** For every `m,n|N`, `E_m E_n=E_n E_m=E_g`, `g=gcd(m,n)`.
Indeed `E_m` averages translations by the subgroup `H_m=mZ/N`. The addition
homomorphism `H_m x H_n -> H_g` is onto (Bezout), with equally sized fibers
`H_m intersect H_n=H_l`, `l=lcm(m,n)`. Its uniform pushforward is uniform on
`H_g`. This proves the identity with exactly the stated normalization. Each
`E_m` is self-adjoint, idempotent, and has image `A_m`, directly from its fiber
averages. Consequently `A_m intersect A_n=A_g`: a common fixed vector is fixed
by the product, and a vector of `A_g` is fixed by each factor. QED.

**M2 (paper proof):** The unital pointwise algebra generated by `A_m,A_n` is
`A_l`, identified with functions on
`C_(m,n)={(a,b): a=b mod g}`, not all `Z/m x Z/n` unless g=1.
For a compatible pair, write `x=a+m*t`. The equation
`(m/g)t=(b-a)/g mod n/g` has exactly one solution modulo `n/g`, since the
coefficient is a unit (when `n/g=1` the congruence is empty). Thus x exists
uniquely modulo l. Incompatibility makes the original equations impossible.
The product `1_(x=a mod m) 1_(x=b mod n)` is precisely the corresponding
singleton indicator modulo l when compatible, and zero otherwise. These
indicators span `A_l`, while every generated function already factors through
l. Thus the multiplication map `A_m tensor A_n -> A_l` is onto; its kernel is
spanned by the incompatible-pair atoms, of dimension `mn-l`. QED.

For any nonempty finite menu `(m_i)|N`, the same argument gives the actual CRT
image and algebra `A_L`, `L=lcm_i m_i`. Pairwise gcd compatibility suffices:
after merging an initial segment to modulus J, for each prime power in
`gcd(J,m_new)` choose an old modulus attaining the largest old valuation.
Pairwise compatibility makes the merged residue agree with the new one at
that prime power. Coprimality of distinct prime powers gives compatibility
modulo the whole gcd, so induction applies. No independent composite place
is introduced by this argument.

**M3 (paper proof):** Linear pooling `A_m+A_n` has dimension `m+n-g`, whereas
the joint algebra has dimension l. Its missing orthogonal sector has projector
`D=E_l-E_m-E_n+E_g` and dimension `l-m-n+g`.
The intersection formula proves the dimension of the sum. Because the two
projections commute, `S=E_m+E_n-E_g` is self-adjoint, idempotent, and is the
identity on each image; its image is their sum. Since `S E_l=S`, `D=E_l-S`
is the complementary orthogonal projector within `A_l`. QED.

## 3. Primitive conductors and arbitrary menu interaction order

Let `chi_k(x)=exp(2*pi*i*k*x/N)`, `0<=k<N`. For k not zero, summing the
geometric progression proves `sum_x chi_k(x)=0`; for k=0 the sum is N.
Thus these N characters form an orthonormal basis. Their conductors are
`d=N/gcd(N,k)` (with conductor 1 for k=0): their least positive period is d,
by reducing the fraction k/N. Averaging along `mZ/N` therefore retains exactly
the characters with `d|m`.

**M4 (paper proof):** Define `P_d=sum_(e|d) mu(d/e) E_e`, for every `d|N`.
It is the orthogonal projector onto characters of **exactly** conductor d;
`rank P_d=phi(d)`, `P_d P_e=0` if d differs from e, and
`E_m=sum_(d|m)P_d`. To prove the selector, on a character of conductor c the
coefficient is zero unless c divides d; if c divides d it is
`sum_(c|e|d)mu(d/e)=sum_(q|d/c)mu(q)`, which equals 1 for c=d and 0 otherwise.
The last identity follows by factoring the divisor sum into `(1-1)` over
the distinct prime factors of d/c; squared-prime factors contribute zero to
mu. There are exactly phi(d) labels of conductor d, namely `(N/d)a` with
`1<=a<=d`, `gcd(a,d)=1`. The spectral conclusions follow. The matrices are
rational, so their rational ranks equal their complex ranks (ranks are the
largest nonzero rational minors). QED.

**M5 (paper proof):** For any nonempty finite menu `m_1,...,m_s|N`, define
`V_d={i:d|m_i}` and `L=lcm_i m_i`. Then

* the linear pool is the orthogonal sum of `im P_d` with `V_d` nonempty;
* its dimension is `sum_(V_d nonempty) phi(d)`;
* equivalently it is `sum_(empty!=I subset [s]) (-1)^(|I|+1) gcd_(i in I)m_i`;
* the joint algebra is `A_L`; its missing sector is the sum of `im P_d`
  for `d|L` with `V_d` empty;
* an exact conductor d first appears at interaction order
  `r(d)=min{|I|:d divides lcm_(i in I)m_i}`, with `lcm(empty)=1`.

Proof: M4 diagonalizes every menu in the same basis. Taking the union of the
retained basis vectors proves the first two assertions. Inclusion-exclusion
on those finite sets proves the third, since an intersection consists of
characters whose conductors divide the gcd and has that gcd as its size.
M2 and its finite-menu extension prove the fourth. For a fixed subset I,
products of one arbitrary function from each menu span `A_lcm(I)`, by its
joint indicators. Summing these spaces over all `|I|<=r` retains exactly the
conductors dividing at least one such lcm, proving the minimum-order formula.
Repeated factors from the same menu multiply within that menu and cannot
lower this minimum. QED.

`collective_sectors` implements this for 1..12 menu algebras. A menu that
already contains several jointly available reads is first replaced by its
lcm. Repeated menus add no dimension; their indices remain explicit. The
code enumerates interaction orders, not a second general portfolio solver.
Missing *joint* sectors exclude conductors outside L, even if N is larger.

## 4. Conductor six, fully explicit

On `Z/6`, ordered `x=0,...,5`, the missing sector for menus 2 and 3 is P6:

```
u = (2, 1,-1,-2,-1, 1)
v = (0, 1, 1, 0,-1,-1)
P6 = (1/6) [[ 2, 1,-1,-2,-1, 1],
            [ 1, 2, 1,-1,-2,-1],
            [-1, 1, 2, 1,-1,-2],
            [-2,-1, 1, 2, 1,-1],
            [-1,-2,-1, 1, 2, 1],
            [ 1,-1,-2,-1, 1, 2]]
```

Every mod2 and mod3 fiber sum of u and v is zero. They are orthogonal with
normalized squared norms 2 and 2/3. M3 gives dimension two, so they are a
complete rational basis. In the already implemented field with
`zeta=zeta_6`, `zeta^2-zeta+1=0`, put `t=zeta-1/2`. Direct reduction gives
`chi_1=u/2+t*v` and `chi_5=u/2-t*v`. Thus P6 is also the character kernel
`[chi_1(x-y)+chi_5(x-y)]/6`. The probe checks all 36 entries exactly.

The local functions `f(x)=(-1)^x` and `g(x)=(2,-1,-1)[x mod3]` multiply to u.
Neither local expectation sees u, so this is a concrete refutation of
linear pooling being closed under multiplication. Also
`chi_(1/2) chi_(2/3)=chi_(1/6)`, because `1/2+2/3=1/6 mod1`.
**Conductor six is a joint interaction of the 2 and 3 factors, not an
independent prime-place 6.**

Hostile noncoprime case: `(m,n)=(4,6)`, L=12. Only 12 of the 24 Cartesian
pairs occur; `(0,1)` cannot occur since its parities disagree. The linear
dimension is 8, missing dimension 4, exactly P12. Multi-menu controls:
`(2,3,5)` has linear dimension 8 and missing conductors `6,10,15,30` of
dimensions `2,4,8,8`; conductor 30 needs three menus. For `(6,10,15)` the
linear dimension is 22; only conductor 30 is missing and any pair can
generate it. Incomparable menus need not contribute independent information.

## 5. Minimal target pair covers and finite separating portfolios

**M6 (paper proof):** For a finite candidate set X with target `t:X->T` and
finite deterministic reads `q_i`, a chosen set J permits an exact target
decoder if and only if every pair x,y with different targets is separated
by at least one selected read. Necessity: an equal joint read vector forces
the same decoder output. Sufficiency: assign to each attained read vector
the target of any representative; the pair condition makes it well-defined.
Consequently the minimum number of selected reads is precisely minimum
set cover of cross-target pairs by their distinguishing-read sets. If all
such pairs have a separator in an arbitrary family of jointly usable reads,
select one for each pair; this finite portfolio suffices. Conversely a pair
with no separator makes every portfolio fail. This is existence, not a
polynomial-time minimum-cover claim. QED.

For modular reads `q_m(x)=a_x mod m`, separation is exactly
`m does not divide a_x-a_y`. Finite candidates with distinct integer labels
have a single sufficient modulus larger than their diameter, *if available*;
distinct targets at the same integer label cannot be separated by any
modular read. The adapter retains this hidden-state countermodel from World.
For the six candidates 0..5 with distinct targets, menus `(2,3)` require
both reads, while `(2,3,6)` permits the single read6. This is a model minimum,
not a performed observation or a claim of affordable physical access.

No finite modular portfolio identifies every integer or every profinite
point: if L is its lcm, the distinct integers 0 and L give identical reports.
Postprocessing their reports cannot distinguish them. General E menus may
be mutually exclusive or source-changing; the adapter assumes one ready
nondestructive state and delegates those other constraints to the existing
solver rather than pooling incompatible branches.

## 6. Affine SUCC/FUCC, phases, and nonunits

In a unit-origin chart with sign `s=+1` (`n_minus_one`) or `s=-1`
(`one_minus_n`), source SUCC is `x -> x+s*h`, and source multiplication is
`x -> a*x+s*(a-1)`. For any affine map A(x)=a*x+b on `Z/N`, Koopman
pullback is `U_A f(x)=f(A(x))`. For every label k,

`U_A chi_k = zeta_N^(k*b) chi_(a*k mod N)`.

**M7 (paper proof):** This follows by splitting the exponent `k(ax+b)`.
A character of conductor d is sent to one of conductor `d/gcd(d,a)`,
since multiplication of its reduced frequency `c/d` by a cancels exactly
`gcd(d,a)`. Let h=gcd(a,N), including a=0. The affine image has N/h points,
each with h preimages: solving `ax=y-b mod N` reduces to a unit congruence
modulo N/h precisely when h divides y-b. Therefore

`||U_A f||_2^2=(h/N) sum_(y in image A)|f(y)|^2 <= h ||f||_2^2`.

Equality for a nonzero function supported on the image proves
`||U_A||^2=h`; its rank is N/h. It is unitary exactly when h=1.
Furthermore `U_A(A_m)=A_(m/gcd(a,m))`: the map has that period, and its
image residues modulo m are in bijection with those input residue classes,
so arbitrary functions of that smaller quotient can be prescribed. If a
is a unit modulo N, it permutes every averaging subgroup; translating the
fiber shows `E_m U_A=U_A E_m`. This need not hold for nonunits. QED.

Concrete control `N=6,a=2,b=1`: `chi_1` loses conductor 6 to conductor 3,
with nontrivial phase `zeta_6`; omitting b gives the wrong value already
at x=0. Norm squared is 2 and rank is 3. On `f=1_(x=0)`, `E_1 U_A f=0`
but `U_A E_1 f=1/6`, proving noncommutation. A prime multiplier can be a
nonunit on this finite clock; a composite multiplier can be a unit on
another clock. Prime source certification does not follow from covariance.
For composition, `U_A U_B=U_(B composed with A)`; action order is reversed.

The Haar hypothesis is essential even for M1. On Z/6 take unnormalized
weights `(2,1,1,1,1,1)` and weighted conditional expectations. Directly,
`(E_2 E_3)[0,0]=1/3`, whereas `E_1[0,0]=2/7`. Thus merely specifying the
same modular partitions under a correlated measure does not imply M1.

## 7. Profinite extension with correct density scope

Let `Zhat=lim_N Z/N` with its compact inverse-limit topology and real Haar
probability. A cylinder `f=F composed with pi_m` is bounded because its image
is finite. All finite-level complex cylinders form a unital *-algebra:
addition, multiplication and conjugation refine to the finite lcm of levels.
“All bounded cylinders” means every individual function is bounded; a fixed
sup-norm unit ball is not an algebra (1+1 leaves that ball).

**M8 (paper proof):** Every finite identity M1--M5 extends to cylinder
functions by choosing a common finite quotient; uniform Haar pushes forward
to uniform measure on each quotient. For bounded continuous f and epsilon>0,
continuity gives, around each point, a cylinder neighborhood on which f
differs from its value there by less than epsilon. Compactness gives a finite
cover. Refining their finitely many moduli to one L makes each L-fiber lie
inside one such neighborhood. Taking one value per fiber approximates f
uniformly, with error at most 2epsilon. Hence cylinders are uniformly dense
in `C(Zhat)`. Rational real-valued cylinders are dense in real C by a further
finite rational approximation; use Q+iQ for complex C.

They are also dense in `L^p(Haar)` for `1<=p<infinity`: on this compact
metrizable space Haar is a regular Borel probability. For a measurable set B,
regularity gives compact K inside B and open O containing B with
`mu(O\K)<epsilon` (use completed-measure representatives when needed).
Each point of K has a clopen cylinder neighborhood inside O; a finite union
C of them is itself a cylinder and `mu(B symmetric_difference C)<epsilon`.
Thus cylinder indicators approximate measurable indicators in Lp. Finite
simple functions are dense in Lp: truncate any Lp function, then quantize its
bounded range, with truncation error tending to zero by integrability. This
proves the claim. Jensen's inequality makes each finite-fiber mean an L2
contraction, so M1 extends uniquely to all L2 by this density. QED.

This is **not** uniform density in all bounded measurable functions, nor
density in their essential-sup norm. An explicit counterexample is the
indicator of `B={x: v_2(x_2) is finite and even}`. For every modulus N,
its zero residue cylinder contains positive-Haar subsets with both even and
odd finite 2-adic valuations (choose either valuation at least v2(N), and
zero residues at other factors of N). Every N-cylinder function is constant
there, so its L-infinity error from `1_B` is at least 1/2.

For a restricted read family M, form D from all finite lcms of members.
The generated cylinder algebra separates Zhat and is uniformly dense in C
**iff** every positive integer divides some member of D. Sufficiency follows
by including every finite cylinder level. For necessity, failure occurs at
some prime power p^k; the p-valuations in D have a finite maximum r<k.
Construct the compatible profinite point z with p-component p^r and all
other prime components zero, using ordinary coprime CRT at every finite
level. Then z is nonzero but every read in M equals its read at 0. A cylinder
at p^(r+1) separates them; no uniform limit of the restricted algebra can do
so. This also proves non-density directly. Mere unbounded numerical modulus
size is insufficient; e.g. all powers of 2 never see the 3-component. QED.

`BoundedCylinder` implements only rational finite values, refinement, sums,
products and exact sup norms, with modulus <=100. It is a finite executable
interface, not a program enumerating Zhat or a certificate of analytic
convergence rates. Refinement is an isometry in sup norm and normalized Lp
because each coarse value repeats equally often, so different compatible
representations give the same cylinder operations.
