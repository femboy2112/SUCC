\begin{abstract}
On $\ell^2(\N_{\geq1})$ and $L^2(\Zhat,\mu)$, consider the atomic
multiplicative shifts and normalized additive-Haar dilation isometries.
We prove that any uniformly bounded sequence intertwining the forward
shifts for increasing finite prime sets converges weakly to zero whenever
the reciprocal sum of the selected primes diverges.  Adjoint intertwining
is unnecessary for this conclusion.  With adjoints it yields an exact
valuation-shell support constraint and a sharp bounded-test coefficient
bound.  We reconstruct the complementary summable-prime star-intertwiner
classification, distinguish weak from strong convergence for finite-prime
unitaries, and exhibit a distributional completion with an explicit
universal property relative to the locally constant test topology.
That completion does not extend the atomic Hilbert norm.  All proofs are
given; exact finite controls are supplementary.  Scholarly priority for
the specific arithmetic refinements remains unverified.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and calibrated operators}

The mathematical statements below have status \textbf{PROVED LOCAL, PAPER};
they are not claims of formal verification.  This paper reconstructs the
atomic/Haar results of the separately inspected repository head
\texttt{e42096ec0186228dd0f748767599e470f2ca2e1d} (PR178), then strengthens its
all-prime star-disjointness to forward disjointness and a finite-to-global
limit theorem.  Its implementation base is PR179,
\texttt{81684b4b8956f9f5da56c376ba7e4339277eb665}; PR178 is not silently treated
as an ancestor.  No analytic source form, zeta zero, or positivity assumption
is used.

Write $\N_+=\N_{\geq1}$, $\Hd=\ell^2(\N_+)$ with basis $(e_n)$, and
$\Hc=L^2(\Zhat,\mu)$, where $\mu$ is normalized \emph{additive} Haar measure.
For $a\in\N_+$ define
\[
 V_ae_n=e_{an},\qquad
 U_af(x)=\sqrt a\,\one_{a\Zhat}(x)f(x/a).
\]
Division is used only on $a\Zhat$.  Multiplication $m_a:x\mapsto ax$
is an injective homeomorphism onto this clopen subgroup, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:haar}
 \mu(aA)=a^{-1}\mu(A)
\end{equation}
for measurable $A$.  Indeed $\Zhat=\prod_p\mathbb Z_p$, the subgroup has
index $a$, and normalized Haar uniqueness gives the restricted scaling law.
Consequently both operators are isometries, and direct change of variables
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjoints}
 V_a^*e_n=\begin{cases}e_{n/a}&a\mid n,\\0&a\nmid n,\end{cases}
 \qquad U_a^*g(y)=a^{-1/2}g(ay),\qquad
 U_aU_a^*=M_{\one_{a\Zhat}}.
\end{equation}
These formulas are well defined on almost-everywhere equivalence classes
by \eqref{eq:haar}.  Each family is multiplicative; distinct prime
isometries doubly commute, as follows from
$qx\in p\Zhat\iff x\in p\Zhat$ for $p\ne q$.

For a set $P$ of primes write
\[
 \mathcal M_P=\{k\geq1:\text{all prime factors of }k\text{ belong to }P\},
 \qquad C_P=\{r\geq1:p\nmid r\text{ for every }p\in P\}.
\]
Every $n\geq1$ factors uniquely as $n=d_P(n)r$, with
$d_P(n)\in\mathcal M_P$ and $r\in C_P$.  For finite $F$ put
\[
 E_F=\bigcap_{p\in F}(\Zhat\setminus p\Zhat),\quad
 \alpha_F=\prod_{p\in F}(1-p^{-1}),\quad
 d_F(n)=\prod_{p\in F}p^{v_p(n)},\quad K_{F,n}=d_F(n)E_F .
\]
Empty products are one.  CRT and \eqref{eq:haar} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shellmass}
 K_{F,n}=\{x:v_p(x)=v_p(n)\ (p\in F)\},\qquad
 \mu(K_{F,n})=\frac{\alpha_F}{d_F(n)}.
\end{equation}
Here $v_p$ is the valuation of the $p$-adic coordinate; its possible value
$+\infty$ does not satisfy any of the finite equalities in this display.

\section{The finite-to-global theorem needs only forward intertwining}

\begin{lemma}[Finite backward eigenvector calculation]\label{lem:eigen}
Let $G$ be a finite prime set, and let $h\in\Hd$ satisfy
$V_p^*h=\lambda_ph$ for $p\in G$, where $|\lambda_p|^2=1/p$.
Writing $h_n$ for its coordinates, one has
\[
 h_{kr}=\lambda_k h_r\quad(k\in\mathcal M_G,\ r\in C_G),\qquad
 \|h\|^2=\alpha_G^{-1}\sum_{r\in C_G}|h_r|^2,
\]
where $\lambda_k=\prod_{p\in G}\lambda_p^{v_p(k)}$.  In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eigenbound}
 |h_n|\leq\|h\|\sqrt{\alpha_G/d_G(n)}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The adjoint equation says $h_{pr}=\lambda_ph_r$ for every $r$.
Iterate it and use the unique factorization $n=kr$.
Nonnegative summation and the finite geometric product give
\[
 \sum_{k\in\mathcal M_G}|\lambda_k|^2
 =\prod_{p\in G}\sum_{j\geq0}p^{-j}=\alpha_G^{-1}.
\]
This proves the norm identity.  The $r$-summand corresponding to
$r=n/d_G(n)$ gives \eqref{eq:eigenbound}.
\end{proof}

Let $\D_m\subset\Hc$ be the $m$-dimensional space of functions depending
only on $x\bmod m$.

\begin{theorem}[Forward-only cylinder estimate and weak collapse]\label{thm:forward}
Suppose a bounded $T:\Hd\to\Hc$ satisfies $TV_p=U_pT$ for $p$ in a finite
set $F$.  For $g\in\D_m$, put $G=\{p\in F:p\nmid m\}$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cylinderbound}
 |\langle Te_n,g\rangle|
 \leq \|T\|\sqrt{\frac{m\alpha_G}{d_G(n)}}\,\|g\|_2.
\end{equation}
If $g$ is a joint eigenvector for the multiplication permutations
$g(x)\mapsto g(px)$ on $\D_m$, for $p\in G$, the factor $\sqrt m$
can be omitted.

Let $F_1\subset F_2\subset\cdots$ be finite sets with union $P$ and
$\sum_{p\in P}p^{-1}=\infty$.  If
\[
 \sup_j\|T_j\|<\infty,\qquad T_jV_p=U_pT_j\quad(p\in F_j),
\]
then $T_j\longrightarrow0$ in the weak operator topology.
In particular every bounded global forward intertwiner for $P$ is zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $p\nmid m$, the operator $R_pg(x)=g(px)$ on $\D_m$ permutes the
residue classes and hence is unitary for the normalized counting inner
product.  These operators commute.  Finite-dimensional simultaneous
diagonalization gives an orthonormal joint eigenbasis $g_1,\ldots,g_m$.
For each eigenvector $g_\ell$,
$U_p^*g_\ell=p^{-1/2}\omega_{p,\ell}g_\ell$ with
$|\omega_{p,\ell}|=1$.  Taking the adjoint of the \emph{forward}
intertwining equation gives
\[
 V_p^*T^*g_\ell=T^*U_p^*g_\ell
   =p^{-1/2}\omega_{p,\ell}T^*g_\ell.
\]
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:eigen} to $h=T^*g_\ell$, whose norm is at most $\|T\|$.
For $g=\sum_\ell c_\ell g_\ell$, the triangle inequality and
$\sum_\ell|c_\ell|\leq\sqrt m\,\|g\|_2$ give
\eqref{eq:cylinderbound}.  No equation $TV_p^*=U_p^*T$ was assumed.

For a fixed $m$, removing the finitely many primes dividing $m$ leaves
the reciprocal sum divergent.  Since
$-\log(1-1/p)\geq1/p$, the corresponding products $\alpha_{G_j}$
converge to zero.  Thus \eqref{eq:cylinderbound} proves convergence for
every fixed $e_n$ and every cylinder test $g$.
The finite linear span of the $e_n$ is dense in $\Hd$, and
$\bigcup_m\D_m$ is dense in $\Hc$: congruence cylinders generate the
profinite Borel sigma algebra and their simple functions are dense in
$L^2$.  The common norm bound extends the conclusion first to all
$g\in\Hc$, then to all $f\in\Hd$.  Explicitly the error from replacing
$(f,g)$ by $(f_0,g_0)$ is at most
$M\|f-f_0\|\|g\|+M\|f_0\|\|g-g_0\|$, where $M=\sup_j\|T_j\|$.
Finally a global intertwiner gives a constant sequence, so it must vanish.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What was strengthened]
The star-intertwiner obstruction in PR178 remains true, but its adjoint
hypothesis is not necessary for atomic-to-Haar bounded disjointness.
Adjoints have a different role: they yield the following exact support
constraint for arbitrary Haar tests.  No forward-only classification in
the summable regime is inferred from a star-intertwiner classification.
\end{remark}

\section{Sharp support under star intertwining}

\begin{theorem}[Exact shell constraint]\label{thm:shell}
Suppose $T:\Hd\to\Hc$ is bounded and
$TV_p=U_pT$, $TV_p^*=U_p^*T$ for every $p$ in a finite prime set $F$.
For every $n\geq1$, $Te_n$ vanishes almost everywhere off $K_{F,n}$, and
\begin{align}
 |\langle Te_n,g\rangle|
 &\leq\|T\|\,\|\one_{K_{F,n}}g\|_2
 &&(g\in L^2(\Zhat,\mu)),\label{eq:localtest}\\
 |\langle Te_n,g\rangle|
 &\leq\|T\|\,\|g\|_\infty
       \sqrt{\alpha_F/d_F(n)}
 &&(g\in L^\infty(\Zhat,\mu)).\label{eq:boundedtest}
\end{align}
The constant in \eqref{eq:boundedtest} is optimal for every $F,n$.
If $P$ is all primes, then for each fixed $n$ and all sufficiently large
$j$, $d_{F_j}(n)=n$, yielding the factor $\sqrt{\alpha_{F_j}/n}$.
For a proper prime subset one must retain $d_{F_j}(n)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $d=d_F(n)$ and $r=n/d$.  Since $r\in C_F$, $V_p^*e_r=0$
for all $p\in F$.  Therefore $U_p^*Te_r=0$.  By \eqref{eq:adjoints},
the joint kernel is exactly $L^2(E_F)$, so $Te_r$ is supported on $E_F$.
Forward intertwining gives $Te_n=U_dTe_r$, supported on $dE_F$.
Cauchy--Schwarz, $\|Te_n\|\leq\|T\|$, and \eqref{eq:shellmass} prove
both estimates.

For optimality use the orthogonal shell decompositions established in
Proposition~\ref{prop:boundary} (whose proof is independent of this estimate).
On the atomic wandering space send $e_r$ to
$\alpha_F^{-1/2}\one_{E_F}$ and every other basis vector to zero, and
extend by the same selected shifts.  This gives a norm-one star
intertwiner $S$ satisfying
\[
 Se_n=\sqrt{d/\alpha_F}\,\one_{dE_F}.
\]
The test $g=1$ attains equality in \eqref{eq:boundedtest}.
\end{proof}

\section{The summable complement and the topology boundary}

For an arbitrary prime set $P$ define
$E_P=\bigcap_{p\in P}(\Zhat\setminus p\Zhat)$ and
$\alpha_P=\mu(E_P)=\prod_{p\in P}(1-1/p)$, by continuity from above.

\begin{proposition}[Reconstructed sharp star-intertwiner classification]\label{prop:boundary}
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\sum_{p\in P}1/p<\infty$;
\item $\alpha_P>0$;
\item the restricted atomic and Haar tuples are unitarily equivalent;
\item a nonzero bounded star intertwiner $\Hd\to\Hc$ exists.
\end{enumerate}
In this regime put $W_{\mathrm d}=\ell^2(C_P)$ and
$W_{\mathrm c}=L^2(E_P,\mu)$.  There are orthogonal decompositions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wold}
 \Hd=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathcal M_P}V_kW_{\mathrm d},\qquad
 \Hc=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathcal M_P}U_kW_{\mathrm c}.
\end{equation}
Relative to these identifications, all bounded star intertwiners are
$I_{\ell^2(\mathcal M_P)}\otimes B$, for arbitrary bounded
$B:W_{\mathrm d}\to W_{\mathrm c}$, with the same norm.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $p\geq2$,
$1/p\leq-\log(1-1/p)\leq2/p$; this proves (1)$\Leftrightarrow$(2).
If the sum diverges, Theorem~\ref{thm:forward} excludes even a nonzero
bounded forward intertwiner.

Assume $\alpha_P>0$.  Unique integer factorization proves the atomic
decomposition.  The Haar sets $kE_P$, $k\in\mathcal M_P$, are pairwise
disjoint, since their selected valuations specify $k$.  They have masses
$\alpha_P/k$.  Monotone finite Euler products give
\[
 \sum_{k\in\mathcal M_P}\frac1k
 =\lim_{F\uparrow P}\prod_{p\in F}(1-1/p)^{-1}
 =\alpha_P^{-1}.
\]
Thus these sets cover Haar measure one.  The isometry $U_k$ maps
$L^2(E_P)$ onto $L^2(kE_P)$, proving the Haar decomposition.

The complement of $P$ is infinite, because the reciprocal sum over all
primes diverges.  In particular $C_P$ is countably infinite.
Also $E_P$ leaves the $\mathbb Z_q$ coordinate unrestricted for any
$q\notin P$; hence its positive finite measure is nonatomic and its
$L^2$ space is infinite-dimensional and separable.  Thus the two wandering
spaces are unitarily equivalent.  Any bounded $B$ extends by
$T(V_kw)=U_kBw$ on the orthogonal sums; norm equality and both
intertwining equations follow directly, including the lowering at
exponent zero.  A unitary $B$ yields a unitary $T$.

Conversely a star intertwiner sends the joint adjoint kernel
$W_{\mathrm d}$ into $W_{\mathrm c}$; forward intertwining determines
its values on every summand in \eqref{eq:wold}.  This proves the
classification and the remaining implications.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite unitaries collapse only weakly]\label{cor:unitary}
For every finite $F$ a unitary star intertwiner exists.  If $F_j$ exhausts
a prime set with divergent reciprocal sum, \emph{every} choice of such
unitaries $T_j$ converges WOT to zero.  It cannot have a strongly
convergent subsequence, and cannot converge in operator norm.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use Proposition~\ref{prop:boundary} and Theorem~\ref{thm:forward}.
A strong subsequential limit would also be the weak limit zero, but
$\|T_jf\|=\|f\|$ for every $f$.  This is impossible for nonzero $f$.
Norm convergence implies strong convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[All possible weak limits and failures of hypotheses]\label{prop:mutations}
For increasing $F_j$ with union $P$, every WOT cluster point of a uniformly
bounded family of finite-$F_j$ star intertwiners is a global $P$ star
intertwiner.  Conversely every bounded global star intertwiner is the
limit of a constant such family.  Thus, for a prescribed norm bound $M$,
the possible cluster points across all such families are exactly the
global star intertwiners of norm at most $M$.

Uniform boundedness cannot be omitted from the weak-collapse theorem.
Reciprocal divergence cannot be omitted even for unitary star intertwiners.
Adjoints cannot be omitted from the exact shell support conclusion,
although they can be omitted from weak collapse.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Multiplication on either side by a fixed bounded operator is WOT
continuous.  Every selected prime belongs to all sufficiently late $F_j$,
so the intertwining equations pass to cluster points.  Norm-closed balls
are WOT closed.  The converse is the constant-family construction.
Existence of cluster points follows, if needed, from compactness of the
bounded operator ball in WOT; it is metrizable here because both Hilbert
spaces are separable.

For the first failure, choose a finite-$F$ star intertwiner $R_F$ whose
wandering restriction sends $e_1$ to $\alpha_F^{-1}\one_{E_F}$ and
annihilates all other atomic wandering basis vectors.  The construction
in Proposition~\ref{prop:boundary} gives
$\|R_F\|=\alpha_F^{-1/2}$ and
$\langle R_Fe_1,1\rangle=1$.  Along a divergent exhaustion this is not
weakly zero.

For the second failure take any summable $P$, including an infinite
sparse choice of distinct primes $p_j>2^j$.  A global unitary $T$ exists,
so $T_j=T$ does not collapse.  The family $T_j=(-1)^jT$ shows that no
convergence is automatic even in that regime.

For the third failure take $F=\{2\}$ and a norm-one star intertwiner
$S$ with $Se_1=\sqrt2\,\one_{E_F}$.  Then $T=U_2S$ still intertwines the
forward shift $V_2$, but $Te_1=2\one_{2E_F}$ is supported off $E_F$.
Furthermore $U_2^*Te_1=Se_1\ne0=TV_2^*e_1$, explicitly detecting the
missing adjoint relation.
\end{proof}

\section{Distributional transport: an exact interface category}

Let $\D=\bigcup_{m\geq1}\D_m$ be the complex locally constant functions
on $\Zhat$, with the locally convex inductive limit topology along
$\D_{j!}$.  Each stage is finite-dimensional.  Every algebraic linear
functional is continuous, because its restrictions to the stages are
continuous and the inductive limit has that universal property.
Equip $\D'$ with the weak dual topology
$\sigma(\D',\D)$ and use the \emph{bilinear} distribution pairing.
The operators $U_p$ and $U_p^*$ preserve $\D$ continuously: each sends a
finite cylinder stage into a finite cylinder stage.  Their transposes
are the continuous weak-dual operators
\[
 \langle\mathsf U_p\eta,\phi\rangle=\langle\eta,U_p^*\phi\rangle,\qquad
 \langle\mathsf A_p\eta,\phi\rangle=\langle\eta,U_p\phi\rangle .
\]
The symbol $\mathsf A_p$ is a transpose action, not a Hilbert adjoint on
$\D'$.  On Dirac distributions they act as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dirac}
 \mathsf U_p\delta_x=p^{-1/2}\delta_{px},\qquad
 \mathsf A_p\delta_x=
 \begin{cases}\sqrt p\,\delta_{x/p}&x\in p\Zhat,\\0&x\notin p\Zhat.\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Algebraic vacuum classification]\label{prop:vacuum}
Linear maps $J_\eta:c_{00}(\N_+)\to\D'$ satisfying
$J_\eta V_p=\mathsf U_pJ_\eta$ and
$J_\eta V_p^*=\mathsf A_pJ_\eta$ for every prime are in bijection with
distributions $\eta$ supported on $\Zhat^\times$, by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jeta}
 J_\eta e_n=n^{-1/2}(m_n)_*\eta,\qquad \eta=J_\eta e_1.
\end{equation}
Every nonzero such $J_\eta$ is injective.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Forward equations force \eqref{eq:jeta}.  The lowering equations at $e_1$
give $\mathsf A_p\eta=0$.  The image $U_p\D$ consists of all locally
constant tests supported in $p\Zhat$, up to the harmless scalar
$\sqrt p$.  Hence $\eta$ annihilates tests supported there.  A locally
constant test supported off $\Zhat^\times$ has compact support covered
by finitely many such clopen sets; refining their Boolean partition
shows that it too is annihilated.  This is precisely the asserted support
condition.

Conversely, support on the units makes the lowered image in
\eqref{eq:jeta} zero when $p\nmid n$ and makes it
$J_\eta e_{n/p}$ when $p\mid n$, by \eqref{eq:dirac} or the same test
calculation for general distributions.  This proves both equations.
For distinct $n$, the compact sets $n\Zhat^\times$ are disjoint: their
valuations at all primes recover $n$.  A finite collection of disjoint
compact sets can be separated by clopen neighborhoods.  The pushforward
under $m_n$ is injective on distributions, since every test on $\Zhat$
can be transferred to $n\Zhat$ and extended by zero.  Therefore no
nontrivial finite linear combination of the nonzero distributions
$(m_n)_*\eta$ can vanish.  This proves injectivity.
\end{proof}

Fix the additional choice $\eta=\delta_1$, and write
$Je_n=n^{-1/2}\delta_n$.  Put on $c_{00}(\N_+)$ the initial locally convex
topology $\tau_J$ generated by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau}
 a\longmapsto\left|\sum_n a_n n^{-1/2}\phi(n)\right|,
 \qquad \phi\in\D .
\end{equation}
This topology is specified by the chosen tests, not by the atomic norm.

\begin{theorem}[Typed completion and universal property]\label{thm:completion}
The map $J$ identifies $(c_{00}(\N_+),\tau_J)$ with a dense linear
subspace of the complete Hausdorff locally convex space
$(\D',\sigma(\D',\D))$.  Thus $\D'$ is its completion.
For every complete Hausdorff locally convex space $Y$ and every continuous
linear $L:(c_{00},\tau_J)\to Y$, there is a unique continuous linear
$\overline L:\D'\to Y$ satisfying $\overline L J=L$.

More particularly, suppose $Y$ carries continuous linear operators
$B_p,C_p$ and $L$ satisfies $LV_p=B_pL$, $LV_p^*=C_pL$.
Then its unique extension satisfies
$\overline L\mathsf U_p=B_p\overline L$ and
$\overline L\mathsf A_p=C_p\overline L$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Injectivity follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:vacuum}, or separation
of any finite set of distinct integers by residue tests.  The initial
topology makes $J$ a topological embedding.
For density fix $\eta\in\D'$ and a finite family of tests.  All those
tests lie in one $\D_m$.  Put
$b_r=\langle\eta,\one_{r+m\Zhat}\rangle$ for $r=0,\ldots,m-1$, and let
\[
 a=\sum_{n=1}^m\sqrt n\,b_{n\bmod m}e_n.
\]
Then $Ja$ agrees exactly with $\eta$ on every test in $\D_m$.
It therefore meets every weak neighborhood specified by the chosen
finite tests, proving density.

A pointwise Cauchy net of linear functionals on $\D$ has a pointwise
limit which is a linear functional.  Every such functional is continuous
for the stated inductive limit topology, so the limit belongs to $\D'$.
This proves weak-dual completeness; the topology is Hausdorff because
the tests separate functionals.  Equivalently $\D'$ is the closed
subspace of $\mathbb C^{\D}$ cut out by the linearity equations.

A continuous linear map between topological vector spaces is uniformly
continuous for their additive uniformities.  Given $\eta$, take a
Cauchy net $Ja_i\to\eta$; completeness of $Y$ supplies the limit of
$La_i$.  Uniform continuity makes that limit independent of the
approximating net, and defines a continuous linear extension.  Density
and Hausdorffness give uniqueness.  These are exactly the completion
extension properties, not a universal property in a larger undeclared
category.  Finally all source and target actions are continuous, so
the equivariance equations on the dense image of $J$ extend to $\D'$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Why this completion cannot preserve the Hilbert interface]\label{prop:hilbert}
There is no continuous extension of $J$ from the unweighted Hilbert space
$\Hd$ to weak $\D'$.  There is no continuous linear map
$R:\D'\to\Hd$ satisfying $RJe_n=e_n$ for every $n$.
More generally, every continuous linear map from $(c_{00},\tau_J)$ into
a normed space factors through one finite cylinder projection
$a\mapsto Ja|_{\D_m}$, and therefore has finite rank.
Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:completion} does not supply a universal carrier
preserving both all locally constant tests and the atomic Hilbert norm.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Evaluation at the constant test $1$ would make
$a\mapsto\sum_n a_n/\sqrt n$ a bounded functional on $\ell^2$.
But for $H_N=\sum_{n=1}^N1/n$ and
$a^{(N)}=H_N^{-1/2}\sum_{n=1}^N n^{-1/2}e_n$, one has
$\|a^{(N)}\|=1$ and $\langle Ja^{(N)},1\rangle=\sqrt{H_N}\to\infty$.
For the second claim, $Je_n\to0$ weakly in $\D'$ since
$|\langle Je_n,\phi\rangle|\leq\|\phi\|_\infty/\sqrt n$ for every
locally constant $\phi$.  A continuous $R$ would force $e_n\to0$ in
Hilbert norm, a contradiction.
For the last assertion, continuity at zero bounds the norm of $La$ by
a constant times the maximum of finitely many defining seminorms
\eqref{eq:tau}.  To see the bound also when all those seminorms vanish,
apply continuity to every scalar multiple of $a$; then $La=0$.
All the finitely many tests lie in some $\D_m$, so
$\ker(a\mapsto Ja|_{\D_m})\subseteq\ker L$.  This yields the claimed
factorization through the $m$-dimensional cylinder dual.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The choice of interface is substantive]
The topology $\tau_J$ is the weakest locally convex topology making the
specified test pairings continuous.  The completion theorem is a
classical completion principle applied to an explicit dense arithmetic
map.  It does not say that $\D'$ is initial among all action-preserving
carriers, nor that $\delta_1$ is forced by the actions: every
unit-supported vacuum in Proposition~\ref{prop:vacuum} is allowed.
For example $\delta_u$ for $u\in\Zhat^\times$ yields another map.
Requiring the integer-point origin fixes $\delta_1$ as additional data.
Nor do the transpose actions define a positive or signed Weil form.
\end{remark}

\section{Prior art, receipts, and the remaining boundary}

Finite doubly commuting pure isometries admit the standard wandering-space
model; Sarkar's Corollary~3.2 and Theorem~3.3~\cite{sarkar} explicitly give
the finite-tuple decomposition and its multiplicity-space interpretation.
Thus the mechanism behind the finite cases of
Proposition~\ref{prop:boundary} is classical.  Dan--Guo's
Definition~1.1 and Corollary~1.4 distinguish the additional
Beurling-generation property for infinite sequences; their
Proposition~3.4 and Remark~3.5 exhibit the failure of nonzero joint
defect without that property~\cite{danguo}.  No novelty is claimed for
failure of a naive countable Wold extension.  Bost--Connes~\cite{bc}
is adjacent arithmetic operator-algebra context, not a cited proof of
the present selected-prime criterion or coefficient estimates.

The contributions new to this repository are the finite-family
quantifiers, the sharp $d_F(n)$ shell estimate, the forward-only
cylinder argument, and the explicit completion interface and its
counterexamples.  The methods are elementary CRT, geometric series,
finite simultaneous diagonalization, density, and completion theory.
The bounded literature check above does not establish scholarly priority
