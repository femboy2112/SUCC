## 1. The correct lattice after rank drop

Let `m>=1`, `d_1,...,d_m,q>=1`, and

\[
 \Lambda=\{x\in\mathbb Z^m:d_i\mid x_i,\ q\mid\textstyle\sum_i x_i\},
 \qquad P=\prod_i d_i,\quad g=\gcd_i d_i.
\]

**Theorem G1 (ambient index).**

\[
 [\mathbb Z^m:\Lambda]=E=Pq/\gcd(q,g).
\]

**Proof.** The diagonal lattice `L0=diag(d_i) Z^m` has index `P`.
The homomorphism `L0 -> Z/qZ`, `x -> sum(x_i)`, has image the subgroup
generated by the `d_i`, of order `q/gcd(q,g)`. Its kernel is `Lambda`.
Multiply the two indices. A basis determinant of `Lambda` has absolute value
`E`; every determinant of `m` lattice vectors is a multiple of it. Thus `E`
is the exact generator of the full-rank determinantal ideal. This gives an
independent proof of PR #214's effective exterior modulus. □

Suppose an exterior argument locates all points of a stratum in a rational
subspace `V` of rank `1<=r<=m`. Let `B` be an integer `m` by `r` matrix whose
columns form a basis of the **saturated** lattice `L=V intersect Z^m`.
Define

\[
 \Phi:\mathbb Z^r\longrightarrow
 \bigoplus_{i=1}^m\mathbb Z/d_i\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z,
 \qquad z\longmapsto ((Bz)_i\bmod d_i,\ \mathbf1^TBz\bmod q).
\]

**Theorem G2 (actual section index and covolume).** If `K=ker Phi` and
`h=|im Phi|`, then

\[
 \Gamma=\Lambda\cap V=BK,\qquad [L:\Gamma]=h,\qquad
 \operatorname{covol}_V(\Gamma)=h\sqrt{\det(B^TB)}.
\]

In particular the ambient `E` must not be substituted for `h`.

**Proof.** Saturation makes `z -> Bz` an isomorphism from `Z^r` onto every
integer point of `V`. The congruences become exactly `Phi(z)=0`.
The finite homomorphism theorem gives `h`; the Gram determinant formula and
the index multiplication rule give the covolume. □

### Constructing and checking the section

The implementation checks that the gcd of all maximal minors of `B` is 1.
This implies saturation directly: each nonsingular row minor gives an
integer adjugate left inverse times that minor; an integer Bezout combination
of the minors gives an integer left inverse `T` with `TB=I`. Thus if an
integer `x=Bz` lies in the rational span, `z=Tx` is integer. Conversely a
saturated integer basis has this familiar determinantal-divisor property
(Smith normal form). Only the proved sufficient direction is needed for
the acceptance guard.

Each congruence `a z=0 mod n` is imposed on the current coordinate lattice.
Pairwise Bezout column operations give a unimodular `U` with
`aU=(gcd(a),0,...,0)` (or the zero row). Multiplying the first column by
`n/gcd(n,gcd(a))` gives a basis of its entire kernel. Repeating over all
constraints constructs `K`; this is not merely a collection of valid
vectors. The determinant is checked against the product of step indices.
The implementation also checks all original congruences and the Gram
determinant identity. This is an exact, finite, Python arithmetic computation,
not an authenticated universal proof receipt.

### A positive-source counterexample to ambient-index inheritance

For every integer `M>=3`, take `d_i=1`, `q=M` in dimension three, and

\[
 B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\M-1&-1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 V=\{x:x_2+x_3=(M-1)x_1\}.
\]

This `B` is saturated (one maximal minor is 1). On `V`, `sum(x_i)=Mx_1`,
so the congruence is automatic: `h=1` while `E=M`. At cutoff `X=M` the
`M-2` points `(1,j,M-1-j)`, `1<=j<=M-2`, are all positive and globally
primitive. This is an exact counterexample to `h=E`, not an assertion that
the full three-dimensional high-`E` rank-drop condition holds here. The
example also shows why positivity does not repair index inheritance.

A nonsaturated input is a separate failure mode: the column `(2,2)` does
not enumerate all integer points of the line `x_1=x_2`; `(1,1)` is missing.
The API rejects this input instead of certifying its incomplete density.

## 2. Quantified counts on the rank-r section

### 2.1 Exact projected-rectangle enclosure

Choose `r` coordinate rows `I` for which `B_I` is invertible. Projection to
these coordinates is injective on `V`. For a basis `C` of `Gamma`, let
`H=C_I U` be any upper triangular integer basis of its projected lattice,
where `U` is integral unimodular; column Hermite normal form is one choice.
Let its positive diagonal be `h_1,...,h_r`. The projected index is

\[
 \Delta_I=\prod_i h_i=|\det C_I|=h|\det B_I|.
\]

**Theorem G3.** In any product of consecutive integer intervals of lengths
`N_1,...,N_r>=0`, the number `A` of points of any translate of the projected
lattice satisfies

\[
 \prod_i\lfloor N_i/h_i\rfloor\le A\le
 \prod_i\lceil N_i/h_i\rceil.
\]

Consequently, with `M0=product(N_i)/Delta_I`,

\[
 |A-M0|\le\max\left(M0-\prod_i\lfloor N_i/h_i\rfloor,
                      \prod_i\lceil N_i/h_i\rceil-M0\right).
\]

**Proof.** Solve the triangular system from the last coordinate upwards.
For each choice of later coordinates, the next coordinate lies in one
residue class modulo its diagonal `h_i`. Its number of choices is between
the floor and ceiling displayed, independently of that residue. Multiply
the bounds inductively. □

Positive simplex-section points with sum at most `X` project into a box of
length `floor(X)` in each selected coordinate. Thus the upper bound applies
to that source. **The lower rectangle bound does not apply to its simplex
subset.** The HNF change of basis is rechecked by computing `C_I^{-1}H` and
verifying that it is integral with determinant `+1` or `-1`.

### 2.2 An actual simplex-section volume theorem with explicit boundary budget

This theorem counts **all** congruence-admissible positive integer points;
global coprimality is not inserted by metadata. Its upper bound also bounds
any primitive subfamily. Obtaining a primitive asymptotic requires an
additional sieve or Mobius argument.

Let `C=(c_1,...,c_r)` be any basis of the actual lattice `Gamma`. Assume `V`
contains a strictly positive vector. Choose any `z in V` with all coordinates
strictly positive and `sum(z_i)<1`. Write

\[
 R_i=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^r|C_{ij}|,\qquad
 R_\Sigma=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^r\left|\sum_i C_{ij}\right|,
 \qquad
 \beta=\max\left(\max_i\frac{R_i}{z_i},
                   \frac{R_\Sigma}{1-\sum_i z_i}\right).
\]

Let `K_1={x in V:x_i>=0, sum(x_i)<=1}`, and define

\[
 a_V=\frac{\operatorname{vol}_r(K_1)}{\operatorname{covol}_V(\Gamma)}>0,
 \qquad
 A_\Gamma(X)=\#\{x\in\Gamma:x_i>0,\ \sum_i x_i\le X\}.
\]

**Theorem G4.** For every real `X>=0`,

\[
 \boxed{a_V(X-\beta)_+^r\ \le A_\Gamma(X)\ \le a_V(X+\beta)^r.}
\]

In particular

\[
 |A_\Gamma(X)-a_VX^r|
 \le a_V((X+\beta)^r-X^r).
\]

For any moving family of these data, the relative error tends uniformly to
zero if `r beta/X -> 0`. No implicit constant hides changes in `V`, `C`,
the moduli, or the dimension.

**Proof.** Use the centered half-open fundamental cell
`F={sum_j t_j c_j:-1/2<=t_j<1/2}`. It tiles `V`, up to boundaries of
measure zero, by translates by `Gamma`; each cell has volume `covol(Gamma)`.
Every `v in F` has `|v_i|<=R_i` and `|sum(v_i)|<=R_Sigma`.

If `lambda` is counted, `lambda+F` belongs to

\[
 K^+ = Xz+(X+\beta)(K_1-z).
\]

Indeed this homothetic polytope has inequalities
`x_i>=-beta z_i` and `sum(x_i)<=X+beta(1-sum z_i)`, which contain the cell.
Its volume is `(X+beta)^r vol(K_1)`. Disjoint cells give the upper bound.

If `X>beta`, the interior relative to `V` of

\[
 K^- = Xz+(X-\beta)(K_1-z)
\]

has `x_i>beta z_i>=R_i` and
`sum(x_i)<X-beta(1-sum z_i)<=X-R_Sigma`. The center `lambda` of the
unique tile containing any such `x` satisfies `lambda_i>0` and
`sum(lambda_i)<X`. Thus this entire interior is covered by counted cells,
and its volume is `(X-beta)^r vol(K_1)`. Every coordinate functional is
nonzero on `V`, since `z_i>0`; discarding its facet boundaries loses no
`r`-dimensional volume. If `X<=beta` the lower bound is zero. Convexity of
`u^r` shows that the positive boundary error dominates the negative one.
Finally `(1+beta/X)^r-1 <= exp(r beta/X)-1`. □

The cell budget is basis dependent and can be conservative. It is explicitly
computable from exact integer/rational data and cannot be omitted when
moduli or subspace heights move. If `V` has no strictly positive vector,
the positive source is empty; one must not fabricate an interior center.
The current implementation computes `beta` and the two powers, not a generic
polytope-volume oracle. A claimed `a_V` must still be derived for the actual
section. For the counterexample above,

\[
 a_V=(M-1)/(2M^2),\qquad
 A_\Gamma(X)=(M-1)T(T+1)/2-T,\quad T=\lfloor X/M\rfloor.
\]

These follow by using coordinates `(k,x_3)` with
`0<=k<=X/M`, `0<=x_3<=(M-1)k`; the Euclidean Jacobian cancels against
the section covolume. They provide an independent exact check of G4.

## 3. Exact logarithmic correction for a growing dimension

For integers `1<=m<=N`, put

\[
 B(N,m)=\frac{m!\binom Nm}{N^m}
       =\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(1-j/N),\qquad \rho=(m-1)/N<1.
\]

For every integer `k>=1`, define the exact rational quantities

\[
 L_k=\sum_{\ell=1}^k\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}j^\ell}{\ell N^\ell},\qquad
 T_k=\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}j^{k+1}}{(k+1)N^{k+1}}.
\]

**Theorem S1 (all finite parameters).**

\[
 \boxed{-\log B(N,m)=L_k+R_k,\qquad T_k\le R_k\le T_k/(1-\rho).}
\]

Consequently

\[
 e^{-L_k-T_k/(1-\rho)}\le B(N,m)\le e^{-L_k-T_k}.
\]

**Proof.** For `0<=u<1`, the positive series
`-log(1-u)=sum_{ell>=1}u^ell/ell` has tail at least
`u^(k+1)/(k+1)` and at most that quantity divided by `1-u`. Sum over
`u=j/N` and use `u<=rho`. There is no interchange of infinite sums over an
unbounded number of residues: each `N,m` calculation contains finitely many
terms `j`. □

Since `sum_{j<m} j^(k+1) <= m^(k+2)/(k+2)`, the remainder is at most

\[
 \epsilon_k(N,m)=
 \frac{m^{k+2}}{(k+1)(k+2)N^{k+1}(1-(m-1)/N)}.
\]

For any fixed depth `k`, if `m^(k+2)/N^(k+1) -> 0`, then
`exp(L_k) B(N,m) -> 1`. This is a corrected relative asymptotic, extending
the uncorrected square-root dimension range. At depth one it applies for
`m=o(N^(2/3))`; at depth two for `m=o(N^(3/4))`. If `m=o(N)` one may instead
choose a depth tending to infinity so that the displayed explicit remainder
tends to zero. There is no claim of constant computational cost for that
choice.

In particular

\[
 L_1=\frac{m(m-1)}{2N},\qquad
 T_1=\frac{m(m-1)(2m-1)}{12N^2}.
\]

This classifies the original normalization at `W=1`, integer cutoff `N`;
every limit in the following table is taken as `N->infinity`:

| Dimension regime | Consequence |
|---|---|
| `m=o(sqrt(N))` | `B(N,m)->1`. |
| `m~c sqrt(N)`, fixed `c>0` | `B(N,m)->exp(-c^2/2)`. |
| `sqrt(N)<<m=o(N)` | `log B(N,m)~ -m^2/(2N)`; the uncorrected volume ratio tends to zero. |
| `m~alpha N`, fixed `0<alpha<1` | Entropy law S2 below; also `N^-1 log B -> -alpha-(1-alpha)log(1-alpha)` by the Riemann sum for `log(1-u)`. |
| `m=N` | Count is exactly one; S1 remains valid, although its low-depth error is loose. |
| `m>N` | Count is zero; no finite logarithm of the volume ratio exists. |

For real cutoff `X`, replace `N` by `floor(X)` in these unrestricted counts;
no ambiguous rounding convention is used.

## 4. A fully uniform entropy enclosure, including the linear regime

**Theorem S2.** For all integers `N>=0` and `0<=m<=N`, with `0^0=1`,

\[
 E(N,m)=\frac{N^N}{m^m(N-m)^{N-m}},\qquad
 \boxed{\frac{E(N,m)}{N+1}\le\binom Nm\le E(N,m).}
\]

For `N>0`, `E=exp(N H(m/N))` where
`H(p)=-p log p-(1-p)log(1-p)`, with the continuous endpoint values.
Thus the logarithmic error is between `-log(N+1)` and zero, uniformly in
every dimension. If `N->infinity` and `m/N->alpha in [0,1]`, then
`N^-1 log binom(N,m)->H(alpha)`. This is an additive-log asymptotic,
**not** a relative-error estimate. If `m>N`, the count and the implemented
enclosure are both zero.

The growth hypothesis is essential: the constant sequence `(N,m)=(2,1)`
has `m/N=1/2` but `N^-1 log binom(N,m)=(log 2)/2`, whereas
`H(1/2)=log 2`. The finite enclosure remains valid in that case.

**Proof.** The endpoint cases are immediate. For `0<m<N` put `p=m/N`.
The binomial probabilities
`b_j=binom(N,j)p^j(1-p)^(N-j)` sum to one. Their successive ratio is
`(N-j)m/((j+1)(N-m))`; it is greater than one before `j=m` and less than
one afterwards (check the adjacent ratios at `m-1` and `m`). Hence `b_m`
is a largest term, and `1/(N+1)<=b_m<=1`. Multiplication by `E` proves
the claim. □

The exponential entropy expression is **rational** at integer parameters.
The implementation compares exact fractions, without evaluating logarithms
or exponentials or importing an unquantified Stirling approximation.

## 5. Uniformity over residues and growing moduli

Fix `m,W>=1`, real `X>=0`, and a canonical residue vector modulo `W`.
Let `rho_i in {1,...,W}` be its positive representatives and define

\[
 t=X/W,\quad n=\left\lfloor\frac{X-\sum_i\rho_i}{W}\right\rfloor,
 \quad N_\rho=n+m.
\]

The exact count is zero if `n<0`, and `binom(N_rho,m)` otherwise. In an
occupied stratum **S1 and S2 apply verbatim with this `N_rho`**. There is
no condition that `W` remain fixed. In particular

\[
 t-1<N_\rho\le t+m-m/W.
\]

For any finite distinct residue set `R` with at least one occupied stratum,
define the corrected main term

\[
 A_k(R)=\sum_{\rho\in R:\,N_\rho\ge m}
 \frac{N_\rho^m}{m!}\exp(-L_k(N_\rho,m)).
\]

**Theorem S3 (dimension-renormalized uniform transport).** If
`epsilon=max_{occupied rho} epsilon_k(N_rho,m)`, then

\[
 \boxed{e^{-\epsilon}A_k(R)\le N_R^{(m)}(X)\le A_k(R),\qquad
 0\le1-N_R^{(m)}(X)/A_k(R)\le\epsilon.}
\]

This follows by the nonnegative termwise bounds in S1. Thus, for fixed `k`,
`t->infinity` and `m^(k+2)/t^(k+1)->0` give a uniform relative asymptotic
over **all** moduli and all nonempty residue sets. The latter condition
implies `m=o(t)`, so every residue is eventually occupied and
`N_rho/t->1` uniformly. The residue-dependent shift in `N_rho` must still
be kept inside the corrected main term. An empty residue set has exact
count zero and no relative-error ratio.

**Counterexample to a residue-independent square-root correction.** Take
`W=2`, `X=2T` with integer `T`, and `m~c sqrt(T)`. For the class with all
positive representatives equal to two, `N_rho=T` and the ratio to
`T^m/m!` tends to `exp(-c^2/2)`. For all representatives equal to one,
`N_rho=T+floor(m/2)` and the same ratio tends to one: the population shift
contributes `exp(c^2/2)` and cancels the first logarithmic correction.
Therefore the correction cannot depend only on `m` and `X/W` when residues
vary. The exact floor and positive representatives are part of the source.
