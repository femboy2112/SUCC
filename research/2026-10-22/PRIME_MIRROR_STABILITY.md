# Quantitative prime-mirror rigidity: a gap and a certified fidelity bound

Date 2026-10-22. **PROVED PAPER**, using classical unilateral-shift and
commuting-projection arguments. **PROVED LOCAL / EXACT FINITE** Q(i) oracle.
No academic priority or Lean certification asserted. This is a quantitative

## S1. Contract

For a normalisable complete set of mirror coefficients c_p, do small mirror
residuals force closeness to the uniquely reconstructed arithmetic state?
The answer is yes for a specified sum of squared **full-space** residuals,
with an explicit gap. Finitely many residuals alone control distance only to
the corresponding larger joint kernel. The closure/tail hypothesis is essential.
The finite oracle preserves leakage and exactly projects onto an **infinite**
geometric kernel using its proved closed formula; it does not replace that
kernel by a truncated matrix nullspace.

Let a in ell²(N>=1) denote the diagonal-pair coefficients, identifying
sum a_n|n,n> with a. Suppose all |c_p|>1 and sum_p |c_p|^-2<infinity.
Let f be the unique unit solution with positive a_1 to all mirror equations,
so f_n=C product_p c_p^-v_p(n). Set

    D_p=I-c_p V_p*,       d_p=|c_p|-1>0,
    P_p=orthogonal projection onto ker D_p.

The original bipartite residual has precisely norm ||D_p a||: its coefficient
at |pn,n> is (D_p a)_n. All statements below concern the declared diagonal-pair
source subspace, not arbitrary off-diagonal bipartite states.

## S2. Single-prime gap

**Lemma.** For every complex c with |c|>1 and unilateral shift T on ell²(N_0),
D=I-c T* has one-dimensional kernel generated by c^-k. Moreover

    ||Dx||² >= (|c|-1)² dist(x,ker D)².                 (S2.1)

**Proof.** The kernel recurrence is x_(k+1)=x_k/c and is summable because
|c|>1. Since T is an isometry,

    ||D*y||=||y-conjugate(c)Ty|| >= (|c|-1)||y||.

Thus ran D* is closed and equals (ker D)^perp. For x=D*y in this perpendicular
space, ||x||²=<y,Dx> <= ||y|| ||Dx|| <= ||x|| ||Dx||/(|c|-1), proving
(S2.1) there. Remove the kernel component to prove it for every x. This proof
uses only Hilbert adjoints, triangle inequality and the closed-range identity.
The same bound holds after tensoring with any spectator space: the inequality
for I⊗D follows on finite sums, then by continuity. QED.

Unique factorisation makes V_p a shift of the p-valuation factor, so S2
implies ||D_p a||² >= d_p² ||(I-P_p)a||². Projections P_p,P_q commute for
distinct primes, since they act on different prime tensor factors. No
commutation of the additive successor S with these projections is claimed.

## S3. Finite-prime and complete-source stability

For every nonempty finite set F of primes define P_F=product_(p in F)P_p.
Commuting projections satisfy

    I-P_F <= sum_(p in F)(I-P_p).

Indeed I-PQ=(I-P)+P(I-Q) <= (I-P)+(I-Q), then induct. Consequently

    sum_(p in F)||D_p a||² >= [min_(p in F)d_p²] ||(I-P_F)a||²,     (S3.1)
    sum_(p in F)||D_p a||²/d_p² >= ||(I-P_F)a||².                 (S3.2)

These bounds apply to every a in ell² because F is finite and each D_p bounded.

The decreasing projections P_F converge strongly to the projection onto
intersection_p ker D_p. To verify this standard fact directly, the squared
norms ||P_F a||² decrease, and for F⊂G,
||P_F a-P_G a||²=||P_F a||²-||P_G a||². The limit lies in every range P_p,
fixes every vector in their intersection, and is orthogonal to its complement.
Prime-mirror rigidity identifies this intersection as C f.

Taking limits in S3.2 proves the **weighted global stability theorem**

    ||a||²-|<f,a>|² <= sum_p ||D_p a||²/d_p².             (S3.3)

The right side is an extended nonnegative sum; a finite useful bound requires
membership in its quadratic-form domain. For a normalised source this is an
upper bound on infidelity with the uniquely reconstructed arithmetic TFD.

Likewise, delta=inf_p d_p² is strictly positive: summability implies |c_p|→infinity
outside finite prime sets, and each of the finitely many remaining d_p is positive.
Thus the **unweighted global gap** is

    sum_p ||D_p a||² >= delta (||a||²-|<f,a>|²).           (S3.4)

No optimality claim for these constants is needed. Thermal c_p=p^(beta/2)
gives delta=(2^(beta/2)-1)².

**Form-domain caution.** Every nonzero finite-support a has infinite unweighted
energy: for p greater than its largest support index, V_p*a=0, so each residual
has norm ||a||. S3.4 is then true but uninformative. It is nonvacuous, for example,
for states with finitely many modified prime factors and all other factors
equal to f's geometric factors; only those finitely many residuals are nonzero.
These finite prime-factor modifications span a dense subspace in the thermal
reference sector. The weighted energy in S3.3 is finite for every finite-support
a, since sum_p d_p^-2<infinity (eventually d_p^-2<=4|c_p|^-2).
Both energy forms are closed on their natural domains: a sequence Cauchy in
the form norm converges in H and in the Hilbert direct sum of the bounded
D_p outputs; coordinatewise continuity identifies the limit outputs as D_p a.

## S4. Exact projection into the infinite finite-prime kernel

For finite F and real rational c_p>1, put

    B_F=product_(p in F)(1-c_p^-2),
    b(s)=product_(p in F)c_p^-v_p(s), for F-smooth s.

Decompose n=r s, with r coprime to every prime in F. The normalised kernel
vector on each r-chain has coefficient sqrt(B_F)b(s). For any finite-support
complex a, direct orthogonal projection therefore gives the rational formula

    ||P_F a||²=B_F sum_(r F-rough) |sum_(s F-smooth)b(s)a_(rs)|².  (S4.1)

Every inner sum is finite because a has finite support; the normalisation
B_F comes from the analytically summed infinite geometric tail. Q(i) inputs
give an exact rational squared norm even though sqrt(B_F) need not be rational.
`finite_prime_kernel_projection_squared` implements S4.1. Its independent gap
certificate computes the full sparse residuals and checks S3.1 with exact
nonnegative slack. For a=e_1, F={2},c_2=2, it gives projection²=3/4,
distance²=1/4, and energy=1. For F={2,3},c=(2,3), the projection² is 2/3.

A deliberately compressed thermal prefix has zero compressed residual but
strictly positive S4 distance. This catches the invalid attempt to use a
finite matrix equality as a full-space reconstruction certificate.

## S5. A finite certified lower bound for infinite thermal fidelity

Now let c_p=p^s with integer s>=1 (beta=2s), and let a be a **declared**
finite-support source, supported in {1,...,N}. Choose integer P>=max(N,2).
For p>P, V_p*a=0. Since p^s-1 >= p^s(1-2^-s),

    sum_(p>P) ||D_p a||²/(p^s-1)²
      <= ||a||² (1-2^-s)^-2 sum_(n>P)n^-2s
      <= ||a||² (1-2^-s)^-2 P^(1-2s)/(2s-1).           (S5.1)

This is a genuine analytic lift from exact finite arithmetic, using a known
finite-support source and an integral-test majorant. It is not a universal
claim about unknown sources that merely matched finite probes.

Combining S3.3 and S5.1 gives

    Fidelity(a/||a||, Omega_(2s))
      >= max(0, 1 - sum_(prime p<=P)||D_p a||²/[||a||²(p^s-1)²]
                    - (1-2^-s)^-2 P^(1-2s)/(2s-1)).     (S5.2)

All quantities on the right are exact rationals for Q(i) a. The module function
`thermal_mirror_fidelity_certificate` implements precisely this bound. It
rejects a declared support outside P. No zeta zeros, analytic continuation,
or floating spectral estimates enter the certificate.

Actual exact local controls:

* beta=2, a_n=1/n for n<=100, P=100: certified fidelity lower >9/10;
  omitted-prime energy majorant exactly 1/25.
* beta=4, a_n=1/n² for n<=16, P=16: certified fidelity lower >99/100;

Decimal diagnostics are not the certification. The checks compare Fractions

## S6. Adversarial scope and verdict

* Positive: exact geometric mirrors; original prime enumeration; S4 vacuum
  projection; finite thermal-prefix fidelity certification.
* Negative: wrong mirror orientation; arbitrary amplitude at6; altered prime

* Null: zero source cannot be normalised; empty finite prime set carries no
