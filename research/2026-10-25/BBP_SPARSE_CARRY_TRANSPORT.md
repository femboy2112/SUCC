## 2. A sparse carry kernel with an unrestricted future

**Theorem SCT-1 (PAPER-PROVED, generic transport).** Suppose two bounded
carry tapes differ by $d_t$, with $|d_t|\le16$ and $d_t=0$ outside a set
$E\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots\}$. Write
$Z_n-\widetilde Z_n=\sum_{j\ge1}16^{-j}d_{n+j}$; common additive
phases in the two histories are allowed. For any nonzero integer $h$ put

$$
L_h=\min\{L\ge0:16^L\ge56|h|\},\quad
A_h=2L_h+\frac2{15},\quad
B_h=L_h(L_h+1)+\frac{2(L_h+1)}{15}+\frac2{225}.
$$

Then, for every $N\ge1$,

$$
\boxed{
\left|\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N
\bigl(e(hZ_n)-e(h\widetilde Z_n)\bigr)\right|
\le\frac{A_h|E\cap[1,N]|+B_h}{N}.}
$$

No bound on $E\cap(N,\infty)$ is assumed.

*Proof.* Since $2\pi<7$, the character difference due to the single carry
$d_{n+j}$ is at most

$$
w_h(j)=\min(2,112|h|16^{-j}).
$$

Telescope the finite product of characters, then pass to the absolutely
convergent carry series. This gives

$$
|e(hZ_n)-e(h\widetilde Z_n)|
\le\sum_{j\ge1}w_h(j)\mathbf1_E(n+j).
$$

All terms on the right are nonnegative, so the following changes of order
are justified by increasing finite sums. For $t=n+j\le N$, a changed carry
contributes at most $\sum_{j\ge1}w_h(j)$. For $t>N$, there are at most
$\min(j,N)$ possible observation indices $n$, hence the total unobserved
future contributes at most $\sum_{j\ge1}j w_h(j)$.

For $L=L_h$, the first $L$ weights are at most $2$ and
$112|h|16^{-L}\le2$. Therefore

$$
\sum_{j\ge1}w_h(j)\le2L+2\sum_{s\ge1}16^{-s}
=2L+\frac2{15}=A_h,
$$

$$
\sum_{j\ge1}j w_h(j)
\le L(L+1)+2\sum_{s\ge1}(L+s)16^{-s}
=L(L+1)+\frac{2L}{15}+\frac{32}{225}=B_h.
$$

Sum over $n$, divide by $N$, and use the triangle inequality. This proves
the theorem with a uniform, explicitly paid future boundary. $\square$

The boundary cannot be omitted: set $E=\{N+1\}$, with the only changed
carry equal to $8$. Then $E\cap[1,N]$ is empty but the final observed
profile changes by $8/16=1/2$, whose first character changes by $2$.
Other observations cannot cancel its contribution to the real part of
$\sum_n(1-e(Z_n))$, because each has nonnegative real part. Thus the
normalized real difference is at least $2/N$.

For completeness, two bounded forced fractional trajectories with
forcing difference $\eta_t$ and carry difference $d_t$ satisfy

$$
\delta_n=-\sum_{j\ge1}16^{-j}(\eta_{n+j}-d_{n+j}).
$$

This follows by backward expansion, with terminal remainder
$16^{-J}\delta_{n+J}\to0$. Consequently SCT-1 extends to their characters
after adding

$$
\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N
\min\left(2,7|h|\sum_{j\ge1}16^{-j}|\eta_{n+j}|\right)
$$

to its right-hand side, whenever the displayed forcing sums converge.
This term cannot be erased merely because the carries agree.

## 3. Original-source conductor births and matrix energy

The original arithmetic theorem AR-5 in

$$

k=\lceil\log_2 X\rceil,\qquad
\mathcal B_N=\min\left(N,
\ell\lfloor\sqrt X\rfloor+\frac{4(2^k-1)}\ell\right).
$$

This follows by charging a conductor birth to a previously absent odd

It is a cumulative source law, not an independence assertion about prime
residues.

For any real phase history $z_1,\ldots,z_N$ define

$$
a_{N,h}(z)=\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^Ne(hz_n),\qquad
R_{N,H}(z)=\left(2\sum_{h=1}^{H-1}
\left(1-\frac hH\right)|a_{N,h}(z)|^2\right)^{1/2}.
$$

Thus $R_{N,H}^2=D_{N,H}/H$ for its Fourier Gram matrix. The definitions
apply to $Y^{\mathrm{flat}}$ whether or not a finite phase is rational.

**Theorem SCT-2 (PAPER-PROVED, original-source transport).** For every

$$
\boxed{
|R_{N,H}(Y)-R_{N,H}(Y^{\mathrm{flat}})|
\le\frac{\sqrt{H-1}}N
\left(A_{H-1}\mathcal B_N+B_{H-1}\right).}
$$

*Proof.* Apply SCT-1 with $d_t=c_t\mathbf1_{\mathcal C}(t)$ and
$E=\mathcal C$. The kernel budgets increase with $|h|$. The reverse

$\sum_{h=1}^{H-1}2(1-h/H)=H-1$. The case $H=1$ is identically zero.
$\square$
