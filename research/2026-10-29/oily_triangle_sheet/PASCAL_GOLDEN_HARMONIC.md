\boxed{p_*=\lambda^2=1-\lambda=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}},\qquad

## 2. Exact weighted-Pascal triangular probability field (PGH-01)

For every integer `m>=0` and `0<=k<=m`, declare

\[
\boxed{\pi_{m,k}=\binom mk p_*^k q_*^{m-k}=\binom mk\lambda^{m+k}.}
\]

`m` is the auxiliary Pascal row (NOT the BBP time `n`). Every row has unit mass; with zero values outside `0<=k<=m`,

\[
\sum_{k=0}^m\pi_{m,k}=(p_*+q_*)^m=1,
\qquad
\boxed{\pi_{m+1,k}=\lambda\pi_{m,k}+\lambda^2\pi_{m,k-1}.}
\]

Thus ordinary Pascal integers count all histories reaching node `(m,k)`, while golden edge probabilities form the local Markov update. The apex is `pi_(0,0)=1`; no asymmetric mass was inserted into the seed. Formally `K_m~Binomial(m,p_*)` is the path position. This is a genuinely finite, compatible, normalized triangular *model*, not a proof of a physical carrier or of pi's selected orbit.

## 3. Exact harmonic readout and its local increment (PGH-02)

Define `H_0=0`, `H_k=sum_(j=1)^k 1/j`, and `A_m=E[H_(K_m)]`. For **any** `0<p<=1` with `q=1-p`, the PGF of `K_m~Binomial(m,p)` is `(q+pt)^m`. The harmonic integral identity

\[
H_k=\int_0^1\frac{1-t^k}{1-t}\,dt
\]

proves, by finite expectation and the change of variable `z=q+pt`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_m(p)
&=\int_0^1\frac{1-(q+pt)^m}{1-t}\,dt
=\int_q^1\frac{1-z^m}{1-z}\,dz \\
&=\sum_{j=1}^m\frac{1-q^j}{j}
=H_m-\sum_{j=1}^m\frac{q^j}{j}. 
\end{aligned}
\]

Alternatively expand `(1-pu)^m` inside the same integral to get the **same** exact Pascal inverse-averaging formula

\[
\boxed{
A_m(p)=\sum_{j=1}^m\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j}\binom mj p^j.
}
\]

At the Oily-selected `p=p_*=lambda^2, q=lambda`, this says

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k=0}^m\binom mk\lambda^{m+k}H_k
=\sum_{j=1}^m\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}j\binom mj\lambda^{2j}
=H_m-\sum_{j=1}^m\frac{\lambda^j}{j}.}
\tag{PGH-02}
\]

Its **exact finite evolution**, without any asymptotic interchange, is

\[
\boxed{A_{m+1}-A_m=\frac{1-\lambda^{m+1}}{m+1}.}
\tag{PGH-03}
\]

This realizes the author's picture of a harmonic step with a geometric/golden correction, generated by local Pascal transition rules. It is strictly classical binomial mathematics.

## 4. A family of finite Oily–Macaroni certificates (PGH-04)

Write `gamma=lim_(m->infty)(H_m-log m)` and recall the absolutely convergent logarithmic identity

\[
\sum_{j=1}^\infty\frac{q^j}{j}=-\log(1-q)=-\log p,\quad 0<q<1.
\]

The *finite* identity therefore upgrades to

\[
\boxed{A_m(p)-\log(mp)=H_m-\log m+\sum_{j=m+1}^\infty\frac{q^j}{j}.}
\tag{PGH-04}
\]

The Euler finite part is obtained for **every** fixed `0<p<=1`:

\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}(A_m(p)-\log(mp))=\gamma.
\]

In the source-selected golden case, define the finite defect

\[
\Omega_m=\lambda-A_m(p_*)+\log(m p_*),\qquad m\ge1.
\]

It converges **from below** to the author's Oily–Macaroni scalar:

\[
\boxed{\Omega_m\uparrow\Omega_{\mathrm{OM}}=\lambda-\gamma.}
\tag{PGH-05}
\]

More precisely, using the standard strict trapezoidal estimate `0<H_m-log(m)-gamma<1/(2m)` and bounding the geometric tail term by a geometric series,

\[
\boxed{
0<\Omega_{\mathrm{OM}}-\Omega_m
<\frac1{2m}+\frac{\lambda^{m+1}}{(m+1)(1-\lambda)}.}
\tag{PGH-06}
\]

**Proof of the harmonic estimate, without a fitted constant:** `H_m-log m` is decreasing; its difference from its limit is the positive sum of `log(1+1/j)-1/(j+1)` for `j>=m`. Since `1/x` is strictly convex on each `[j,j+1]`, the trapezoidal bound on that integral gives each term below `(1/2)(1/j-1/(j+1))`. These differences telescope to `1/(2m)`. The golden tail is below `(lambda^(m+1)/(m+1)) sum_(k>=0)lambda^k`. This proves the displayed strict bounds.

Sample **numerical holdouts only** (not interval certificates): `m=16: Omega_m≈0.0098539321362`, `m=64: Omega_m≈0.0330261684038`, while `Omega≈0.0408183238484`. Exact algebra in `Q(lambda)` validates the finite binomial/harmonic identities separately; the finite inequality and limit have the analytic proof above.

## 5. A second Pascal route to golden: Fibonacci diagonals (PGH-07)

The shallow diagonals of Pascal's triangle satisfy the elementary exact tiling-count identity

\[
\boxed{\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\binom{m-k}{k}=F_{m+1}},
\]

so `lim_(m->infty) F_m/F_(m+1)=lambda`. Independently, the unweighted binomial transform obeys

\[
\boxed{\sum_{k=0}^m\binom mk\lambda^k=(1+\lambda)^m=\lambda^{-m}.}
\]

The same Pascal coefficient array therefore yields Fibonacci/golden growth along one class of diagonals and the exact projective inversion `lambda^m -> lambda^(-m)` under its row/binomial transform. These are classical facts, not a proof of an intrinsic canonical Oily-to-Fibonacci source equivalence.
