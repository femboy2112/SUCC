# Primary-source literature audit, 2026-10-30

This is a targeted audit, not a priority certification or exhaustive bibliography.
Searches included the original BBP paper; Bailey--Crandall Hypothesis A and
Stoneham normality; BBP p-adic congruences; Cartier/Frobenius, Lucas and Dwork
congruences; Gauss periods and multiplicative subgroup exponential sums.
Publication years below come from the papers, not search-engine crawl dates.

1. **Bailey, P. Borwein, Plouffe (1997), _On the rapid computation of various
   polylogarithmic constants_, Math. Comp. 66, 903--913.**
   [Author PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/digits.pdf).
   Theorem 1 proves the original four-pole identity. Its following zero relation
   has eight-slot vector `(-8,8,4,8,2,2,-1,0)`. This matters here: the reflected
   shadow `(0,8,4,4,0,0,-1,0)` equals twice the original vector plus that null
   vector, so its infinite radix sum is **2 pi**, not an unidentified constant.
   This relation is classical and explicitly credited. The paper supplies digit
   extraction, not normality. Our prime-dependent clocks and finite p-adic
   formulas are locally derived; this limited search does not establish priority.

2. **Bailey--Crandall (2001), _On the random character of fundamental constant
   expansions_.** [Author PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/baicran.pdf),
   [journal record](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2001.10504441).
   Hypothesis A is a hypothesis about perturbed expanding orbits. Restricting it
   to the authentic rational forcing would suffice after excluding its finite
   attractor alternative; it does not supply an independently proved estimate.
   The exact source lies within its rational-function setup, but fitting that
   setup is not a proof of the hypothesis.

3. **Bailey--Crandall (2002), _Random generators and normal numbers_, Exp.
   Math. 11(4), 527--546.**
   [Author PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/bcnormal-em.pdf),
   [journal](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2002.10504704).
   Hypothesis 3.1 restates Hypothesis A. Theorem 4.8 and Corollary 4.9 prove
   normality of the coprime Stoneham family; Lemma 4.4 controls prime-power
   periods and Theorem 4.6 controls incomplete sums. These hypotheses are
   not supplied by the exceptional BBP primes 3 and 5. The rational-translation
   preservation theorem also corrects the older Main XVI requirement that
   every periodic rational seed should fail a normality target.

4. **Lagarias (2001), _On the normality of arithmetical constants_, Exp. Math.
   10(3), 355--368.** [Author submission](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0101055).
   Relates individual perturbed and radix orbits and discusses G-functions and
   Furstenberg connections. This is context for the Green-tail bridge and
   selected-orbit distinction, not an automatic entropy theorem for pi.

5. **Mellit--Vlasenko (2013), _Dwork's congruences for the constant terms of
   powers of a Laurent polynomial_.**
   [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.5811).
   Its constant-term and Newton-polytope hypotheses must actually be realized
   before applying the congruences. We did not construct such a realization
   of the normalized BBP prefixes. The exact denominator-layer identity in
   this campaign is proved directly and does not assume a Dwork theorem.

6. **Rowland--Yassawi, _Automatic congruences for diagonals of rational
   functions_.** [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.8635),
   [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.5802/jtnb.901).
   Gives finite automata modulo prime powers for appropriate integral
   diagonal/algebraic sequences. BBP prefixes have arbitrarily negative
   p-adic valuations at the first power births proved here, so they cannot
   be inserted unchanged into the integral-sequence theorem. A new
   normalization and proof of its hypotheses remain necessary.

7. **Beukers--Vlasenko, _Dwork crystals I_.**
   [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.11155).
   Constructs Cartier operations and congruences under specified rational-form
   and Hasse--Witt hypotheses. No source-correct transfer from those objects to
   the BBP complementary real character has been supplied here. Similar
   Frobenius vocabulary does not provide such a transfer.

8. **Di Benedetto, Garaev, Garcia, Gonzalez-Sanchez, Shparlinski and Trujillo,
   _New estimates for exponential sums over multiplicative subgroups
   and intervals in prime fields_.**
   [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.06165).
   Provides subgroup estimates in a stated size regime. This campaign uses an
   elementary Gauss-sum proof where sufficient; stronger subgroup bounds still
   leave the actual complementary weight uncontrolled. The signed CRT dossier
   gives a certified authentic-source witness against a proposed marginal
   transfer. No unweighted estimate is cited as a bound for that weighted sum.

The Stoneham/geometry dossier supplies its own PNT-in-progressions reference
for the asymptotic grid obstruction; the finite divisibility theorem is
elementary and independent of that asymptotic input. The inherited corrected
Kempner counterexample is retained with its published provenance. No new
claim is made about Furstenberg's conjecture, pi normality, or RH.

## Access record

The original Plouffe-hosted BBP PDF and a journal-hosted Dwork page returned
access errors. The original authors' BBP PDF and arXiv Dwork papers were then
read successfully. Search results without primary text were not used as
proof authority. The audit found directly relevant classical methods and the
reflected-source null identity; it did not establish that the present clock
formulas are absent from the literature.
