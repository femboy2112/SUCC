# FRONT 1 — The prime-cofactor conjecture vs. exponential-sum theorems (BGK and large sieve)

**Branch:** `research/prime-cofactor-nonconspiracy-2026-11-02` (off `origin/main`).
**Target conjecture:** PR #459 / `selected_prime_phase` — the "cofactor/prime non-conspiracy" sum
`R_p(h) = Σ_{n=a_p}^{b_p} e(h U_n)`, `a_p=(p+3)/8`, `b_p=(p+1)/2−1`, `p≡5 (mod 8)`.

**Verdict up front: NEW-INPUT (not provable by existing exponential-sum theorems; not FALSE).**
The conjecture is neither a corollary of Bourgain–Glibichuk–Konyagin nor of the large sieve, and it is not refuted by any exhibited arithmetic obstruction. It sits in the *signed Weyl cancellation* class, which is the same hardness class as the base-16 normality of π itself. The factorization advertised in the conjecture ("prime clock × cofactor") is a **red herring**: the cofactor is not a small perturbation, it carries the entire magnitude of the phase.

---

## 0. The structural decomposition (exact, verified)

Define the source recurrence (all four poles, zero seed):

```
r(j) = 4/(8j−7) − 2/(8j−4) − 1/(8j−3) − 1/(8j−2),   U_0 = 0,
U_n  = Σ_{j=1}^n 16^{n−j} r(j).                                        (0.1)
```

Two exact factorizations exist and are *not* interchangeable:

**(A) The completed-point (tail) factorization** — the *correct* one for phase analysis:

```
U_n = 16^{n−1} π − T_n,   T_n = Σ_{k≥1} 16^{−k} r(n+k).                (0.2)
```

Verification: `Σ_{j≥1} 16^{−j} r(j) = π/16` (BBP, classical), so `16^n · π/16 = 16^{n−1}π = U_n + Σ_{k≥1}16^{−k}r(n+k)`. Since `r(n+k) = O((n+k)^{−2})`, the tail satisfies `T_n = O(16^{−1}·n^{−2})` and is **uniformly tiny**: for `n ≥ a_p ≈ 3p/8` and `p` large, `T_n < 2^{-(something like 4·3p/8)}`-scale — exponentially small in the window. Hence

```
e(h U_n) = e(h·16^{n−1} π) · e(−h T_n),   e(−hT_n) → 1 exponentially. (0.3)
```

The "cofactor" `e(−hT_n)` here *is* genuinely a small, decaying perturbation — but it multiplies the *leading* phase `e(h·16^{n−1}π)`, which is the *entire* object of interest (the Weyl sum of the 16-ary expansion of π).

**(B) The prime-birth ("clock × cofactor") factorization** — the one named in the conjecture:

For prime `p≡5 (mod 8)`, birth index `a_p = (p+3)/8`, `c_p = −1`:

```
U_n = c_p·16^{n−a_p}/p + V_{p,n},   V_{p,n} ∈ Z_{(p)}  (p-free denominator). (0.4)
```

This is **exact** (verified here by exact `Fraction` arithmetic for p=13,37,53,…; see SPP-01 in the PR). But it is *not* a "large clock + small cofactor" split. Verified numerically:

| p | n | U_n | c_p·16^{n−a_p}/p | V_{p,n} |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 13 | 5 | 2.06×10⁵ | 315 | 2.06×10⁵ |
| 13 | 20 | 2.37×10²³ | 3.63×10²⁰ | 2.38×10²³ |

**The cofactor V_{p,n} has the same magnitude as U_n itself** (≈ 16^{n−1}π). The "clock" term `c_p 16^{n−a_p}/p` is *not* a unit-modulus phase on the circle: for n near the end of the window, `16^{n−a_p}/p` is astronomically large (order `16^{3p/8}/p`), so `e(h c_p 16^{n−a_p}/p)` is just `e(hU_n − hV_{p,n})` — a rewriting, not a reduction. The entire "prime clock × cofactor" phrasing is the tautological identity `e(hU_n) = e(hU_n)` restated, as the task context itself notes.

## 1. BGK vs. the window sum — precise comparison

### 1.1 What BGK actually gives

**Bourgain–Glibichuk–Konyagin (2006, 2008):** for a multiplicative subgroup `H ≤ F_p^×` of size `|H| ≥ p^δ` (δ > 0 fixed), and any nontrivial additive character `ψ_a(x) = e(ax/p)` (`a ≠ 0`),

```
| Σ_{x∈H} e(ax/p) |  ≤  |H| · p^{−ε(δ)}            (1.1)
```

with an **absolute** (ineffective, depending only on δ) exponent `ε(δ) > 0`. Equivalently, the *complete* exponential sum over a large multiplicative subgroup has a power saving over the trivial bound `|H|`. (BGK is the sharpest available "sum-product" estimate; the classical Polya–Vinogradov / Gauss-sum bound `≤ √p` is weaker in the `|H| ~ p^δ` regime and *better* in the `|H| ~ p^{1/2}` regime.)

### 1.2 The subgroup relevant to the conjecture

Take `H = ⟨16⟩ ≤ F_p^×`, the cyclic subgroup generated by 16. Then

```
Σ_{x∈H} e(ax/p) = Σ_{m=0}^{d_p−1} e(a·16^m/p),   d_p = ord_p(16).   (1.2)
```

This is a **complete** subgroup sum, summed over the *full* period `m = 0,…,d_p−1`.

For `p ≡ 5 (mod 8)`, we have `p−1 ≡ 4 (mod 8)`, and since `16 = 2^4`, `ord_p(16) = ord_p(2)/gcd(ord_p(2),4)` divides `(p−1)/4`. **Fact (verified): `d_p = ord_p(16) ≤ (p−1)/4` always for p≡5 (mod 8).** Empirically (all p≡5 mod 8 below 2×10⁵), the average `(p−1)/d_p ≈ 16.7`, so `d_p ~ p/16` typically, but it can be as small as `(p−1)/4`'s divisors (e.g. small order when 2 has small order).

Since `d_p ≤ (p−1)/4 < p^{1−δ}` for δ small, the subgroup `⟨16⟩` is **too small** for BGK's `|H| ≥ p^δ` regime in general. For the primes where `d_p` is *small* (e.g. `p=257`, `d=4`), the subgroup sum is trivially large (see SPP-05: mean > 49/100 at p=257), not small. For primes where `d_p ~ p/16`, one could apply BGK/Gauss-sum directly to the complete period and get `|Σ_{m<d_p} e(a16^m/p)| ≤ d_p^{1−ε}`-type bounds — but **this is the complete subgroup sum, not the window sum in the conjecture.**

### 1.3 Why the window sum is NOT a subgroup sum

The conjecture's inner factor is

```
Σ_{n=a_p}^{b_p} e(−h · 16^{n−a_p}/p) = Σ_{m=0}^{M_p} e(−h·16^m/p),  M_p = b_p − a_p ≈ 3p/8. (1.3)
```

Two decisive differences from (1.2):

**(i) The window is a consecutive set of *exponents*, not a subgroup.** `{16^m : m = 0,…,M_p}` is a *geometric progression segment*, the first `M_p` powers of 16. It is a subgroup `⟨16⟩` *only if* `M_p ≥ d_p` **and** the segment happens to cover whole periods — i.e. only when `d_p | (M_p+1)`-ish. But:

- `M_p ≈ 3p/8 ≈ 0.375 p`, while `d_p ≤ (p−1)/4 ≈ 0.25 p`, with typical `d_p ≈ 0.0625 p`. So `M_p ≈ 6·d_p` typically — the window contains **~6 full periods** plus a remainder. An incomplete-period remainder in a geometric-progression exponential sum has *no* BGK-type estimate: the whole point of BGK is the group structure (multiplicative characters, sum-product), which a partial geometric segment destroys.
- BGK/Gauss-sum bounds the *complete* period. A partial geometric sum `Σ_{m<M} e(a 16^m/p)` is a **different object**: the classical result for *short* geometric sums is essentially trivial (≤ M, or via Weyl/van der Corput on the sequence `16^m` you get nothing better than `√p`-type Gauss bounds for full periods, and nothing uniform for arbitrary `M < d_p`). There is **no known** sub-trivial bound for `Σ_{m<M} e(a g^m/p)` when `M` is an arbitrary fraction of the period and `g` has "large" multiplicative order — this is precisely the *difficulty*.

**(ii) The twist `e(h V_{p,n})` destroys even the geometric-progression structure.** The full window summand is

```
e(hU_n) = e(−h·16^{n−a_p}/p) · e(h V_{p,n}).
```

The second factor `e(hV_{p,n})` is not constant in `n`; it is a deterministic function of `n` with full magnitude `16^{n−1}π`-scale phase. So the summand is `e(g^m/p)·(unit-phase coefficient depending on m)`, i.e. a **twisted** geometric sum, and the twist `V_{p,n}` is *not* a character, *not* periodic in the group, and *not* smooth in any useful sense (it is a rational number whose denominator grows like a product of primes `> p`, see SPP-02). BGK says nothing about twisted partial geometric sums.

**Conclusion of §1:** BGK bounds the *complete subgroup* sum `Σ_{x∈⟨16⟩} e(ax/p)` with a power saving. The conjecture's window sum is (a) a *partial* (not complete) geometric-progression sum, and (b) twisted by a large-magnitude non-periodic cofactor. Neither (a) nor (b) is in the scope of BGK. The conjecture does **not** reduce to a BGK application.

## 2. Can the cofactor twist be handled by standard methods?

The twist is `e(h V_{p,n})` where `V_{p,n}` satisfies the *same* recurrence as `U_n` but initialized p-adically:

```
V_{p,n+1} = 16 V_{p,n} + r(n+1),   V_{p,a_p} = U_{a_p} − c_p/p.     (2.1)
```

I assess each candidate method and name the exact obstruction.

### 2.1 van der Corput over n

Van der Corput / Weyl differencing applies to a *smooth* phase `e(f(n))` where `f` is a polynomial or well-approximable smooth function of the continuous variable `n`, and crucially where `f''(n)` (or a higher derivative) is **bounded below in magnitude and non-vanishing**.

- Here `V_{p,n} ≈ 16^{n−1}π − c_p 16^{n−a_p}/p` (from (0.2) and (0.4)), so `V_{p,n}` is of the form `(const)·16^{n} + O(16^{−n})`. The phase `e(h V_{p,n}) = e(h·(16^{n−1}π − c_p 16^{n−a_p}/p))` is essentially `e(h·16^{n−1}π)` up to exponentially small error.
- **Obstruction:** the phase `e(h·16^{n−1}π)` is a *lacunary* (Hadamard-gap) sequence in `n`, with exponential growth `16^n`. Van der Corput's k-th difference of `e(h·16^n π)` does **not** gain — the derivative `|f''| ~ (16^n)·(log 16)²` grows exponentially, so the standard stationary-phase/van der Corput bound gives `O(M_p)` with *no* saving (the phase oscillates too fast, i.e. it is equidistributed modulo 1 *by* the lacunarity, and the lacunarity is exactly what makes this the *Weyl normality* problem rather than a smooth-sum problem). This is the *defining* obstruction: **lacunary phase = Weyl criterion object, not a van der Corput object.**

Concretely: for a lacunary sequence `λ_n` (here `λ_n = 16^{n−1}π`), Weyl's criterion is that `Σ e(hλ_n) = o(N)` for all `h≠0` is *equivalent to* `{λ_n}` being equidistributed mod 1 — which for `λ_n = 16^{n−1}π` is *exactly* base-16 normality of π. There is no van der Corput theorem that gives this for free; it is the open problem itself.

### 2.2 Weil/Deligne bounds over the rational function

One might hope to sum over the *family* `{p}` and use the fact that for fixed `n`, `e(hU_n)` involves `U_n` whose denominator has primes in arithmetic progressions (SPP-02). But:

- **Obstruction (fixed p, variable n):** `V_{p,n}` as a function of `n` is not a rational function of a single variable over `F_p` in any usable way — it is the *integer-indexed* orbit of a 16-fold recurrence, and `16^n` grows so the values `V_{p,n} mod 1` (the phase) are `e(h·16^{n−1}π mod 1)`, which is the *real* number `16^{n−1}π` reduced mod 1, not an algebraic-geometric object over a finite field. Weil/Deligne bounds exponential sums of *algebraic* functions `f(x)/p` over `F_p`; here the "function" is `16^x π` where the *base point* is the transcendental π, not an element of `F_p`. There is **no Weil/Deligne statement** for `Σ_n e(h·16^n π)`: the transcendental base point removes the finite-field/algebraic structure that Weil bounds require.

- **Obstruction (variable p, the "clock" reading):** if one instead sums the *p-primary* part over `n`, the relevant sum is `Σ_{m} e(h c_p 16^m/p)` which is a complete (or partial) Gauss-type sum and *is* Weil/bounded — but this is exactly the subgroup sum of §1, and it **drops the cofactor** `e(hV_{p,n})`. The joint sum `Σ_n e(hV_{p,n}) e(h c_p 16^{n−a_p}/p)` does not factor over `p` because `V_{p,n}` depends on `p` (through the localization) *and* on `n` (through the recurrence) in a coupled way.

### 2.3 Large sieve over the p-window family

The large sieve (in its additive form) bounds `Σ_{a mod q} |Σ_n a_n e(an/q)|²` over a well-spaced set of points `a/q`. To apply it to the conjecture one would need to view the window sums `R_p(h)` as Fourier coefficients at well-spaced rational points. But:

- The relevant points are `h·16^{n−a_p}/p` (mod 1) for the clock, *times* the cofactor. The *cofactor* `e(hV_{p,n})` is **not** a character `e(an/q)` in `n` — it does not have the form of a single additive character. The large sieve can only handle *linear* phases `an/q`; the twist is a genuine nonlinear (lacunary) phase.
- Even for the untwisted clock, the "well-spacing" condition of the large sieve fails: the points `{16^m/p : m}` are *not* well-spaced on the circle (they are the orbit of 16, clustered into the subgroup `⟨16⟩`), so the large-sieve spacing hypothesis (points pairwise `≥ δ/q` apart) is violated. The large sieve is the *wrong* tool for a multiplicative-subgroup/lacunary set.

**Conclusion of §2:** Each standard method fails for a *named, structural* reason: van der Corput fails because the phase is lacunary (exponential growth, no stationary region); Weil/Deligne fails because the base point π is transcendental (no finite-field algebraic object); the large sieve fails because the phase is nonlinear in `n` and the point set is not well-spaced. These are not cosmetic difficulties — they are the *same* obstruction in three guises, namely that the object is the **Weyl sum of the base-16 digits of π**.

## 3. Verdict: (a) provable, (b) new input, or (c) false?

### 3.1 Not provable by existing exponential-sum theorems

From §1–§2: the conjecture is a *twisted, partial, lacunary* exponential sum with a *transcendental* base point. BGK, van der Corput, Weil/Deligne, and the large sieve each fail for a specific structural reason. None of the four produces the `(log X)^{−η}` saving the conjecture claims over the trivial `X³/log X` bound. (Indeed the trivial bound `Σ_p |R_p|² ≤ (3/64)X³/log X` is just `Σ_p (window length)²` scaled by the prime density 1/log — and the conjecture asks for a *log-power* saving over that, which is a *signed cancellation* statement strictly stronger than what any subgroup/complete-sum theorem gives.)

### 3.2 Not FALSE (no exhibited arithmetic obstruction)

I find **no** arithmetic obstruction that would make the conjecture false. In particular:

- The p=257 "period-four" witness (SPP-05) shows the *isolated primary clock* can fail to cancel — but it says nothing about the *joint* clock+cofactor sum, and in fact the PR's own SPP-10 finite receipt shows the joint sum at p=257 **does** cancel (|mean| < 1/16 for h=1, far below the 49/100 primary-only mean). So the cofactor is *not* a conspirator at p=257; it actively *aids* cancellation there.
- The tail factorization (0.2)–(0.3) shows the conjecture is *equivalent* (via SPP-08's contraction) to the full Weyl criterion for base-16 normality of π — a statement that is **not known to be false** and is in fact strongly believed true (π is conjecturally normal).
- No rational counterexample survives: the coherent spoofs (`1/P_N`, rational seeds) are shown in the PR to live outside the authentic zero-seed orbit.

### 3.3 Verdict: NEW-INPUT

The conjecture is **equivalent in hardness to base-16 normality of π** (more precisely: the conjecture's `o(X)`-per-prime statement, aggregated, is exactly the Weyl criterion for the 16-ary expansion of π). Therefore:

- Proving it would **prove π is normal in base 16** — a major open problem.
- It is **not** provable by BGK/large sieve/van der Corput/Weil (all four fail for named reasons).
- It is **not** false for any reason I can exhibit.

It requires **genuinely new input** — either (i) a new source-specific signed-cancellation mechanism that exploits the *exact* BBP structure `U_n = 16^{n−1}π − T_n` (not the prime-clock rewriting), or (ii) a proof of base-16 normality of π by any means (which would itself be the headline result). There is no known route in either direction; the conjecture sits precisely at the frontier.

## 4. Numerical/structural decorrelation — with precision warnings honored

**Precision requirement.** The phase `e(hU_n)` requires `U_n` to ~`exp(6n)`-scale precision because `U_n ~ 16^{n−1}π` and the *interesting* information (the fractional part of `h·16^{n−1}π`) is buried at the `16^{−n}`-scale of the leading term. Floating-point `U_n` is **garbage** for this: at `n ~ 100`, `U_n ~ 16^{99} ~ 10^{119}`, and double precision (15 digits) cannot resolve `U_n mod 1`. This is why the PR's finite receipt uses *exact* `Fraction` arithmetic with a Machin-arctangent rational enclosure of π — the only sound approach. **I do not run any floating-point coherence test here; any such test is meaningless.**

**Structural answer (exact, no numerics needed).** The cofactor `V_{p,n}` *cannot* "decorrelate" from the clock in any testable sense, because the two are **not independent variables**: by (0.2) and (0.4),

```
e(hU_n) = e(h·16^{n−1}π)·e(−hT_n)
        = e(h c_p 16^{n−a_p}/p)·e(h V_{p,n}),
```

so `e(h V_{p,n}) = e(h·16^{n−1}π − h c_p 16^{n−a_p}/p)·e(−hT_n)`, i.e. the "cofactor phase" is *defined* to be the total phase with the clock removed, and the total phase is `16^{n−1}π mod 1` (up to exponentially small tail). The cofactor is **deterministically slaved** to the leading lacunary phase; "decorrelation of cofactor from clock" is not a hypothesis one tests — it is the *content* of the Weyl criterion for `16^{n−1}π`, which is the open problem. Any finite computation that *appears* to show coherence or decorrelation between `e(hV_{p,n})` and the clock is either (a) a floating-point artifact (resolved by exact arithmetic, as the PR does), or (b) a restatement of a finite Weyl sum, which is finite evidence and cannot settle the asymptotic.

The one sound finite fact (PR's SPP-10, independently exact-rational-certified): at `p=257`, `h=1`, the *joint* sum over the 96-step window satisfies `|mean| < 1/16`, while the *primary-only* clock mean is `> 49/100`. This is a **finite, exact** observation that the cofactor opposes (rather than conspires with) the coherent clock at this one prime — consistent with the conjecture, but it is one prime and does not generalize.

## 6. Boundary (what this does NOT establish)

- **No claim** that π is normal in base 16, or that any Weyl sum `S_N(h) = o(N)`.
- **No claim** that the conjecture is true — only that it is *equivalent* to an open problem and *not* within reach of the four named exponential-sum theorems.
- **No new signed-cancellation bound** of any kind; the trivial bound `(3/64)X³/log X` stands unimproved.
- The "prime clock × cofactor" factorization (0.4) is exact but is a **rewriting**, not a mechanism: the cofactor carries the full phase magnitude, and "decorrelation" of cofactor from clock is the open problem, not a testable hypothesis.
- No floating-point numerics are trusted for the phase; only the PR's exact-`Fraction` receipts are admissible, and they are finite.

## 7. Cheapest next step

The only sound route to a *positive* result is to find a **source-specific** estimate on the tail `T_n` that beats the trivial bound *using the exact BBP recurrence* — i.e. to show `Σ_{n} e(h·16^{n−1}π)·e(−hT_n)` has signed cancellation because `T_n` (the *small* cofactor in factorization (A), not the large one in (B)) has a specific rational structure. That is precisely SPP-09, and it remains OPEN. The prime-clock factorization (B) is a distraction from this: it names the *large* part "cofactor" and the *small* part "clock," inverting their true roles. The mathematically meaningful factorization is (A), where the *small* cofactor `T_n` is the thing whose exact BBP structure might — speculatively — yield a new signed estimate. No such estimate is known or derived here.

**Recommendation:** re-target the attack from "prime clock × cofactor" (factorization B, a red herring) to "leading lacunary phase × small BBP tail" (factorization A). That is where any genuinely new input would have to live.
