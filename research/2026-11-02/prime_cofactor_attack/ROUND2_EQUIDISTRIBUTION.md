## 1. Precise statement of the hypothesis (and its correction)

**Notation.** For prime `p>5`, `p≡5 (mod 8)`: birth `a_p=(p+3)/8`, window end `b_p=(p+1)/2`
(exclusive), window length `L_p=b_p−a_p=(3p+1)/8`. Four-pole forcing
`r(n)=4/(8n−7)−2/(8n−4)−1/(8n−3)−1/(8n−2)`, orbit `U_0=0`, `U_n=16U_{n−1}+r(n)`.
Inside the window, `U_n = c_p·16^{n−a_p}/p + V_{p,n}` with `c_p=−1` (p≡5 mod 8) and
`V_{p,n}` p-free in the denominator. Define

```
clock index   c_n = 16^{n−a_p} mod p   ∈ Z/p,
cofactor phase θ_n = h V_{p,n} mod 1    ∈ T = R/Z,   h ∈ Z\{0}.
```

**The literal hypothesis (as posed in the handoff).**

> **(H0)** As `n` runs over `[a_p, b_p)`, the pair `(c_n, θ_n)` is equidistributed on
> `(Z/p) × T`, i.e. for every `(a, ψ) ∈ Z/p × T`-test function the empirical measure converges,
> and in particular the clock `c_n` is uniform on `Z/p` **and** `c_n ⊥ θ_n`.

**H0 is FALSE (PROVED, elementary, exact).** The clock index satisfies `c_n = 16^{n−a_p} mod p`,
so it ranges over the *multiplicative coset* `{16^k mod p : k≥0} = ⟨16⟩`, the cyclic subgroup of
`(Z/p)^×` generated by 16. Its size is `ord_p(16)`, and for `p≡5 (mod 8)`:

```
ord_p(16) = ord_p(2)/gcd(ord_p(2),4)  ≤  (p−1)/4.          (CLK-1)
```

Since `16=2^4` and `(p−1)/4` is **odd** for `p≡5 (mod 8)`, we actually have
`ord_p(16)=(p−1)/4` exactly *whenever* `ord_p(2)=p−1` (2 is a primitive root), and a proper
divisor of `(p−1)/4` otherwise. In every case `ord_p(16) ≤ (p−1)/4 < p`, so **the clock visits at
most a quarter of `Z/p`** (it never hits the non-quadratic-type residues outside `⟨16⟩`). It is
therefore *not* equidistributed on `Z/p`; its marginal distribution is the uniform measure on the
subgroup `⟨16⟩` (with multiplicity given by how many times the window `[a_p,b_p)` wraps the
period `ord_p(16)`, plus a remainder). **H0 is refuted by a margin that is structural, not
asymptotic: no `n`-limit can repair a coordinate whose support is a strict subgroup.**

**The corrected, non-vacuous hypothesis (what the question actually means).**

> **(H1 — conditional independence / non-conspiracy.)** *Conditioned on the clock residue
> class `c_n mod p` (equivalently on `c_n` itself, since the clock is a deterministic function of
> `n`), the cofactor phase `θ_n = h V_{p,n} mod 1` is asymptotically uniform on `T`, uniformly in
> the residue class:* for every fixed `h≠0` and every fixed `a ∈ ⟨16⟩`-coset,
>
> ```
> 1/L_p  ∑_{a_p≤n<b_p, c_n≡a (mod p)}  e(k·h V_{p,n})  =  o(1)   for each fixed k∈Z\{0}.  (H1)
> ```

Equivalently, the joint empirical measure on `⟨16⟩ × T` factors as (uniform on `⟨16⟩`)⊗(Haar on
`T`) in the limit. This is the precise quantitative statement; the clock's own non-uniformity is
*removed* by conditioning, so H1 isolates exactly the question "does the cofactor conspire with

**Relation to the prior round (PROVED, restatement).** H1 is *not* weaker or stronger than the

## 2. Does p-integrality give any rigorous handle? (Answer: NO, with the exact reason.)

**Claim (R2-01).** The p-integrality of `V_{p,n}` (denominator coprime to `p` throughout the
window) is a **p-adic valuation fact** and is **orthogonal** to the real-phase distribution of
`V_{p,n} mod 1`. It provides **no** rigorous handle toward H1 (or H0).

**Why, precisely.** There are two distinct structures and the question conflates them:

1. **The p-adic structure.** `V_{p,n} ∈ Z_(p)` means `v_p(V_{p,n}) ≥ 0`, i.e. the denominator is
   not divisible by `p`. This is a statement about the *p-adic valuation*, a local (p-adic)
   property. It is PROVED in the prior round by a congruence lemma (FRONT_2_COFACTOR §1.5): for
   `(p+3)/8 < j < (p+1)/2`, none of `8j−7,8j−4,8j−3,8j−2` is divisible by `p`, so the forcing
   `r(j)` (and hence `W_{p,n}`, `V_{p,n}`) is p-free in the window.

2. **The real/archimedean structure.** The quantity `θ_n = h V_{p,n} mod 1` lives in `R/Z`. Its
   (non-)uniformity on the circle is governed by the *archimedean* size and arithmetic of
   `V_{p,n}`, specifically by the real-valued sequence `{h V_{p,n}}` (fractional parts). The p-adic
   valuation `v_p(V_{p,n})` is *insensitive* to the fractional part: two numbers `x` and `x + p^m`
   (any `m≥0`) have the same `v_p` but their fractional parts `{x}`, `{x+p^m}` are unrelated.
   Conversely `x` and `x+q` for a large prime `q≠p` share `v_p=0` but have wildly different
   fractional parts.

**The exact reason p-integrality gives nothing (PROVED by counterexample / carrier-invariance).**
The prior round already established (FRONT_2_COFACTOR §3.2, SCB-06/SCB-07/E7) that the quadratic
numerator, the tower identity, the p-integrality lemma, and the no-periodicity theorem are
**all shared by every carrier with the same forcing** — including the changed-seed carriers of
SCB-07/E7 for which the signed-cancellation bound is *false*. Concretely: p-integrality is a
property of the *forcing* `r` and the *localization* at `a_p`; it does not distinguish the
authentic zero seed from any other seed. Since a property shared by carriers with *opposite*
phase behavior cannot imply anything about the phase distribution, **p-integrality cannot imply
H1.** It is a necessary coherence condition (the p-adic pole is correctly isolated in the clock
term), not a sufficient phase-control condition.

**The one thing integrality *does* give (negative, PROVED in ROUND2_CORE.md).** Via the `f_m`
decomposition, the pole `−1/p` is carried *exclusively* by the `f_4` edge at `j=a_p`, so the
clock and the cofactor arise from **disjoint forcing edges**. This rules out a *specific* mechanism
(the pole's own edge re-cohering with itself), but it does **not** slow or shrink the cofactor: the
cofactor is still a full-magnitude 16-geometric sum `V_{p,n} ≈ 16^{n−1}π`, whose phase oscillates
as fast as the leading lacunary term. Partial summation is blocked (total variation `O(|h|16^n)`
over a window of length `~3p/8`). **Status: the decoupling is PROVED and useful only negatively.**

**Verdict (task 2):** p-integrality lives on the p-adic grid; the question lives on the real
circle. They are orthogonal, and the fact that `V_{p,n} mod 1` has p-free denominator does **not**
imply anything about its joint distribution with `16^{n−a_p} mod p`. This is not a conjecture to
be tested — it is a *category error* to be named, and it is named.

## 3. Finite joint test over ~50 primes (OBSERVED FINITE, two methods)

**Precision discipline (inherited, correct).** The cofactor phase has denominator `exp(6n+o(n))`.

**Reading the table (three clean separations).**

1. **`|mean(clock)|` is large (0.13→0.06) and slowly decaying** — the clock is *not* uniform on

   marginal.

2. **`|mean(cofactor)|` is small (~0.007) and roughly `1/√L_p`** — the cofactor phase is

3. **`Δ=|joint−pred|` is large but entirely explained by the clock.** Since `mean(clock)` is large
   and `mean(cofactor)` is tiny, `pred(joint)` is tiny; but `mean(joint)` is *not* tiny because
   `mean(joint)` is dominated by the clock's own non-uniformity (`e(c_n/p)` averaged against the
   cofactor's slowly-varying `conj(e(hV_{p,n}))` inherits the clock's large mean). The correct

   - **χ²/dof ≈ 0.97, 1.02, 1.04 ≈ 1** — the cofactor phase, *conditioned on the clock residue

## 4. Firewall: the coherent counterseed `s*=−π/16`, and whether H1 can be source-derived

**The counterseed and why it breaks cofactor independence (PROVED).**

The orbit is not determined by the forcing alone; it depends on the seed. The **coherent
counterseed** is the seed whose orbit is `U_n = −T_n`, where `T_n = Σ_{k≥1} 16^{−k} r(n+k) > 0`
(the positive BBP tail). This arises from the completed-point identity `U_n = 16^{n−1}π − T_n` by
*removing the leading lacunary term* (equivalently the seed `s*=−π/16`, since the general
solution of `U_{n+1}=16U_n+r(n+1)` is `U_n = 16^{n}·U_0 + (particular)`, and choosing
`U_0 = −π/16` cancels the `16^{n−1}π` term). For this orbit:

```
e(h U_n) = e(−h T_n) → e(0) = 1   exponentially,  because T_n < (8n+1)^{−2} → 0.
```

Every phase collapses to 1; the Weyl sum `Σ_n e(hU_n) = L_p·(1+o(1))` saturates the trivial bound
`M/T→1` (this is the `coherent0` control in the prior round, observed at exactly 1.000000). Here
"cofactor independence" is **trivially FALSE**: both the clock and the cofactor are slaved to the
same constant phase 1, so they are perfectly *dependent* (both point at 1).

**Why the authentic zero seed differs (PROVED).**

The authentic orbit (`U_0=0`) is `U_n = 16^{n−1}π − T_n`, i.e. the *leading lacunary term*
`16^{n−1}π` is **present**. Then:

```
V_{p,n} = U_n + 16^{n−a_p}/p = 16^{n−1}π − T_n + 16^{n−a_p}/p.
```

The cofactor `V_{p,n}` is **not** small and **not** constant: it carries the full lacunary
magnitude `16^{n−1}π`, whose fractional part `{16^{n−1}π}` is *exactly the sequence of base-16
digits of π*. Therefore the cofactor phase

```
e(h V_{p,n}) = e(h·16^{n−1}π) · e(−h T_n) · e(h·16^{n−a_p}/p)
             = e(h·16^{n−1}π) · (1 + o(1)) · (a p-periodic factor)
```

is *slaved* to the base-16 digit sequence of π, up to the exponentially-tiny tail `T_n` and the
`p`-periodic clock factor. **The cofactor is not an independent noise source; it is the π-digit
phase itself in disguise.** Its equidistribution on the circle *is* the statement that the base-16
digits of π are equidistributed — which is base-16 normality of π, the open problem.

**Can any cofactor-independence statement be source-derived (vs assumed)? Answer: NO, not
currently, and the firewall makes this precise.**

- **PROVED (structure):** `e(hV_{p,n})` is *deterministically* slaved to `e(h·16^{n−1}π)` (up to
  `o(1)` tail and a `p`-periodic clock factor). "Cofactor independent of clock" is therefore not
  an assumption one can freely make — it is **equivalent to** equidistribution of
  `{16^{n−1}π}`, i.e. the Weyl criterion for the base-16 digits of π. The prior round's
  FRONT_1 §5 states this exactly; it is PROVED as a *reduction*, not a theorem.

- **PROVED (no cheap route):** the p-integrality, quadratic numerator, tower identity, and
  no-periodicity are all shared by every same-forcing carrier (SCB-06/07/E7), so none can produce
  a π-specific bound; the exponential conductor `exp(6n)` forbids the Stoneham-style polynomial
  period; BGK/van der Corput/Weil/large-sieve each fail for a named structural reason (partial
  geometric sum, lacunary phase, transcendental base, non-well-spaced points).

- **The firewall in one line:** the counterseed `s*=−π/16` kills the leading term and *forces*
  cofactor-coherence (all phases →1, `M/T→1`); the authentic seed keeps the leading term, so its
  cofactor is the π-digit phase. Any "cofactor independence" for the authentic seed is therefore
  **exactly as strong as base-16 normality of π**, and cannot be *source-derived* by any fact that
  is also true for the counterseed (which exhibits the opposite behavior). No such π-specific

**Bottom line (task 4):** the counterseed is a *countermodel to the mechanism*, not to the
conjecture: it shows that "cofactor independence" is a **property of the seed's leading term**,
