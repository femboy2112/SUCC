The first failed derivative inference is bounding an unwrapped monotone phase
without separating its increments from integers (`e(n)=1` is an exact
falsifier). The first failed Gram inference is selecting a prescribed coordinate
and a little-o rate from an unweighted bounded square sum. CFG-2 survives both
that Gram bound and qualitative normality.

The first failed finite-place inference is replacing the actual centered
numerator by any CRT representative. BDE-3 gives one chronologically consistent
alternative and spells out exactly which data it preserves. The authentic
zero seed AND every original injection uniquely specify U; none of these
countermodels is a counterexample to a theorem retaining that complete pair.

The first failed analytic inference is exchanging a fixed-character boundary
limit with a moving H_A and growing Lambda_A without a uniform bound. The
correct changed-seed Ward equation also has the changed initial term. Its Abel
normalization vanishes, which cannot recover the lost quantitative rate.

Fresh finite falsifiers retain raw failure indices: dropped fourth pole in
AH-01, reversed digit orientation in CFG-2, nonunit modular frequency and the
wrong angular channel in the BDE instrument, and a zero full-epoch sum with nonzero maximal
subinterval in the signed instrument. A finite negative control rejects a
component inference; it is not a probabilistic proof of any universal theorem.
