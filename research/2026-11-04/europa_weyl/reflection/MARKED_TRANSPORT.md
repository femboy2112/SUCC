**Proof of 1.** At n≥2 the three positive taps contribute at most 5/(8n−7)<1. The first positive tap alone dominates the three negative magnitudes, by 2/(8n−7)−5/[4(8n−3)]>0. Hence r(n)={y_n−16y_(n−1)}. Solve the eight equations r(n)=Σ_(j=1..8)w_j/[8(n−1)+j] for consecutive n=a+1,...,a+8. Two solutions would give a rational function Σ v_j/(z+j) vanishing at the eight distinct sample points z=8(n−1). Its numerator over the common degree-eight denominator has degree at most seven, hence vanishes identically. Residues at z=−j then force every v_j=0. This also supplies an explicit linear system using only rational data. For an unknown tap vector the small-forcing interval or a different unwrap rule must be part of the model. After recovering the taps the reflected infinite kernel 1/(1+x²) yields all four-periodic beta coefficients, and their Dirichlet logarithmic derivative yields the signed atoms (the finite-prefix recovery theorem gives a finite alternative). This is exact reconstruction in the declared tap class with provided order, clock and units. A seed shift s=1/5 changes y_n by 1/5 while preserving every inferred forcing because 16≡1 mod 5.

**Proof of 2.** From b and the unit coefficient u(1)=1 recover recursively

    u(m) = Σ_(d|m,d>1) b(d) u(m/d) / log m,  m≥2.

The identity b*chi=chi log proves inductively that u(m)=chi(m). Each log is the natural logarithm of an integer; free rational log-prime vectors also allow exact calculation on beta data, since independence over the rationals follows from unique factorization. For 0≤l≤8N−2, form

    q_l = u(l+1) − 16^(−N) Σ_(j=0..l)(−1)^j binom(8N,j) u(l+1−j).
