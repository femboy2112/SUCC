**EW2 — The marked affine step (PROVED; classical algebra with the cited source data).** ZX=e_M(1)XZ by action on each basis vector. Write D(u,h)=e_M(uh/2)X^u Z^h, where 1/2 means inverse of 2 in R. Then
```

Multiplication of these affine matrices gives (b,s)(c,t)=(b+sc,st) with no additional scalar in this gauge. Translations here are Heisenberg elements, distinct from the linear symplectic subgroup.

Use F|x⟩=M⁻¹ᐟ² Σ_y e_M(xy)|y⟩ with **positive** exponent. Orthogonality of the finite characters gives F†F=I and F²|x⟩=|−x⟩. This fixes central phases on the displayed operators. The negative-exponent convention reverses the rotation. The covariance characterizes Weil implementers of each linear symplectic transformation up to a scalar: any two differ by a unitary commuting with X,Z, necessarily scalar since Z has simple spectrum and X cycles it. **EW3 — Refinement and its obstruction (PROVED).** Let M|N, d=N/M. Physical positions inject by i(a)=d a. Character labels restrict by reduction y↦y mod M, since e_N(y d a)=e_M((y mod M)a). Such a section of reduction exists exactly when gcd(d,M)=1: its generator lift c must satisfy c≡1 mod M and d|c, and these conditions suffice. Modulus 9 to 27 already gives a nonsplit dual restriction.

Define the *other* isometry K|h⟩=d⁻¹ᐟ²Σ_{y≡h (mod M)}|y⟩. Then
```
F_N J = K F_M,          F_N K = J F_M.
```
The first follows by inspecting the y coefficient on |a⟩. For the second, the finite sum over each fiber vanishes unless its output index is divisible by d, in which case the normalization gives M⁻¹ᐟ². Both J and K compose under divisibility. This gives a canonical rotation diagram between the position and dual towers.

For M<N there exists **no isometry W** with W|0_M⟩ a scalar multiple of |0_N⟩ and F_N W a unit scalar multiple of W F_M. Taking the absolute value of the zero return coefficient would equate 1/√M with 1/√N. This proof allows all global phase gauges and every extension of the map on the other kets. It leaves EW3's two distinct isometries and every fixed-level rotation intact.

*Elementary relative Mann slicing.* Suppose m≤A distinct roots of unity occur in a relation with nonzero coefficients in K=ℚ(ζ_B), minimal in support. It must have m≥2. Choose any two members and a prime p>m coprime to B dividing their ratio's order. Take L the lcm of the roots' orders and B, write L=p^a D with (p,D)=1. All roots have unique coordinates ζ_{p^a}^{v_j}η_j with η_j of order dividing D. Put K'=ℚ(ζ_D)⊇K. The minimal polynomial of ζ_{p^a} over K' is Φ_{p^a} since [ℚ(ζ_L):K']=φ(L)/φ(D)=φ(p^a). Grouping the weighted η_j by 0≤v<p^a yields a polynomial of degree <p^a, with at most m nonzero coefficients in K', divisible by Φ_{p^a}. Every nonzero such multiple has at least p coefficients: the multiplier has degree <p^{a−1} and its p shifted copies in Φ_{p^a} times the multiplier have disjoint supports. As m<p, the grouped polynomial is identically zero. The two chosen roots lie in distinct v groups, so one nonempty proper group furnishes a smaller vanishing relation with the *original* K-coefficients. This contradicts minimal support. This proves EW7; it uses no Galois averaging. Compare Henry B. Mann, *On linear relations between roots of unity*, Mathematika 12 (1965), 107–117, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300005210). The elementary argument only needs the large-prime part of Mann's bound and also handles the stated coefficient fields.
