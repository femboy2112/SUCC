# Chronological checkpoint transfer and the denominator of a selected phase difference

Use the original four poles, literal seed zero, and the unreduced LCM register of the main dossier. Write den for the positive reduced denominator, || || for circle distance and log for the natural logarithm. All statements below are paper arguments. All prime asymptotics use the classical prime number theorem in a fixed residue class. No finite sample serves as asymptotic authority.

## 1. Uniform difference denominator (disclosed)

For all integers 0<=n<t, with d=t-n, uniformly as t grows,
$$
 \log \operatorname{den}(U_t-U_n)\ge(11/3-o(1))t .
$$
More explicitly, let G_t be the product of p>5 in the disjoint sets
$$
 p\equiv1\pmod4,\quad 2t<p\le8t-7,
 \qquad
 p\equiv3\pmod4,\quad 8t<3p, p\le4t-1.
$$
Then G_t/gcd(G_t,16^d-1) divides den(U_t-U_n). The log of this gcd is at most O(sqrt(t) tau(d) log(2t)) uniformly. In particular, after the finite evacuation of #492 AV-07, only the A diagonal pairs remain for sufficiently large epochs, contributing q/A.

**Proof.** For a split prime in the stated interval the only pole through time t is the denominator p in the 8j-7 or 8j-3 channel, depending on the class modulo 8. The next positive multiple of p congruent to 1 modulo 4 after p is 5p>8t. In the 8j-4 channel the first possible multiple is 4p>8t, and in the 8j-2 channel it is at least 2p; for split p, 2p is 2 mod 8 and cannot occur (8j-2 is 6 mod 8); the first such multiple is 6p. For inert p the only pole through t is 8j-2=2p at j=(p+1)/4. The other odd channels first admit at least 3p>8t and the 8j-4 channel at least 4p. All displayed upper endpoints ensure the unique birth b is at most t. Its residue c=p r(b) is a p-unit (4 or -1 in the split case, -1/2 in the inert case). Before b the selected state is p-integral, and after b it has pU_j congruent to c·16^(j-b) mod p. Thus if b>n the gap survives at p; if b<=n it survives unless p divides 16^d-1.

The weighted size log G_t=(11/3)t+o(t) by PNT-AP. For a removed p, s=ord_p(16) divides d. The removed primes with s<=sqrt(t) have log product at most the sum over divisors s|d, s<=sqrt(t), of s log16, at most sqrt(t) tau(d) log16. For s>sqrt(t), the progression p=1 mod s contains at most 8sqrt(t)+1 positive integers up to 8t. Summing over the divisors of d bounds their log product by (8sqrt(t)+1) tau(d) log(8t). The elementary divisor bound tau(d)=O_epsilon(t^epsilon) for d<=t makes the removal o(t). For completeness the divisor bound follows by writing d as a prime-power product: (a+1)<=2^a<=p^(epsilon a) for sufficiently large p; finitely many smaller p each contribute a bounded factor sup_a (a+1)/p^(epsilon a). This proves the uniform bound.

## 2. Almost every temporal difference saturates the whole conductor (disclosed)

For every fixed $\eta>0$ and all sufficiently large integers $A$, there is a set $D_A(\eta)$ of at most $C_\eta$ positive lags, with $C_\eta$ independent of $A$, such that
$$
 A\le t<2A,\quad 0\le n<t,\quad t-n\notin D_A(\eta)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \log\operatorname{den}(U_t-U_n)\ge(6-\eta)t .
$$
All upper bounds are log den(U_t-U_n)<=log Q_t=6t+o(t), since both states lie on the source grid. Thus at most $O_\eta(A)$ ordered off-diagonal epoch pairs violate the near-maximal lower bound, with normalized energy contribution $O_\eta(q/A)$.

**Proof.** It suffices to treat $0<\eta<1$. Fix $0<\epsilon<\eta/8$. For every $t\in[A,2A)$ the weight of the primes $p>\epsilon A$ born by time $t$ is at least $(6-\eta/4)t$ for large $A$: split primes up to $8t-O(1)$ and inert primes up to $4t-O(1)$ are born, and removing all $p\le\epsilon A$ costs at most $(\epsilon+o(1))A$ by PNT. Since $p>(\epsilon/2)t$, the on-main paper theorem CG-5 (also PF-04/05 of #497) gives $v_p(U_t)=-1$ uniformly for each such $p$. Write $S_t$ for this set; its members have $p^2>16A$, so $pU_j$ is defined modulo $p$ throughout $0\le j<2A$. The union $S$ of all $S_t$ has total log weight at most $(12+o(1))A$.

Count all $p$-pole events $j<2A$ for $p\in S$ with weight $\log p$ per event. Every $p$ has $O_\epsilon(1)$ events because the four denominators are bounded by $16A$ and $p>\epsilon A$. The total event weight is $O_\epsilon(A)$. At a single time $j$ the event weight is at most $4\log(16A)$ by the product of the four denominators.

Partition the indices $[0,2A)$ greedily into consecutive cells: stop just before the next index would bring the cell's event weight above $\eta A/8$. Each closed cell except possibly the last has weight at least $\eta A/8-4\log(16A)\ge\eta A/16$ at sufficiently large $A$, so there are $O_\eta(1)$ cells. For a pair $(n,t)$ let $S_{n,t}$ be the subset of $S_t$ left after discarding every $p$ with a pole event in either the $n$-cell or the $t$-cell. Its weight is at least $(6-\eta/2)t$. Define $E_{n,t}$ to consist of $p\in S_{n,t}$ with $pU_n=pU_t\pmod p$. Every pool prime outside $E_{n,t}$ divides the reduced gap denominator. Hence if the pair violates the claimed lower bound (a bad pair), its lost set $E_{n,t}$ has log weight at least $\eta t/2\ge\eta A/2$.

Take bad pairs $(n,t)$ and $(n',t')$ in the same ordered rectangle of cells. Every common lost prime has no injections between $n$ and $n'$, nor between $t$ and $t'$. Transport gives $pU_{n'}=16^{n'-n}pU_n$ and $pU_{t'}=16^{t'-t}pU_t$ modulo $p$, with inverses used for negative exponents. Each lost equality has nonzero common value since $p\in S_t\cap S_{t'}$. Therefore if the lags $d=t-n$ and $d'=t'-n'$ differ, $p$ divides $16^{|d-d'|}-1$. By Section 1's order grouping, valid for all $p\le16A$ and $0<|d-d'|\le2A$, the common log weight is $o(A)$ uniformly.

Choose a fixed integer $k>26/\eta$. If a rectangle contains $k$ distinct bad lags, choose one bad pair of each lag. The union of their lost sets has log weight at least $k\eta A/2$ minus the sum over the $\binom{k}{2}$ pairwise intersections. This exceeds $(12+o(1))A$ for sufficiently large $A$ because $k$ is fixed, contradicting the weight of $S$. Hence fewer than $k$ lags occur per rectangle. Unioning over the rectangles proves the claim. For $0<\eta<1$ take $\epsilon=\eta/16$. There are $O(1/\eta)$ events per retained prime, $O(A/\eta)$ event weight in total, $O(\eta^{-2})$ cells, and $O(\eta^{-4})$ rectangles. Thus $C_\eta=O(\eta^{-5})$ with an absolute implied constant; the sufficiently-large-$A$ threshold still depends on $\eta$. Each lag supports at most $A$ pairs ending in the epoch; the reverse order doubles the bound and the triangular kernel is at most one. This is a constraint on the actual zero-seeded gap denominators. A large denominator permits a centered numerator as small as 1.

**Where exceptional lags can lie.** Along every sequence $t\to\infty$ with either $n=o(t)$ or $d=t-n=o(t)$ the true gap has log denominator $(6+o(1))t$, without deleting any lag. For $n=o(t)$ use $\operatorname{den}(U_t)\mid\operatorname{den}(U_t-U_n)\operatorname{den}(U_n)$ and CG-01. For $d=o(t)$ fix $\epsilon>0$ and retain large born primes $p>\epsilon t$ in the selected state at $t$ as above. Discard the uniformly $o(t)$ log weight of primes dividing $16^d-1$. Without any $p$-pole between $n$ and $t$, the nonzero end residue equals $16^d pU_n$, so the surviving prime divides the gap. Each remaining possible loss requires $8j-c=kp$ for one of the four offsets, $n<j\le t$, $1\le k\le8/\epsilon$. For each fixed $k,c$, such $p$ lie in $((8n-c)/k,(8t-c)/k]$, an interval of length $o(t)$ with endpoints comparable to $t$. Ordinary PNT bounds its prime log weight by $o(t)$ by subtraction of the two theta asymptotics. There are only finitely many intervals at fixed $\epsilon$. Discarded small primes cost at most $(\epsilon+o(1))t$. Let $t\to\infty$ and then $\epsilon\downarrow0$. Thus a fixed positive fractional deficit on an infinite sequence forces both $n/t$ and $d/t$ to stay bounded away from zero along that sequence after discarding finitely many terms.

## 3. Copying genuine checkpoint data into a clustered source-native trajectory (disclosed countermodel)

For integer A large, let B be the birth times b in [A,2A) with p=8b-7 prime. Let L,m>=1 be integer depths with max(L,m)=o(A). Pick an arbitrary subset of the digit positions in an epoch by requiring all L forward completed base-16 digits at each b in B. Simultaneously there exists a nonintegral rational seed s_A, of denominator dividing Q_A, with **the original forcing at every time**, having all the following properties:

1. At every b in B, the completed phase agrees with the genuine completed orbit in its first L digits, and ||U_b(s_A)-U_b(0)||<16^(-L).
2. For all times, U_j(s_A)-U_j(0) is p-integral for every inert prime p, every p<=2A, and every first-pole birth prime in this epoch or later. Thus these primary fractional components and passports agree exactly. For j>=A the state lies on the genuine unreduced Q_j grid. The initial condition is changed.
3. With at least g=[A-(L+m-1)|B|]_+ good times, all in the non-birth interior, the genuine-forcing countertrajectory lies in the circle image of [-1/(64A²),16^(-m)]. Consequently
$$
 J_{A,q}(s_A)/q\ \ge (g/A)^2\big(1-q(16^{-m}+1/(64A^2))\big)_+ .
$$
4. For every pair in the epoch the same s_A can additionally preserve
$$
 \log\operatorname{den}(U_t(s_A)-U_n(s_A))
 \ge \log\operatorname{den}(U_t(0)-U_n(0))-O(\log^2 A).
$$
The absolute implied constant is independent of the ordered pair. This is a one-sided height comparison; equality of exact numerators is a strictly finer observation.

**Proof.** Let alpha=sum r(j)16^(-j)=pi/16 and T_n=sum_{j>=1}16^(-j)r(n+j). The exact completion is U_n(s)=16^n(alpha+s)-T_n. From 0<r(k)<15/[(8k-7)(8k-6)] and the geometric tail, 0<T_n<1/(64 n²) for n>=1. In the M=A-1+max(L,m) digit positions of the completed orbit at time A, start with zeros and copy the corresponding genuine digits in positions b-A+1,...,b-A+L for all b in B. Overlaps are consistent, since all words come from the same genuine path. Any completed phase at A in the middle half of this cylinder keeps its digits through M (the middle-half choice also supplies a certification margin). The forward m-digit window of time n intersects a copied window for at most (L+m-1)|B| distinct n, by a union bound alone. At all other times the completed phase belongs to [0,16^(-m)], proving the stated forced arc after subtracting T_n. The same tail cancels in each checkpoint comparison.

To place this word on the actual odd clock, take C to be the product of all split primes 2A<p<8A-7. Every such prime has appeared by A through 8j-7 or 8j-3 and divides Q_A; the strict upper endpoint protects a possible birth at A. PNT-AP gives log C=(3+o(1))A. Since 16 is invertible mod C, setting the seed residue s_A=16^(-A) j/C modulo 1 (here multiplication by 16^(-A) means its inverse in Z/CZ on the NUMERATOR j) gives every translate j/C at time A. Thus an interval of H consecutive j mod C realizes the desired word, with H at least floor(C/(2·16^M)) and log H=(3-log16+o(1))A. Ignore j=0 if necessary. The denominator of this seed divides C for all times and hence has the protections claimed. This proves (1)–(3) independent of (4).

To obtain (4), use a uniform count on this finite interval of j, without introducing any randomness in the true seed or source. Only p|C can remove an original denominator factor, and p>2A has p²>16A for all sufficiently large A. At these p all original states and all shifts have at most one negative power of p. For a fixed pair n<t, a prime p originally dividing its gap can be lost only if the shift (16^(t-A)-16^(n-A))j/C cancels its nonzero p-primary residue. This is either impossible or a single residue class of j mod p. For any k distinct candidate primes CRT gives at most H/(product p)+1 admissible j with all k losses (use integer representatives on one consecutive interval, including intervals that wrap mod C). Hence the fraction with at least k distinct losses on that pair is at most
$$
 \frac{(\sum_{p|C}1/p)^k}{k!}+\frac{(8A)^k}{H}
 \le \frac{4^k}{k!}+\frac{(8A)^k}{H}.
$$
Here all p>2A, with fewer than 8A candidates. For k=ceil(4 log A), Stirling's elementary factorial bound shows even after multiplication by A² this tends to zero; the second term does also because log H is linear in A. Thus some nonzero residue j simultaneously loses fewer than k primes for every pair. Each lost prime costs at most log(8A), proving (4). This argument even allows extra single-time gaps against the zero value, by the same one-residue count. The construction is effective for any specified finite epoch once its conditions hold: the original rational BBP prefix and its positive tail bound certify each requested completed digit by increasing the cutoff (irrationality of pi excludes a radix endpoint). Enumerate the admissible j and test the finitely many exact reduced denominators. No externally supplied pi digits enter.

**Scale and consequence.** Take m=ceil(2 log_16 log A). For L=o(log A), |B|~2A/log A from PNT-AP gives J/q_A→1. If L=ceil(theta log_16 A), with 0<theta<log16/2, then liminf J/q_A>=(1-2theta/log16)^2. In particular the entire collection of true accumulated birth phases to error A^(-1), plus exact Gamma forcing, the protected prime clocks and essentially **every actual pair denominator height**, coexists with order-q collision energy in one different-seed path. The same conclusions hold for the restriction to non-birth times. The all-pair height comparison implies the same near-maximal conductor result of Section 2 on these epochs after a fixed eta is enlarged by an arbitrarily small constant. A fixed rational seed across epochs is outside this counterexample. Authentic polynomially precise observations at rate A^(-4) of the *accumulated states* exceed the range of this union bound; #498 GP-04 supplies that precision only for *injections*. The unsampled chronological numerator in the old split reserve remains a possible discriminator.
