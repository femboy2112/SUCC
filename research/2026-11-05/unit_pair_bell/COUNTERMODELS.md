# Countermodels — where the paired zero/entanglement interpretation stops

1. **Zero is not entanglement by itself.** Declare a Hilbert tensor product \(\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^2\). Both (a) the entangled \((|00\rangle-|11\rangle)/\sqrt2\) and (b) separable \(|+\rangle\otimes|-\rangle\) have zero overlap with \(\Phi^+=(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt2\). State (a) has Schmidt rank 2, state (b) rank 1. A zero projected scalar cannot certify entanglement.

2. **Classically correlated is not coherent.** The dephased mixture of \(|00\rangle\) and \(|11\rangle\) with probabilities proportional to \(a_n^2,b_n^2\) has exactly the same one-qubit reduced states as the pure encoded pair, but it has a fixed joint Bell-plus probability 1/2. The pure encoded state has \(p_+=(a_n-b_n)^2/[2(a_n^2+b_n^2)]<1/101\). The off-diagonal coherence matters.

3. **A split exact sequence is not a nontrivial extension.** The difference map \(A^2\to A\) has section \(u\mapsto(u,0)\); its kernel \(\{(x,x)\}\) is nonzero but the extension splits. Only adding the source/time operator gives a nontrivial cokernel obstruction. Do not claim an Ext^1 class from the split sequence.

4. **General pair lift underdetermined.** Replacing \(a_n,b_n\) with \(a_n+c_n,b_n+c_n\) leaves the difference \(U_n\) invariant but changes all Hilbert amplitudes, Schmidt magnitudes and bright probabilities after normalization. The common-mode law \(c_n=16^n\), initial paired unit and chosen Hilbert tensor product are essential EXTRA specifications.

5. **Same source, wrong homogeneous seed.** With original forcing and initial \(s_*=-\pi/16\), the completed orbit is coherent \(-T_n\) rather than π-normal. Its rational forcing classes, radial completed zero, radial \(\tau\) loop, and most phase-transport identities are unchanged. The paired-unit condition \(a_0=b_0=1\) rejects this seed by initial authentication, but that does NOT prove signed mixing for the authenticated seed.

6. **Coherent zero-forcing control.** Let r=0, a0=b0=1, a_n=b_n=16^n. The normalized Bell lift is \(\Phi^-\) for every n and its bright probability is zero. The difference orbit is identically zero, maximally nonnormal. Thus Bell entanglement, permanent darkness or source-relative zero alone do not imply π mixing.

7. **Quantum/local phase ambiguity.** Under a local phase rotation \(e^{iG(t)Z_A/2}\otimes I\), entanglement and local marginals are constant while joint Bell brightness varies. The identity \(p_++\mathcal N=1/2\) belongs to the real-amplitude phase convention; the proper complex law is \(p_+(\varphi)=1/2-\mathcal N\cos\varphi\). Reusing the real identity for changing phase is a refuted inference.

8. **Two nontrivial rational-time classes do not imply quantum entanglement.** Weighted residues establish algebraic nontriviality in a specified rational-function source module. The actual BBP arithmetic is not given as physical qubits, and the chosen Bell Hilbert representation is not canonical. No Bell inequality or experiment has been performed on π digits.

9. **Encoding Weyl as Bell brightness is tautological.** A phase \(\varphi_n=2\pi hU_n\) injected into Bell amplitudes makes quadrature Bell measurements encode \(\operatorname{Re} e(hU_n)\) and \(\operatorname{Im}e(hU_n)\), but gives no bound on either chronological mean. This is a representation (class B), not a signed cancellation result (class A).

10. **Source-relative analytic moment ≠ chronological orbit.** The \(\tau\)-Mellin/Beta observer from #509 can retain a positive oriented current when its scalar moment vanishes. That source integral is distinct from the source-selected time-indexed signed Weyl sum. A source-to-orbit inequality with certified transport and original carries remains unproved.

**Verdict:** none of these controls refutes π normality. They constrain the evidentiary scope of proposed routes from relative zero, source cohomology and quantum-like entanglement to it.
