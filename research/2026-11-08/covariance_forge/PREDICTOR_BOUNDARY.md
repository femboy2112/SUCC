# A sufficient predictor premise is genuinely stronger in the factor class

This is a **changed-complement analytic control**, not a BBP orbit or a new
seed. Keep the authentic local-prime character a_n on J_A=[A,2A), and define

\[
b'_n=\overline{a_n}e(n/A).
\]

Both factors have unit modulus, and for every A>=2 their full product average
is exactly zero: the A values e(n/A) run over all Ath roots of unity.
Nevertheless no accurate cheap polynomial-chirp predictor of the type in
CF-R1/R3 exists for b'. Indeed for v=sum_j c_j e(P_j), degree budget d_A>=1,
and K=sum_j|c_j|, the same uniform bound applied to P_j(n)-n/A gives

\[
1=\left|\mathbb E_{J_A}a_n e(-n/A)b'_n\right|
\le\rho_A K+\mathbb E_{J_A}|b'_n-v_n|.
\]

Thus rho_A K->0 and prediction error->0 are impossible for this family,
although its full mean is identically zero. This rigorously separates the
cheap-predictor sufficient condition from cancellation in the general
unit-factor class with the same authentic local channel. The literal BBP
complement remains a particular source-specific object; this control neither
proves nor disproves that it admits the required predictor.

The control was proposed in the spectral bearing's bounded review and derived
here using its already-proved uniform twist bound. The exact algebra is the
root-of-unity identity, not a numerical fit. It is useful for framework design:
a failed predictor certificate is a failure of that route, not evidence of
failure of the mathematical target.
