# Second-round discriminating probe, before spectral measurements

The first covariance pilot/holdouts did not refute R4. That observation does
not determine whether the complementary phase is temporally compressible.
The independent spectral derivation gives a sufficient premise involving a
small cyclic Fourier predictor and its L2 residual. This is stronger than
the desired full-phase cancellation, and could fail for the original source.

Use clean primes p=281,617,1009,1601,2017, each with interval
I=[(p-1)/4+1,(p-1)/2+1). These cases are fresh **spectral** probes, not fresh
full-Weyl data where their intervals overlap the first experiment. For each
h=1,2, fold b=e(h(U-[U]_p)) over the m=ord_p(16) residue classes of I.
Test the deliberately falsifiable finite proposal: the best s=floor(sqrt(m))
temporal Fourier terms approximate this folded B with normalized L2 error
at most1/2. No fit or data-dependent change in s is allowed.

Compute every DFT coefficient with outward integer intervals. Parseval and
the exact best-sparse projection theorem give a lower bound on the optimum
residual by subtracting the s largest coefficient upper energies from a lower
bound on ||B||2². A lower bound above1/4 for residual squared is a certified
refutation; intervals straddling1/4 are inconclusive. Also retain signed
cross-spectrum, prime-clock flatness and covariance, including matched AR
controls with the same p-clock. Check direct and transformed products overlap.

This probe tests whether a promising sufficient premise actually explains
the finite source cases. Failure does not refute normality, the spectral
theorem, or every possible learning observer.
