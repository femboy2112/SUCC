# Chronology front protocol (written before new numerical measurements)

Pinned research parent: 1990b05117d84b33094c8e0f53f7d19b60f48060.
Original source: r(n)=4/(8n-7)-2/(8n-4)-1/(8n-3)-1/(8n-2), U0=0.

This front will prove exact identities and their asymptotic consequences first;
finite cases validate implementations and find algebraic mistakes. No finite
pass will promote original-zero covariance decay.

1. Derive all lag-character moments of the clean selected p-principal clock,
   retaining both integer and modular resonances. Independently compare a
   literal Fraction BBP recurrence with the modular clock at primes
   17, 41, 73, 89, 97, 137, 193, 233, 281. These are finite correctness cases,
   not an asymptotic prime sample and not a fit/holdout experiment.
2. Enumerate lag coefficient vectors in {-2,-1,0,1,2}^3 and the exact controls
   (-16,1), (p,0), (1,0). Compare phase numerator multisets before taking
   exponentials. Deliberately omit the modular resonance test as a negative
   control; the (p,0) case must reject that shortcut.
3. Prove an interval/cylinder discrepancy bound using exact finite-field
   Fourier completion and the same declared BK input. Independently enumerate
   all hexadecimal words of lengths 1..4 at these clocks. Verify exact source
   digit extraction equals interval extraction, the support-size TV lower
   bound, and the exact saturation formula when 16^d >= p.
4. Validate source-boundary truncation against a real pole transition; the
   periodic clock extension must fail after the next source birth. This is an
   authenticated-source control, not an error in the clock formula's domain.
5. Prove the finite support information horizon and a fixed-seed chronology
   rigidity result. Test rational carry-cylinder endpoints, a nonzero seed
   that eventually leaves the zero carry prefix, and integer seeds whose
   carries agree when the initial floor tag is discarded.

No numerical BK exponent is supplied. Counts and phases are exact rational
or integer data. There is no all-epoch numerical hypothesis being fitted.
