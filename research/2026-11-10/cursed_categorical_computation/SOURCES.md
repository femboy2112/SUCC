## Checked external primary sources

### S1. Two-object Cartesian closed representation

P. H. Rodenburg and F. J. van der Linden, “Manufacturing a cartesian closed category with exactly two objects out of a C-monoid,” *Studia Logica* **48** (1989), 279–283. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00370825).

The primary theorem statement and author-uploaded text were inspected through the [author upload](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/226368017_Manufacturing_a_cartesian_closed_category_with_exactly_two_objects_out_of_a_C-monoid) and the [indexed manuscript](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=0cd6e84d7da97f11f9f5527d049605cb342307b8&repid=rep1&type=pdf). A direct publisher/PDF fetch was unavailable in the archival pass. The theorem assumes a C-monoid; no claim is made that the present SUCC compiler already satisfies its input contract.

### S2. Scott retracts and unary substitution

[UniMath, `AlgebraicTheories.CategoryOfRetracts`](https://unimath.github.io/UniMath/gen/rocqdoc/UniMath.AlgebraicTheories.CategoryOfRetracts.html) gives the retract-category construction, its corner equations, terminal object, products, and exponentials under the beta-lambda hypotheses. Its definitions and theorem signatures were inspected.

[UniMath, `AlgebraicTheories.PresheafEquivalence`](https://unimath.github.io/UniMath/gen/rocqdoc/UniMath.AlgebraicTheories.PresheafEquivalence.html) states the functor-category equivalence between the Lawvere theory and its unary substitution monoid. The target-category colimit hypothesis is retained. Reading generated formalization documentation is not a claim that Rocq or UniMath was compiled in this workspace.

### S3. Boundary defect and matrix units

Daniel P. Bossaller, “Ideal Extensions and Directly Infinite Algebras,” [arXiv:2009.04055](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.04055), version 2, 2021; published as [Journal of Pure and Applied Algebra, article 106982](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpaa.2021.106982).

The primary PDF was opened. Section 2 explicitly constructs matrix units from $xy=1\ne yx$. Round II specializes to rational coefficients, uses zero-based indices, and proves the finite-corner category statement with the unilateral shifts.

### S4. Incidence coalgebras and arithmetic decomposition

Imma Gálvez-Carrillo, Joachim Kock, and Andrew Tonks, “Decomposition Spaces in Combinatorics,” [arXiv:1612.09225](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.09225), version 3, 2024.

The primary PDF was opened. Sections 2.2.1–2.2.2 give the divisibility/multiplication-monoid relationship, lower décalage, and the Dirichlet incidence algebra. The affine Möbius formula in this archive is a direct coefficientwise specialization with its own proof and two-sided finite controls. No claim that the raw divisor coproduct is compatible with ordinary basis multiplication is imported.

### S5. Finite spans and matrices

David I. Spivak, *Category Theory for Scientists*, September 2013, [MIT-hosted textbook](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-s996-category-theory-for-scientists-spring-2013/9cf51d1394fc107baa6764d553dd0bb6_MIT18_S996S13_textbook.pdf), §2.5.2, pp. 47–48.

The primary PDF's definitions and matrix-composition discussion were inspected. The finite-family functor equivalence in Round II is supplied with a direct proof from decomposition into elementary families. Numeric coding is an explicit additional presentation; it does not turn matrix composition into ordinary integer multiplication.

### S6. Matrices/profunctors as functors on families

Robert Paré, “Multivariate Functorial Difference,” [author-hosted manuscript](https://www.mscs.dal.ca/~pare/Multivariate.pdf), §1.3.

The manuscript was opened and the discussion of cocontinuous functors located. It supplies the broader presheaf/profunctor context. The archive only needs the elementary finite discrete case, proved directly. No claims about the paper's more advanced Newton-series results are used.

### S7. Groupoid cardinality

John C. Baez, Alexander E. Hoffnung, and Christopher D. Walker, “Groupoidification Made Easy,” [author-hosted primary PDF](https://math.ucr.edu/home/baez/groupoidification.pdf), 2010.

The definition $|\mathcal G|=\sum_{[x]}1/|\operatorname{Aut}(x)|$ and the product/disjoint-union rules were inspected. The signed four-pole BBP groupoid is an assembly made in this research round, not a result attributed to that paper. Sign grading and absolute convergence of the separate completed sums are explicit.

### S8. FRACTRAN and PIGAME

J. H. Conway, “FRACTRAN: A Simple Universal Programming Language for Arithmetic,” in *Open Problems in Communication and Computation* (1987), [primary paper scan](https://gwern.net/doc/cs/computable/1987-conway.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-4808-8_2).
