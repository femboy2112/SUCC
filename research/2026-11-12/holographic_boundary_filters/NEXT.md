# Next cuts after the boundary-filter round

## Earned starting point

The instrument question is substantially sharper now. We can certify closure
of an accessible operator sector, characterize target ambiguity, and recover
an integer zero-grade count from finitely many authenticated damped responses
with controlled error. The scalar filters have bounded coefficient norm and
monotone even/odd brackets. This is a reusable theorem, not a missing
arithmetic estimate hidden under a new name.

The source-specific fixed-resolution obstruction also changes the search:
no fixed finite bank of damped responses giving a pointwise zero-grade
minorant can certify all sufficiently large genuine Goldbach midpoints.
The next route must vary the resolution (order or damping), change the
observable class, or exploit a source-specific inequality that is stronger
than a pointwise minorant over all nonnegative grade laws.

The positive aggregate calibration is now proved as FR04: for fixed damping,
a common even degree `d_X` of sufficiently large order `sqrt(log log X)`
makes the sum of filtered responses through X asymptotic to the total
Goldbach count `X^2/(log X)^2`. This applies one degree to the entire
cutoff experiment. It neither gives individual positivity nor estimates
each coupled response, so the next cut below is genuinely pointwise.

## Cut A: adaptive, coupled-source response estimates

**Object.** Keep
`F_n(s)=sum_(a=0)^(n-2) s^[Omega(n+a)+Omega(n-a)-2]`, with the same midpoint
in both factors. Do not replace it by a product of marginal averages.

**Desired statement.** Select declared `d_n,t_n` and obtain an authenticated
lower estimate of `sum_r a_r F_n(t_n^r)` that exceeds its total error for
every sufficiently large `n`. The admissible domain, dependence on `n`,
absolute versus normalized errors, and the acquisition method must be
written before a claim is tested.

**Noncircularity test.** Remove every use of the desired prime-pair count,
or a premise equivalent to its positivity. What inequality remains?
Choosing `epsilon_d(n-1)<1` and evaluating the source by factoring all
endpoints is a valid finite counting procedure; it is not an all-target
proof. Importing a pointwise shifted multiplicative-function correlation
as a “routine estimate” is particularly dangerous: it may contain the
original additive-prime difficulty.

**Cheapest discriminating probe.** Derive a quantitative range of validity
for an actual proposed analytic response estimate as the smallest damping
node approaches zero. A fixed-parameter Selberg--Delange theorem controls
the midpoint average used in FR-01; it does not control each midpoint, and
its asymptotics cannot be assumed uniform when the bank changes. Retain
rare-source, composite-grade and source-coupling mutants. A failure should
leave an explicit countermodel, nonuniformity estimate or precision lower
bound.

**Possible exit products.** A source-specific adaptive inequality;
a rigorous obstruction for a broader filter class; or a quantitative
oracle/precision tradeoff. An impressive finite positivity plot alone
is not an exit product.

## Cut B: one uniform BBP escape certificate

**Object.** The literal-zero four-pole recurrence and the completed phase
`16^n*pi/16`. Preserve integer lifts, future-tail transport and the selected
source. The open target is R0: a constant `eta>0` with
`1-Re W_B(1)>=eta` for every sufficiently large integer shell start `B`.

**Available instrument.** A source-derived cell census gives a rational
minorant and an explicit vanishing tail payment. If a proof can bound the
mass away from the zero phase uniformly, the instrument will transfer that
bound to R0. Stating that census bound as a hypothesis is a reformulation
until it is derived from source properties already known.

**Discriminator.** Any proposed argument must explain why the authentic
source survives while the coherent completed-zero counterseed fails. A
criterion insensitive to that distinction cannot prove the selected
positive margin. Carry and winding must remain if the criterion needs them.
Uniform marginal spectra, filter invertibility or zero-seed authentication
alone supply no such inequality.

**Exit products.** A uniform source-specific escape estimate; an exact
invariant obstructing the proposed observer; or a proved source-sensitive
criterion strictly weaker than the original target and not merely renamed.
Finite shell extension is appropriate only to falsify a specified rival,
not as a substitute for the uniform quantifier.

## Cut C: when full interior reconstruction is worth doing

Use the ordered closure construction when a concrete model can supply
the joint Gram data and when a finite invariant reachable sector is
plausible. Pay the global contraction assumption for approximate word
bounds, and measure Gram conditioning. A new direction with positive
closure residual is useful evidence of an incomplete instrument.

Prefer a target-specific dual certificate when full reconstruction would
cost more data than the desired property needs. The exact ambiguity
diameter identifies which target distinctions still need new probes.
For phase reconstruction, check support-group torsion as well as rank;
full support is sufficient but not a generic guarantee of uniform stability.

## Execution discipline for the next round

1. Reconstruct the then-live main and donor heads; do not inherit stale
   “open” or “proved” labels from this snapshot without checking.
2. Choose one of the explicit cuts above and preregister the rival outcomes.
3. Separate response access, source authentication, inversion and the
   arithmetic estimate. Name the weakest unproved arrow.
4. Derive a falsifier that can reject the preferred frame. Use a different
   implementation or mathematical decomposition, with common provenance
   disclosed.
5. Bank the theorem, counterexample or exact failed-premise receipt, then
   update the ledger without promoting a universal claim from finite runs.

The meaningful next question is now concrete: **which authentic boundary
responses can we estimate uniformly while retaining enough resolution to
separate a single prime-pair witness or a persistent BBP escape?**
