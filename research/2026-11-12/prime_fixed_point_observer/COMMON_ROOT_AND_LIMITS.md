# A common root, its analytic image, and the finite-observation boundary

**Date:** 2026-11-12. **Continuation:** [return families](RETURN_PATHS.md).
**Verdict:** the constructed return algebra has a common eigenvector. At
source coordinate zero it is the nonzero basis vector `epsilon_0`; in the
Hardy-space realization it is the constant function one. This is an exact
local/analytic correspondence. It does not make every dynamical operator
share that eigenvector or establish an absolute proof-hardness scale.

## 1. Recentring is a change of coordinates

Let `J:l2(N_{>0})->l2(N_0)` be the unitary `J e_(k+1)=epsilon_k`.
The vector `epsilon_0` has norm one. It is **not the zero vector**; zero is
its source label. Write `D_d epsilon_k=epsilon_(dk)` for dilation about this
root. The pointed dilation `W_d` becomes `D_d`, while original multiplication
has an offset:

\[
J W_d J^*\varepsilon_k=\varepsilon_{dk},\qquad
J V_d J^*\varepsilon_k=\varepsilon_{dk+d-1}.
\]

Successor still sends `epsilon_k` to `epsilon_(k+1)`. Omitting `d-1` would
change the arithmetic rather than recenter it. For `g=gcd(n,a)`, `q=n/g`,
`r=a/g`, the proper return becomes

\[
\boxed{T_{q,r}=J R_{n,a}J^*=D_rD_q^*,\qquad
T_{q,r}\varepsilon_k=
\begin{cases}\varepsilon_{rk/q},&q\mid k,\\0,&q\nmid k.\end{cases}}
\]

Here `q>r>=1` and `gcd(q,r)=1`; these hypotheses matter for iteration.

## 2. The common eigenvector theorem

**ROOT-01 — PROVED LOCAL.** Every proper return and its adjoint satisfies

\[
T_{q,r}\varepsilon_0=T_{q,r}^*\varepsilon_0=\varepsilon_0.
\]

The common **fixed space**, with eigenvalue one, is exactly `C epsilon_0`.
Even one proper return has that fixed space by the tail proof in Section 4.
Each operator has block form `1 directsum T_perp` on the root line and its
orthogonal complement. Universality is relative to this declared family.
For an arbitrary family of contractions `B_i`, the operators `1 directsum
B_i` also share a root; that property alone puts no bound on the remaining
dynamics or their complexity.

There is a useful distinction: `epsilon_1` is killed by every strict return,
so it is another common eigenvector, at eigenvalue **zero**. For the full
family, the common kernel is exactly `C epsilon_1`: the maps `D_q*`, `q>=2`,
force the coefficients at zero and at every index at least two to vanish.
This second line is not invariant under the adjoints. We claim a unique
common fixed line, not a unique common eigenvector at every eigenvalue.

Let `A` denote the unital star algebra generated by the returns. Every
operator in it has a scalar root action, defining a character

\[
\chi(A)=\langle\varepsilon_0,A\varepsilon_0\rangle,\qquad
\chi(AB)=\chi(A)\chi(B),\qquad
\chi(A^*)=\overline{\chi(A)}.
\]

The generator words have root eigenvalue one; linear combinations have the
corresponding sum of coefficients and need not fix the root. The rank-one
projection `E_0` commutes with the algebra. No claim that it belongs to the
norm-closed generated algebra is needed.

Root agreement does not determine the whole operator. For example,
`T_(5,1)-T_(5,2)` kills `epsilon_0` but sends `epsilon_5` to
`epsilon_1-epsilon_2`. This is a shared reference with nontrivial relations
off the reference.

## 3. Actual motion can break root invariance

**ROOT-02 — PROVED LOCAL countercontrol.** Authentic successor satisfies

\[
S\varepsilon_0=\varepsilon_1,\qquad
\boxed{[S,E_0]=|\varepsilon_1\rangle\langle\varepsilon_0|.}
\]

The forward unilateral shift has no nonzero Hilbert-space eigenvectors.
If `Sx=lambda x` with nonzero `lambda`, its coordinate-zero equation gives
`x_0=0`, then successive coordinates give all other coefficients zero.
For `lambda=0`, use injectivity of `S`.

The root functional ceases to be multiplicative after adding `S,S*`:
`chi(S)=chi(S*)=0`, but `chi(S*S)=chi(I)=1`. Thus the fixed-reference
construction does not extend unchanged to every source dynamic.
A coherent defect must be named as an operator or failed relation. If a
quotient identifies two routes, their difference is zero in that quotient;
a nonzero defect requires a richer representation or a relation not imposed
by that quotient.

## 4. A global limit and the exact finite bridge

**ROOT-03 — PROVED LOCAL.** Coprimality gives

\[
T_{q,r}^{\,j}=D_{r^j}D_{q^j}^*,\qquad
\|(T_{q,r}^{\,j}-E_0)x\|^2
=\sum_{k\ge1}|x_{q^jk}|^2\longrightarrow0
\quad(x\in\ell^2).
\]

The square-summable tail proves convergence for every fixed input. It does
not give uniform decay over all normalized inputs:

\[
\boxed{\|T_{q,r}^{\,j}-E_0\|=1\quad\text{at every finite }j.}
\]

The witness `epsilon_(q^j)` is sent to `epsilon_(r^j)` and has no root
component. The tail formula also gives the matching upper bound one.

Additional finite information gives explicit bridges:

- For input supported on `0,...,M`, the strict inequality `q^j>M` gives
  **exactly** `T^j x=E_0 x`. Equality fails on the witness at index `q^j`.
- If `sum_(k>=1) k^(2s)|x_k|^2 <= C^2` for `s>0`, then
  `||(T^j-E_0)x|| <= C q^(-sj)`. Every surviving positive index is at least
  `q^j`, so comparison with the weighted square sum proves the bound.

Without a support or tail bound, the analytic convergence theorem supplies
no stage common to all unit inputs. This is a quantifier distinction, not
an absolute hierarchy of proof difficulty. The classical tail/moving-basis
mechanism is also illustrated by Baez and Walker,
[*Comparing Operator Topologies*, Problem 2](https://math.ucr.edu/home/baez/quantum_theory_and_analysis/HW1.pdf).

## 5. The same root becomes the constant function one

Define `U x(z)=sum_(k>=0) x_k z^k` for `|z|<1`. With normalized circle
measure, Hardy space `H^2(D)` has norm `||f||^2=sum |x_k|^2`, so this is a
unitary correspondence:

\[
\boxed{\varepsilon_0\longleftrightarrow1,\qquad
\varepsilon_k\longleftrightarrow z^k.}
\]

Finite-support states become polynomials. The representation retains all
data. Arbitrary analytic functions need not belong to `H^2`; an undamped
infinite prime-indicator sequence is not square-summable. The classical
identification is established in the ETH Functional Analysis I notes,
[Problem 5.7](https://metaphor.ethz.ch/x/2021/hs/401-3461-00L/ex/Solution05.pdf).

**ROOT-04 — CLASSICAL correspondence with PROVED LOCAL transport.**

\[
\mathcal U D_d\mathcal U^*f(z)=f(z^d),\qquad
\mathcal U T_{q,r}^{\,j}\mathcal U^*f(z)
=\sum_{k\ge0}x_{q^jk}z^{r^jk}.
\]

The root projection takes the constant coefficient. This makes a localized
source root and an analytic constant two exact views of one state, without
claiming that all arithmetic relations collapse to that constant.

## 6. Analytic smallness can hide coefficient information

Fix `0<rho<1`. Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[
\sup_{|z|\le\rho}|(T^j-E_0)f(z)|
\le\left(\sum_{k\ge1}|x_{q^jk}|^2\right)^{1/2}
\frac{\rho^{r^j}}{\sqrt{1-\rho^{2r^j}}}.
\]

**ROOT-05 — PROVED LOCAL exact observation norm.** On the whole `H^2` unit
ball, the bound is sharp:

\[
\boxed{\|T^j-E_0\|_{H^2\to C(\overline D_\rho)}
=\frac{\rho^{r^j}}{\sqrt{1-\rho^{2r^j}}}.}
\]

Set `t=rho^(r^j)` and take the unit-norm function

\[
f_j(z)=\frac{\sqrt{1-t^2}\,z^{q^j}}{1-tz^{q^j}}.
\]

Its coefficients on successive positive multiples of `q^j` are
`sqrt(1-t^2)(1,t,t^2,...)`; its image at `z=rho` attains the bound.
The standard Hardy evaluation kernel is given in the ETH notes,
[Problem 6.7(d)](https://metaphor.ethz.ch/x/2021/hs/401-3461-00L/ex/Solution06.pdf).

For `r>1`, this compact-disk operator norm tends to zero, while the
`H^2 -> H^2` error norm remains one. For `r=1`, the compact-disk norm stays
at `rho/sqrt(1-rho^2)`. Every fixed input nevertheless converges in both
observations for all `r>=1`. The radius is fixed below one; no uniform claim
up to the boundary is made.

The simplest escaping witness is `f_j(z)=z^(q^j)`, sent to `z^(r^j)`.
Its output has coefficient norm one, but compact-disk magnitude
`rho^(r^j)`. For `r>1`, an interior analytic observation becomes insensitive
to this moving coefficient mass. It has not proved that mass absent.

### An interface-specific complexity scale

Cauchy's coefficient formula on `|z|=rho` gives

\[
|\Delta c_N|\le\rho^{-N}
\sup_{|z|=\rho}|\Delta f(z)|.
\]

This is sharp for a perturbation proportional to `z^N`. The alternatives
`0` and `z^N`, both in the unit ball, differ by only `rho^N` in this
observation. If unknown worst-case observation error is at most `epsilon`,
their balls overlap when `2 epsilon >= rho^N`: the midpoint fits both.
Uniform distinction in this interface requires `epsilon < rho^N/2`.

At `rho=1/2`, coefficient-error amplification is `2^N`. Successively higher
coefficients require finer absolute precision from that measurement. This
is a concrete graded **conditioning** scale. It does not lower-bound
symbolic coefficient extraction from an exact formula, and is not a
proof-complexity theorem.

## 7. Numeral order is not automatically a hardness order

**ROOT-06 — scoped refutation and open interpretation.** Under `T_(2,1)`,
positive input `k` survives `v_2(k)` divisions before the next step rejects
it. Thus `8 -> 4 -> 2 -> 1 -> rejection`, whereas `9 -> rejection`.
Larger integer labels do not monotonically increase this local depth.
This refutes that candidate ordering, not every possible resource grade.
A proof-hardness theorem needs a proof system, allowed operations, input
encoding, resource measure, and quantified bounds.

The [typed-channel companion](TYPED_CHANNELS.md) keeps channel types, state
coefficients, resource grades, and coupling maps distinct. A cost on literal
paths can fail to descend to an equivalence class of operations because
equivalent words can have different lengths. An unexplained infinite
identification can erase just the information that an observer needs.
