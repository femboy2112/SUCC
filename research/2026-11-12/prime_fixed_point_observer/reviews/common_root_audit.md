# Common root, strong limits, and analytic observation: audit

**Date:** 2026-11-12. **Status:** direct proof audit of the centered return
operators. This document introduces no new computational experiment. It
checks the proposed statements by basis calculations and exact norm
estimates. Same-model review and a change of representation do not constitute
independent provenance for a conjecture.

## 1. The root is a nonzero vector with label zero

Center the previous positive-integer carrier by writing
`epsilon_k=e_(k+1)`, and let

\[
\mathcal H=\ell^2(\mathbb N_0),\qquad
D_d\epsilon_k=\epsilon_{dk},\qquad
E_0=|\epsilon_0\rangle\langle\epsilon_0|.
\]

Here `epsilon_0` has norm one. It is distinct from the zero vector of the
Hilbert space, which is not an eigenvector. A number label, a vector, and
the zero operator remain different objects.

For relatively prime integers `q>r>=1`, set

\[
T_{q,r}=D_rD_q^*.
\]

This is the centered version of a proper return in `RETURN_PATHS.md`.
Each `D_d` is an isometry; `D_d*` keeps multiples of `d` and divides their
labels by `d`. In particular,

\[
T_{q,r}\epsilon_0=T_{q,r}^*\epsilon_0=\epsilon_0.
\]

The unit span reduces both the operator and its adjoint. Consequently
`E_0` commutes with every such return and its adjoint.

## 2. Common fixed span and the root character

For every integer `j>=0`, coprimality gives

\[
T_{q,r}^{\,j}\epsilon_k=
\begin{cases}
\epsilon_{r^j k/q^j},&q^j\mid k,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad
T_{q,r}^{\,j}=D_{r^j}D_{q^j}^*.
\]

For `x=sum_{k>=0}c_k epsilon_k`, this implies the exact error identity

\[
\boxed{\|(T_{q,r}^{\,j}-E_0)x\|^2
       =\sum_{k\ge1}|c_{q^j k}|^2.}
\]

Since `q>=2`, the sum is bounded by a square-summable tail tending to zero.
Thus `T_(q,r)^j -> E_0` strongly, and its fixed space is exactly
`C epsilon_0`. Every **nonempty** family of these proper returns therefore
has that same common fixed space. The empty family imposes no fixed-vector
condition. The coprimality assumption is essential to the displayed power
formula; unreduced pairs are not silently included.

This is uniqueness at eigenvalue **one**, not uniqueness among all common
eigenvectors. The vector `epsilon_1` is killed by every strict return,
because no `q>=2` divides its label. It is therefore a common eigenvector
with eigenvalue zero. For the full family, its common kernel is exactly
`C epsilon_1`: `T_(q,1)x=0` for all `q>=2` forces `c_0=0` and every
`c_m=0` for `m>=2`, by choosing `q=m`. A smaller chosen family may have a
larger common kernel. This second span is not reducing for the full family:
`T_(2,1)* epsilon_1=epsilon_2`. A common eigenvector for forward returns
alone therefore does not automatically supply a character of their
`*`-algebra.

Let `A` be the unital `*`-algebra of bounded operators generated by any such
family. Relative to `H=C epsilon_0 direct-sum epsilon_0^perp`, every
`B in A` has the block form

\[
B=\chi(B)\oplus B_\perp,\qquad
\chi(B)=\langle\epsilon_0,B\epsilon_0\rangle.
\]

It follows directly that

\[
\chi(BC)=\chi(B)\chi(C),\quad
\chi(B^*)=\overline{\chi(B)},\quad
\chi(I)=1,\quad |\chi(B)|\le\|B\|.
\]

Thus the root functional is a character and extends continuously to the
operator-norm closure of `A`. Every word in the generators and their
adjoints has character one. A difference of two words kills the root,
although it can act nontrivially on its orthogonal complement. For example,
with `T=T_(2,1)=D_2*`,

\[
TT^*-T^*T=I-D_2D_2^*,\qquad
(TT^*-T^*T)\epsilon_0=0,\quad
(TT^*-T^*T)\epsilon_1=\epsilon_1.
\]

The common root therefore does not authenticate an operator identity away
from the root. It is insensitive to these nonzero defects.

## 3. Successor changes the algebra and destroys root fixation

Let `S epsilon_k=epsilon_(k+1)`. Then

\[
\boxed{[S,E_0]=SE_0-E_0S
       =|\epsilon_1\rangle\langle\epsilon_0|\ne0.}
\]

The root span is not reducing for the enlarged family. In fact, the forward
unilateral shift has no nonzero eigenvector at any complex eigenvalue.
If `Sx=lambda x` and `lambda!=0`, its zeroth coefficient forces `c_0=0`,
then all further coefficients vanish recursively. If `lambda=0`,
injectivity of `S` forces `x=0`.

The same root expectation is no longer a character once `S,S*` are allowed:
`chi(S)=chi(S*)=0`, while `chi(S*S)=chi(I)=1`. This is an explicit failure
of multiplicativity, not a philosophical objection to a universal reference.

A stronger algebraic boundary holds if the return family includes
`T_(2,1)`, hence also `D_2`. The operators `D_2` and `S D_2` are isometries
with orthogonal even/odd ranges whose projections sum to `I`. Any unital
`*`-character on this enlarged algebra would send both range projections
to one, hence send their sum to two instead of one. Therefore this enlarged
algebra admits no unital character at all. This last assertion requires
the explicitly stated generator `D_2`; it is not asserted for arbitrary
smaller enlarged families.

## 4. Strong convergence needs a declared input class for a rate

For every finite `j`,

\[
\|T_{q,r}^{\,j}-E_0\|_{\mathcal H\to\mathcal H}=1.
\]

The upper bound follows from the exact tail identity. Equality is attained
by `x_j=epsilon_(q^j)`, which is sent to `epsilon_(r^j)`. The witness varies
with `j`; this does not contradict convergence for each fixed `x`.

Two additional input assumptions provide explicit quantitative conclusions:

- If `x` is supported on `0,...,M`, then `q^j>M` implies
  `T_(q,r)^j x=E_0 x` exactly.
- If, for a declared `s>0`,
  `sum_(m>=1) m^(2s)|c_m|^2 <= C^2`, then

\[
\|(T_{q,r}^{\,j}-E_0)x\|\le Cq^{-sj}.
\]

The second claim follows by multiplying each sampled coefficient by its
index weight, using `q^j k>=q^j`, and bounding the sampled moment by the
full moment. If a different convention calls `sum m^s |c_m|^2` the moment,
the square root and exponent must be changed accordingly. A rate without
its input hypothesis would be an overclaim.

Neither numeral size nor return depth defines a universal proof-hardness
order. For the particular halving return `T_(2,1)`, label 8 survives three
divisions as `8->4->2->1` before the next application rejects it. The
larger label 9 is rejected immediately. One can state a worst-case
finite-window bound in terms of `M`, but it does not order individual
mathematical statements by difficulty.

## 5. The analytic realization preserves the Hilbert information

The coefficient map

\[
\epsilon_k\longmapsto z^k,\qquad
x\longmapsto f(z)=\sum_{k\ge0}c_kz^k
\]

identifies `l2(N_0)` isometrically with the Hardy coefficient space
`H^2(D)`, whose squared norm is `sum |c_k|^2`. The root becomes
the constant function one. Under this representation,

\[
(T_{q,r}^{\,j}f)(z)=\sum_{k\ge0}c_{q^j k}z^{r^j k},\qquad
E_0f=f(0).
\]

This unitary representation changes no Hilbert-space operator norm or
fixed-space conclusion. A new conclusion can arise only after specifying
a different observation norm or a restricted state class.

## 6. Exact compact-disk observation norm

Fix `0<rho<1`, `j>=0`, and give analytic outputs the observation norm
`sup_(|z|<=rho)|g(z)|`. Write `Q=q^j`, `R=r^j`. Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\sup_{|z|\le\rho}|(T_{q,r}^{\,j}-E_0)f(z)|
\le\|f\|_{H^2}
    \left(\sum_{k\ge1}\rho^{2Rk}\right)^{1/2}
=\|f\|_{H^2}\frac{\rho^R}{\sqrt{1-\rho^{2R}}}.
\]

This is the **exact** operator norm, not just an estimate. The function

\[
f_j(z)=\sqrt{1-\rho^{2R}}\,
       \frac{z^Q}{1-\rho^Rz^Q}
\]

has `H^2` norm one: its nonzero coefficients form the geometric sequence
`sqrt(1-rho^(2R)) rho^(R(k-1))` at indices `Qk`, `k>=1`. At `z=rho`,

\[
((T_{q,r}^{\,j}-E_0)f_j)(\rho)
=\frac{\rho^R}{\sqrt{1-\rho^{2R}}}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\|T_{q,r}^{\,j}-E_0\|_{H^2\to C(\{|z|\le\rho\})}
=\frac{\rho^{r^j}}{\sqrt{1-\rho^{2r^j}}}.}
\]

For each fixed `0<rho<1`, this norm tends to zero exactly when `r>1`.
When `r=1`, it remains the positive constant `rho/sqrt(1-rho^2)`.
Nevertheless, for **every fixed** `f in H^2`, convergence on that compact
disk still follows from strong `H^2` convergence. The lack of a unit-ball
rate and the convergence of each fixed input are compatible.

At `rho=0` the error observation is identically zero for all `r`; that
degenerate case must be separated from the stated iff. At `rho=1` point
evaluation and the supremum norm are unbounded on the `H^2` unit ball,
so the finite-radius formula cannot be used as a finite boundary bound.

If the observation radius also changes with time, the precise condition is
`rho_j^(r^j)->0`. A fixed-radius assumption cannot be silently dropped.
For example, with `r>1` and `rho_j=exp(-c/r^j)`, `c>0`, the norm is the
positive constant `exp(-c)/sqrt(1-exp(-2c))` at every time.

## 7. Why finite analytic observation has a reconstruction cost

For the exact monomial witness `f_j(z)=z^(q^j)`, the output is `z^(r^j)`.
Its `H^2` norm is one, while its norm on the radius-`rho` disk is
`rho^(r^j)`. Thus a vanishing interior observation does not imply vanishing
Hilbert-state error. The observation increasingly suppresses high modes.

Conversely, if an analytic error `g` is bounded by `delta` on that disk,
Cauchy's coefficient formula gives `|[z^m]g|<=delta rho^(-m)`. Recovering
high coefficients from finite interior accuracy therefore has an explicit
amplification factor. An exact analytic function determines its coefficients;
finite precision measurements do not furnish the same uniform information.

This supplies a concrete interpretation of an easier analytic observation
and a harder finite reconstruction. It does not prove a universal duality
between numeral layers and proof difficulty, nor does it make the observer's
weaker norm a universal certificate about the underlying arithmetic source.

## Audit verdict

The common-root statement, root character, exact tail law, conditional
moment estimate, and compact-disk operator norm are valid within the stated
domains. The successor control and the nonzero off-root commutator disprove
unrestricted versions. Uniform compact-disk decay for `r>1` is a change in
observation norm, not an improvement in the full Hilbert operator norm.
No execution claim, independent-provenance claim, universal proof-complexity
order, or prime-pair theorem is made here.
