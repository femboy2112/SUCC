# What can actually be transferred: states, reflections and visible phase differences

**Authority:** paper proofs with explicit hypotheses, plus exact finite rational
probes. These are applications and reconstructions of classical mathematics; no
priority claim, full Lean verification, Yang–Mills gap, or arithmetic cancellation
theorem is asserted. The upstream source is pinned in `upstream_manifest.json`.

The most useful lesson of the inspected construction is to bind the source,
observables, limits and reconstruction to one object. The strongest useful
comparison does **not** require every coherent direction to decay. It requires
control of the directions seen by the declared target.

## 1. Source-bound GNS transport

**YT-01 — CLASSICAL, proof reproduced.** Let A and B be unital C*-algebras,
with positive normalized states omega_A and omega_B. Suppose Phi:A->B is a
unital star-homomorphism and

\[
 \omega_A=\omega_B\circ\Phi.
\]

Write (H_A,pi_A,Omega_A) and (H_B,pi_B,Omega_B) for their GNS triples.
There is a canonical isometry V:H_A->H_B such that

\[
 V[a]_A=[\Phi(a)]_B,\qquad
 V\Omega_A=\Omega_B,\qquad
 V\pi_A(a)=\pi_B(\Phi(a))V.
\]

Its image is exactly the closed cyclic subspace generated by Phi(A) acting on
Omega_B. It is onto H_B iff that subspace is dense.

*Proof.* For a,b in A,

\[
 \langle[\Phi(a)],[\Phi(b)]\rangle_B
 =\omega_B(\Phi(a)^*\Phi(b))
 =\omega_A(a^*b)
 =\langle[a],[b]\rangle_A.
\]

Thus null vectors map to null vectors, and V is a well-defined isometry on
the dense algebraic GNS subspace. Complete it. Its range is the claimed
closed cyclic subspace. Multiplicativity proves the representation identity
on the dense subspace, hence everywhere because C*-algebra representations
are bounded. Unitality identifies the cyclic vectors. ∎

This theorem uses **all** the declared moments and products. Checking a few
Gram entries is not its hypothesis. The external construction's local fields
are unbounded operators; an application to their polynomial field algebra
also needs the common invariant domains and closure conditions. The bounded
C*-algebra theorem does not silently discharge those requirements.

### Dynamics and the direction of a spectral inference

Suppose the states are invariant under strongly continuous automorphism groups
alpha_t and beta_t, and Phi alpha_t=beta_t Phi. Their canonical GNS unitary
implementations then satisfy V U_A(t)=U_B(t)V, by applying the identity to
every class [a]. State preservation alone does not imply this equation.

For a positive-energy application it is convenient to assume explicitly
nonnegative self-adjoint Hamiltonians H_A,H_B and the bounded-semigroup identity

\[
 Ve^{-tH_A}=e^{-tH_B}V\quad(t\ge0).
\]

If H_B obeys a gap estimate
`||exp(-t H_B) v|| <= exp(-Delta t)||v||` for every v perpendicular to
Omega_B, the identical estimate holds for H_A perpendicular to Omega_A.
Indeed V maps that orthogonal complement into Omega_B's orthogonal complement
and preserves norms. Inferring a gap for **all of B** from one for A requires
surjectivity or a separate bound on the remaining B-sector.

No Phi with these properties between the authenticated BBP system and the
published Yang–Mills construction is currently supplied by SUCC. Moreover,
the external existence theorem supplies no Delta to transfer.

## 2. Reflection positivity requires the correct reflection

**YT-02 — PROVED LOCAL.** In addition to YT-01, let theta_A and theta_B be
complex-linear involutive star-automorphisms, and let A_+,B_+ be declared
positive-time observable subalgebras. Assume

\[
 \Phi(A_+)\subseteq B_+,\qquad
 \Phi\theta_A=\theta_B\Phi.
\]

If omega_B(theta_B(b*) b)>=0 for every b in B_+, then
omega_A(theta_A(a*) a)>=0 for every a in A_+.

*Proof.* Apply state preservation, multiplicativity, preservation of the
adjoint, and the reflection identity to the form itself. It becomes
omega_B(theta_B(Phi(a)*) Phi(a)), which is nonnegative. ∎

Here theta is the linear coordinate reflection; the conjugation is the
displayed adjoint. Other conventions must be translated explicitly. Ordinary
GNS positivity omega(a*a)>=0 is a different form. The exact cosine control
in `WILSON_REFLECTION_SLICE.md` separates them.

### What survives a limit

For each finite list a_1,...,a_k of positive-time observables, let
K_j(u,v) be the reflected matrix at regulator j in one common observable
identification. Suppose every entry converges to K(u,v), and

\[
 \sum_{u,v}\overline{c_u}c_v K_j(u,v)
 \ge-\epsilon_j\left(\sum_u|c_u|\right)^2,
 \qquad \epsilon_j\to0,
\]

for all complex c. The inequalities concern real Hermitian forms. Passing
to the limit of the **finite** sum gives c* K c>=0. The same argument for
ordinary moments gives positivity of a limiting state, when its linearity,
normalization and observable-domain identification are retained.

This reproduces a positivity-preservation step. It neither proves that these
limits exist nor supplies the distributional continuity, Euclidean covariance,
growth, clustering, mixed-arity domains and continuation required for the
full Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction. Those are separate upstream theorems.

## 3. Reproduce the inspected common-coefficient phase lemma

The concrete source is
[`YMReview.OPE.phase_difference`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/YM/blob/55ba9fc36293b3f4d52c6b1f813169a0be76ee98/submissions/openai-20261028/checks/OPECheck.lean),
not a guessed analogy. Its coefficients are common to all phases, and its
radius is chosen before the two phase variables and all admissible cluster
and spectator configurations.

**YT-03 — REPRODUCED PAPER SLICE.** Suppose for each s in an index set,

\[
 F_s=\sum_{i=1}^d c_i G_{s,i}+R_s,\qquad |R_s|\le\epsilon.
\]

The same c_i and epsilon apply to every s. Then for all s,t,

\[
 \left|(F_s-F_t)-\sum_i c_i(G_{s,i}-G_{t,i})\right|
 \le2\epsilon.
\]

*Proof.* The expression inside the norm is R_s-R_t. Apply the triangle
inequality. Opposite residuals +epsilon and -epsilon make the factor two
sharp. ∎

In the inspected OPE, epsilon is

\[
 r^{D+1-\sum_i\deg(a_i)-\eta},\qquad \eta>0.
\]

It tends to zero only when the exponent is positive. Even then, the
correlations and coefficients may refer to scale-dependent configurations;
one must prove a common transport to any fixed target before treating this
as a closed equation for that target. The source OPE is **not automatically
a contracting return map**. Its authors do not derive phase uniqueness from
this lemma.

The source endpoint `OPE.constructed` is separate from the combined acceptance
endpoint. To use both on one chosen hierarchy, apply the proved
`OPE.from_source` to that hierarchy's OPE property; do not join unrelated
existential witnesses by their similar names.

`common_phase_comparison` reproduces the finite rational-real special case.
The paper argument above covers complex values. Exact tests reject changed
phase coefficients and false remainder budgets.

## 4. A sharper consequence: control only the target-visible differences

**YT-04 — PROVED LOCAL, finite-dimensional target factorization.** Let V,W
be finite-dimensional normed complex vector spaces. Let A:V->V and L:V->W
be linear. The following are equivalent:

1. `ker(I-A)` is contained in `ker L`.
2. There is a linear B:V->W such that

\[
 L=B(I-A).
\]

*Proof.* The factorization immediately gives the kernel containment.
Conversely define B_0((I-A)v)=Lv on range(I-A). The kernel containment
makes this definition independent of the choice of v. Extend B_0 linearly
from range(I-A) to V by a complementary basis. In finite dimensions the
extension is bounded. ∎

This is a statement about a target seminorm/linear readout. It does not assert
that A induces autonomous dynamics on V/ker L: that additional interpretation
requires A(ker L) to be contained in ker L. For example, with

\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad L=(1,0),
 \quad B=(1,1),
\]

the factorization holds while A sends the target-invisible vector e_2 to
the visible vector e_1. We use only the proved factorization.

### Uniform phase comparison with surviving coherent directions

Suppose one common affine relation holds for every state s in a class K:

\[
 x_s=A_r x_s+b_r+R_r(s),\qquad \|R_r(s)\|_V\le\epsilon_r.
\]

Assume L=B_r(I-A_r). Subtraction gives the exact identity

\[
 L(x_s-x_t)=B_r(R_r(s)-R_r(t)),
\]

and therefore

\[
 \boxed{\sup_{s,t\in K}\|L(x_s-x_t)\|_W
       \le2\|B_r\|\epsilon_r.}
\]

If x_s and L are fixed under a declared common identification and
`||B_r|| epsilon_r -> 0`, their target readouts agree. More generally the
same estimate yields convergence of scale-dependent readouts once their
transport and limit are separately proved. A complete observable family
can identify states; a finite family identifies only its own readings.

**The distinction that matters:** a nondecaying direction in ker(I-A_r) is
allowed when L does not see it. A visible kernel direction prevents this
particular reader. No suppression of every coherent or moving-lag direction
has been assumed.

The rational function `target_reader` constructs a B by row reduction and
rechecks B(I-A)=L exactly. It fails precisely on inconsistent finite linear
systems. It sets free coordinates to zero; it does not minimize ||B||. A
large norm from this particular choice is not a theorem that every reader
is badly conditioned. In infinite dimensions kernel inclusion alone need
not give a bounded B; closed-range/continuity issues must be paid separately.

### Four exact controls

- **Invisible resonance survives.** A=diag(1,1/2), L=(0,1), B=(0,2).
  The first coordinate can vary arbitrarily between phases. The target still
  obeys the bound. `||A||=1`, so full contraction would be an unnecessary demand.
- **Visible resonance fails.** The same A with L=(1,0) has no reader.
  States 0 and e_1 have the same zero residual and different target readings.
- **Oscillation is useful, not disqualifying.** A=-I and L=I give B=I/2.
  A quarter-turn matrix also has a bounded signed inverse for I-A. No decay
  of A's powers is required for this algebraic comparison.
- **Vanishing residual alone is insufficient.** A_N=1-1/N,
  x_+=1/2, x_-=-1/2, b_N=0 and R_N=+/-1/(2N). Here B_N=N,
  so the bound and actual difference both equal 1 for every N.

These controls are in `test_ym_phase_transport.py`.

## 5. Contraction is a convenient sufficient certificate

Let positive weights w_i define `||v||_w=max_i |v_i|/w_i`. If

\[
 \sum_j |(A_r)_{ij}|w_j\le q_r w_i,\qquad q_r<1,
\]

then ||A_r||_w<=q_r. For two phases the common affine relation implies

\[
 \|x_s-x_t\|_w\le q_r\|x_s-x_t\|_w+2\epsilon_r,
 \quad
 \|x_s-x_t\|_w\le\frac{2\epsilon_r}{1-q_r}.
\]

Equivalently, the Neumann inverse yields a reader for any bounded L. The
error must be measured relative to `1-q_r`, not merely shown to vanish.
Signed row sums cannot replace the displayed entrywise absolute norm bound.
Weights can help: A=((0,2),(1/8,0)) has unweighted row bound 2 but weighted
bound 1/2 with weights (4,1). This sufficient certificate is not necessary
for YT-04, as the invisible-resonance and oscillation controls show.

## 6. Apply the instrument to a real SUCC obstruction

For a T(x)=16x mod 1 invariant circle probability measure mu, define
`x_j = mu_hat(16^j h)` for fixed nonzero h. Invariance yields

\[
 x_0=x_1=\cdots=x_{d-1}.
\]

Represent this finite chain by A having the superdiagonal equal to 1 and
last diagonal entry 1. The constant vector lies in ker(I-A), and the target
Lx=x_0 sees it. YT-04 rejects a target reader. This is a concrete diagnosis:
the invariant atom at 0 has all these Fourier values equal to 1, whereas Haar
has them all zero. The invariance relations do not distinguish them.

Changing the last equation to `x_(d-1)=0+residual` makes A nilpotent and
gives B=(1,...,1), with infinity-norm d. The algebra now gives a useful bound
**only after paying the added boundary estimate**. That boundary condition is
not a consequence of invariance. In particular an arbitrary invariant law need
not have Fourier coefficients tending to zero at the high-frequency endpoint.
This exposes the missing estimate without falsely demanding the decay of all
coherence or treating an unproved boundary value as a harmless gauge choice.

For the original BBP sequence, finite empirical invariance has a small endpoint
defect. That defect alone does not supply the missing far-end Fourier bound.
Any next attempt must derive a different source-specific return relation or a
controlled boundary condition from the literal four-pole forcing and zero seed.

### A quantified version on the literal BBP source

**YT-08 — SOURCE TRANSPORT, re-derived.** Retain the original forcing, U_0=0,
and Y_n=U_n+T_n=16^n(pi/16). For integer B>=1, integer d>=0, and integer h,
write

\[
 M^U_{B,h}=\frac1B\sum_{n=B}^{2B-1}e(hU_n),\qquad
 M^Y_{B,h}=\frac1B\sum_{n=B}^{2B-1}e(hY_n),
 \qquad e(t)=e^{2\pi it}.
\]

The positive tail estimate can be checked directly. For n>=1 the authentic
forcing obeys

\[
 r(n+1)=\frac6{(8n+1)(8n+4)}
       +\frac4{(8n+1)(8n+5)}
       +\frac5{(8n+1)(8n+6)}
       <\frac{15}{64n^2}.
\]

Each positive summand decreases with n, so
`0<T_n=sum_(j>=1) 16^(-j) r(n+j)<r(n+1)/15<1/(64n^2)`.
Using |e(u)-e(v)|<=2pi|u-v| gives

\[
 |M^U_{B,h}-M^Y_{B,h}|\le\frac{\pi|h|}{32B^2}.
\]

For the completed orbit, Y_(n+d)=16^d Y_n. Shifting the finite window cancels
its common terms and leaves at most 2d terms of modulus one. Hence

\[
 |M^Y_{B,h}-M^Y_{B,16^dh}|\le\frac{2d}{B},
\]

where the bound remains valid, though loose, for d>B. Combining the two
completion errors proves

\[
 \boxed{|M^U_{B,h}-M^U_{B,16^dh}|
 \le\frac{2d}{B}+\frac{\pi|h|(1+16^d)}{32B^2}.}
\]

This is an all-parameter bound on transport of a signed response, not a bound
forcing that response to zero. For fixed h it becomes small whenever
`d=o(B)` and `16^d=o(B^2)`. It can move a proved estimate between those two
observables. It cannot provide the high-frequency estimate at the other end.
Thus the exact source itself exhibits the same distinction as the reader
test: controlled transport is useful, and a still-visible boundary response
requires further mathematics. The tail calculation is also the one underlying
the source completion budget in sibling #612; no priority claim is made.

### The target need not be the whole coordinate representation

Sibling #612's gauge-complete criterion uses the complete fixed-lag pinbed law,
and permits normality-preserving torsion shifts even though they change the
literal source representative. This round retains that distinction. A lawful
quotient must preserve the theorem's target; it need not reconstruct information
that the target does not use. Conversely a declaration of gauge equivalence
does not prove that a visible non-Haar mode has disappeared.

To prove normality using this pattern, one could control each fixed nonzero
Fourier observable relative to an independently justified Haar reference.
One must prove that the same expansion applies to that reference, or identify
the uniquely reconstructed state as Haar by another argument. Uniqueness of
an unspecified limit is insufficient: a constant radix orbit also has a unique
empirical limit. Neither the required expansion nor its quantitative reader
bound has been obtained for the original-zero BBP orbit in this round.

## 7. Verdict

We reproduced a real lemma from the published Yang–Mills verification and
derived an exact target-sensitive stability interface. It accepts persistent
invisible coherence, rejects visible residual modes, and exposes the cost of
an unstable inverse. This is a **reusable proof step in the transfer framework**,
with classical novelty status. It is not the missing arithmetic bound.

The next falsifiable task is to construct an authentic carry/block operator
and common observable transport, then bound `||B_r|| epsilon_r` on the
complete target-relevant family. The Wilson construction suggests an architecture
for that work; its theorem does not already provide the arithmetic inputs.
