## Faithful target

An observer starts at a contextual zero, sends lawful programmable SUCC
automata to obtain source-labelled records, and constructs models of those
records and models of models. Identify exact thresholds at which specified
unvisited future reports can be certified. Determine what an additional
level of understanding means without assuming an executed infinity, a new
physical dimension, or new information from deterministic self-processing.

## Live frontier and dependency cut

Target descent, finite behavioral/algebra closure, source-relative policies,
and epistemic macro-updates already exist in SUCC. RFO-07 in draft #628 also
proves finite predictive-span closure. Repeating those results is background,
not a new proof step. The missing distinction is between **which horizons**
are predictable and **which operations** can express a certified predictor.

1. **Horizon target (initial state OPEN):** for a fixed source map T:D→D
   and report h:D→Y with Y=h(D), characterize
   H={n≥0 : h∘T^n=g_n∘h for a function g_n:Y→Y}.
   Prove composition laws, finite certificates, sharp non-closure controls,
   and determine finite-source structure. This is global report descent;
   an individual target or one realized fiber has different quantifiers.
2. **Toolkit target (initial state OPEN):** separate target determinability
   from representability by declared operations. Test the polynomial source
   T(x,y)=(xy,y), report x, successive reports x,xy,…,xy^m.
   Compare polynomial construction with division plus a justified zero branch.
3. **Stability target (initial state OPEN):** determine whether exact
   prediction from finite reports has a continuous or quantitatively stable
   extension, with and without bounds on hidden source variables.

## Acceptance, controls and novelty tax

Deliver exact quantified proofs, reproducible exact probes, mutation controls,
and an explicit provenance/claim ledger. No priority claim over classical
quotient dynamics, numerical semigroups, observability or polynomial algebra.
The contribution must be an additional theorem/interface or discriminating
example beyond the existing all-horizon closure theorem.

Cheapest falsifiers: a report collision with different future reports;
a delayed-state trap; one target whose predictable-time set is not additive;
a singular report pair; a monomial that lies outside every proposed finite
polynomial stage; and arbitrarily close records with separated futures.
Fresh finite domains will be declared separately from illustrative examples.

The source and its admitted domain remain fixed throughout each theorem.
The literal zero-origin BBP source is not replaced by a counterseed.
No new claim about π normality, R0, RH, universal cognition, or physical
instantaneous communication follows from these targets.

## Finite/computational boundary and next lamp

Exhaustive finite checks corroborate proofs and calibrate verifiers; they do
not establish unrestricted universal claims. A finite algebraic expression
or one epistemic macro-update need not have constant running time or output
size. The next lamp is a replayable predictor certificate whose accepted
operations, source domain, horizon and stability modulus are explicit.
