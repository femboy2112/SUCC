## 1. One Boolean conclusion, a joint report, and repeated access

Write $\mathsf{Acc}(P)$ if there exists a lawful finite deterministic protocol on one unresettable copy that determines the target $P$ of its **initial** state with certainty. Boolean conjunction asks for $\mathsf{Acc}(P\land Q)$. Joint recovery asks for $\mathsf{Acc}((P,Q))$. A protocol recovering the pair can compute its conjunction by postprocessing. A Boolean conjunction returns just one bit, including when the pair has three or four possible values.

**CM-1. Literal conjunction fails with the minimum of three initial states.** Take Astra's inherited three-state example with source $S=\{A,B,C\}$. Instrument $a$ flags $A$, instrument $b$ flags $B$, and either sends all states to $A$. The separate targets $P=1_{S\setminus\{A\}}$ and $Q=1_{S\setminus\{B\}}$ are accessible by complementing the corresponding readout. Their conjunction is $1_{\{C\}}$. First querying $a$ merges $B,C$ on output zero and first querying $b$ merges $A,C$ on output zero. The current states also agree, so every subsequent transcript agrees. The conjunction is inaccessible. On a source of size at most two, any nonconstant accessible Boolean target determines the full initial state. If both targets are constant, their conjunction is constant. This proves minimality in the number of possible initial states. The original three-state carrier and its inaccessible $1_C$ target predate both forges. [Carrier-relative targets §3](../../../docs/CARRIER_RELATIVE_TARGETS.md) · [Astra Book III](../../../docs/principia_observatoris/03_knowledge_proof.md)

**CM-2. Every Boolean question can be accessible while the full class is inaccessible.** Extend CM-1 by a third probe flagging $C$, with the same reset to $A$. Every subset of three elements is empty, full, a singleton or the complement of a singleton. Thus all eight Boolean targets are accessible. Every first probe still merges two distinct initial states irrevocably, so the class-identity target is inaccessible. For at most two possible initial states, one singleton question already determines the state. The three-state construction is therefore sharp even counting *all* carrier states. Fable FO-10d uses the same flagging construction with a separate reset state $D$ and $S=\{A,B,C\}$. Its example establishes joint failure while all Boolean conjunctions there are accessible. The literal-conjunction counterexample is CM-1. [FO-10(d)](../principia_observatoris_second_forge/agents/OPUS_I_FINITE_OBSERVER.md) · [Fable C-21 and C-23](../principia_observatoris_second_forge/COUNTERMODELS.md)

The distinction also resolves the inherited `free_observability` docstring. If “every target” includes a class-injective scalar coding or the class identity itself, existence of an identifying policy follows by choosing that target. If it means every Boolean target with its own policy, CM-2 refutes the implication. The implementation computes the joint class by tracking the original label and current class. [Second Forge finite-observer code](../../../observational_succ/observatoris_finite_observer.py)

**CM-3. Sharp copy bound for pooling binary answers (paper proof).** Let $P:S\to Y$ have a finite image of size $k\ge1$. For every Boolean function $b:P(S)\to\{0,1\}$ suppose there is a legal one-copy protocol recovering $b(P(x))$ for each possible original $x$. Assume fresh, separately operable preparations with that **same original** $x$, retention of all earlier answers and access to the prescribed protocols for each copy. Then $\lceil\log_2 k\rceil$ copies suffice to identify $P(x)$. This counts copies. Protocol depths and total action cost depend on the Boolean protocols supplied.

*Proof.* For $k=1$ answer immediately. Otherwise inject $P(S)$ into binary strings of length $m=\lceil\log_2 k\rceil$. Apply the one-copy protocol for each coordinate to its own fresh copy. The $m$ retained bits decode the target. For sharpness take $k$ initial states labelled $0,\ldots,k-1$. Supply an action for each subset that reports membership and resets that copy to state zero. After the first action on a copy its subsequent responses are deterministic functions of the chosen actions and state zero. For a fixed deterministic policy, only the first query on each copy can branch on $x$. With at most $c$ copies its informative binary decision tree has at most $2^c$ leaves, even with interleaving and adaptive subset choices. Fewer than $m$ copies leave two of the $k$ initial states in one leaf. The displayed upper and lower bounds agree. An authenticated reset to the same initial state supplies the same result with $m$ runs and $m-1$ resets for $k>1$. Independent draws from an unknown source distribution do not supply copies of an unknown common state. ∎

Only $m$ accessible separating Boolean targets are needed for the upper bound. The stipulated availability of *every* Boolean cut gives one simple universal resource guarantee. The k-state reset family sharpens Fable's three-label handoff question and realizes its obstruction at every finite cardinality. The counting argument and binary encoding are classical.

## 4. Selected-start exact refinement on the reachable subgraph

**CM-13. The weighted conjecture in the Second Forge handoff is false on a four-state unit-cost chain.** Fix a source start $s$ and restrict the source to its reachable states. Call a partition $p$ *exact from $s$* if, for every block $B$, the shortest cost from $p(s)$ to $B$ in the existential quotient equals the least source cost from $s$ to a member of $B$. A coarsest exact refinement of $j$ would be an exact refinement coarser than **every** other exact refinement of $j$. The Second Forge already refuted this globally and asked whether restricting to the reachable source repaired it. [Handoff §9](../principia_observatoris_second_forge/TENACIOUS_D_HANDOFF.md) · [Fable C-13](../principia_observatoris_second_forge/COUNTERMODELS.md)

Take the directed chain $0\to1\to3\to2$ with edges of cost one, rooted at $0$. The source distances in chain order are $0,1,2,3$, so all four states are reachable. Compare the partitions
$$
p=\{0,2\}\mid\{1\}\mid\{3\},\qquad
r=\{0\}\mid\{1,2\}\mid\{3\},\qquad
j=\{0,1,2\}\mid\{3\}.
$$

For $p$ the source block minima, in the displayed order, are $(0,1,2)$. Its quotient is a three-cycle with those same first-arrival costs. For $r$ the source minima are also $(0,1,2)$ and its quotient is a path entering a two-cycle, with the same costs. Thus $p$ and $r$ are exact from $0$. Their join in the partition lattice is $j$. The quotient of $j$ goes from the initial block to $\{3\}$ in **one** step, using the source edge $1\to3$. The only source route from $0$ to $3$ takes **two** steps. Consequently $j$ is inexact. Any common coarsening of $p,r$ which refines $j$ equals $j$, so $j$ admits no coarsest exact refinement. The proof uses a finite acyclic source with unit weights.

Four states are minimal in this cost model. With at most three states, a proper nontrivial partition admits only itself and the discrete partition as refinements. The discrete partition is always exact. The indiscrete partition is exact at the start, as its sole block has distance zero both upstairs and downstairs. These possibilities exhaust the partitions with at most three states. The Boolean endpoint version on the restricted reachable graph behaves differently: all report blocks have a source witness from the root, so the original partition is always exact at that root. The all-start join theorem for a fixed quantale in Fable Geometer CG-10 uses a stronger premise.

**Exact finite decision criterion.** On a finite directed graph of nonnegative edge costs, restricted to states reachable from $s$ by finite-cost paths, let $d(v)$ be the source distance from $s$ and $\ell_p(B)=\min_{v\in B}d(v)$. The partition $p$ is exact at $s$ if and only if
$$
\ell_p(p(v))\le \ell_p(p(u))+c(u,v)
$$
for every source edge $u\to v$. If exactness holds, take a cheapest quotient route to $p(u)$ and append the edge, giving the inequality. Conversely the inequalities along a quotient route telescope from $\ell_p(p(s))=0$ to show its cost is at least $\ell_p$ of its end block. Projection of source paths gives the reverse bound. In particular, refining a partition by the exact values $d(v)$ produces an exact refinement, because the inequalities reduce to those for the source distances. To decide whether a **greatest** exact candidate below a given $j$ exists in coarseness order, enumerate its finitely many refinements, retain those passing the edge test, join them and test the join. A passing join is the desired candidate. If it fails, any greatest candidate would have had to equal that join. This construction claims finite decidability without a polynomial-time guarantee. The edge test is the cost instance of QG-3's selected-start potential, whereas the sharp four-state example resolves the reachable-source conjecture.
