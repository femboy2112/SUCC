**FH-9 proof correction.** For two scalar word laws of sizes $n,m$, set $N=n+m$, $r=(\alpha_1,-\alpha_2)$ and let $V_j$ span $rA_w$ through length $j$ for block-diagonal letter matrices $A_a$. With output column $b$ and first distinguishing word length $k_0>0$, it is $V_{k_0-1}\subseteq b^\perp$. The detecting row at $k_0$ rules out $V_\infty\subseteq b^\perp$. For a reactive acceptor, each letter fixes the all-ones column $e$ and $re=0$, giving $V_\infty\subseteq e^\perp$. Starting at dimension one, the span then stabilizes by length $N-2$. Arbitrary weighted and generative models have the $N-1$ bound; a generative model fixes total mass only after summing the letter operators. This repairs the containment in the inherited proof while preserving its normalized bounds. [FH-9 and its classical context](PRIMARY_LITERATURE.md)

**QG-7 origin resolved.** Let $C=\mathrm{Cl}_R$ and $S=q^*q_!$, with composition acting from right to left. Preservation by $S$ of the $C$-closed predicates is $CSC=SC$. In the forward-completeness convention $f\rho=\rho f\rho$, this is precisely $\rho=C$, $f=S$. Applying $S$ yields $(SC)^2=SC$. Conversely, extensivity gives $SC\le CSC\le SCSC$, so idempotence of $SC$ recovers the criterion. Equality in a preorder means mutual comparison. This settles the historical attribution question at the stated operator types. QG-3/QG-10 supply the separate graph-theoretic characterization. ## Noncommuting word test and a finite universal probe

Fix a labelled graph, a quotient map $q$, and a root $x$. Write $L_x(B)$ for the set of words on source paths from $x$ to the block $B$, and $\bar L_{q(x)}(B)$ for the existential quotient path language with the same endpoints. Word weights here depend only on the chosen labels. If preserving an output, cost or source-event attribute matters, include it in the letter and keep initial reports separately typed. For a word $ab$, the product of language weights is the singleton $\{ab\}$. Chronological evolution on column states instead has matrix $T_bT_a$. The two conventions agree when their evaluation composes in the same chronological order.

**Finite separation lemma (paper).** Equality $L_x(B)=\bar L_{q(x)}(B)$ is equivalent to equality of their joined path values under every singleton-letter valuation into the powerset quantale of a **finite monoid**. The assertion also holds for the two languages obtained by pooling the initial representatives on both sides, but CM-4 separates that weaker statement from the rooted one.

*Proof.* A letter valuation extends multiplicatively to a word. Joined path weights are its direct image of the language, so equal languages have equal evaluations. Conversely suppose a word $w$ belongs to exactly one language, of length $k$. Make a finite deterministic automaton for the singleton language $\{w\}$, with one state for each prefix length $0,\ldots,k$, and a dead state. Each step matching the next letter advances the prefix counter. Every wrong letter and every letter after completion leads to the dead state. For $k=0$ the initial state is already the only accepting state. Each letter acts by a transformation of this finite state set. Its finite transformation monoid has chronological multiplication $f\cdot g=g\circ f$. Assign each letter its singleton transformation. A set of transformations in the joined path value contains an element carrying prefix state $0$ to $k$ exactly when the original language contains $w$. The two evaluated values differ. ∎

For a fixed missing word, this observer has $k+2$ automaton states; its transformation monoid has at most $(k+2)^{k+2}$ elements. The family can depend on the missing word. A single commutative cost valuation, a single bounded word prefix, or a pooled endpoint oracle does not by itself recover the same invariant. Fable Geometer CG-6 and its S-7 review already connect a degree-one letter grading to one-step stability; the finite test above explains exactly which retained labels and endpoint quantifiers matter when that connection is used for Astra PO-Q1. ## Primary mathematical neighbours

- Giacobazzi, Ranzato and Scozzari distinguish completeness for an operation from completeness at a fixed point. Their constructions concern abstract domains and hypotheses on their operators. They explain a classical setting for the distinction between action descent, path closure and complete refinements. The selected-start order obstruction CM-13 has its own explicit finite proof. [Making Abstract Interpretations Complete, JACM 47 (2000)](https://www.sci.unich.it/~scozzari/paper/JACM00.pdf)
- Kiefer, Murawski, Ouaknine, Wachter and Worrell prove in Proposition 3.1 that a nonzero $n$-state field-weighted automaton has a word witness of length at most $n-1$, by spanning its forward space. This is an antecedent of Astra H2/H3 and Fable FH-9. The coefficients describe an exact word law, so this result does not price collecting noisy samples. [On the Complexity of Equivalence and Minimisation for Q-Weighted Automata, §3](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/james.worrell/fossacs12-journal.pdf)
- Aaronson and Gottesman develop classical simulation algorithms for stabilizer circuits. The restricted four-ray one-qubit simulation in CA-6 sits in this established family. The statements involving an arbitrary chosen commutative observable algebra and positivity of each instrument branch require their own hypotheses and proof. [Improved Simulation of Stabilizer Circuits](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0406196)

These references establish antecedents for the specified claims. The new comparison, strict examples and finite proofs carry no assertion of scholarly priority.
