# Classical antecedents and precise SUCC specializations

This register compares the manuscript in [Astra Book IV](../../../docs/principia_observatoris/04_horizons_completion.md), [Fable's horizon forge](../principia_observatoris_second_forge/agents/OPUS_III_HORIZON_HUNTER.md) and the [weighted quotient formalization](../../../formal/ObservatorisQuantale.lean) with published work. A proof in SUCC establishes its stated implication. Priority requires a separate historical search. Source access and theorem-level attributions below are as checked on 2026-11-14.

## What a future experiment distinguishes

**Moore.** E. F. Moore, *Gedanken-experiments on sequential machines* (1956), Theorems 1, 6 and the disjoint-machine comparison following Theorem 6. Moore exhibits pairwise distinguishable states without one simple experiment identifying every initial state on one copy. His partition at experiment length one groups states by their present output. Refinement adds a common input followed by a shorter distinguishing experiment. His length counts output slots, including the initial one. In FH-8 the empty word already reads the present output, so Moore's bound of $N-1$ slots is $N-2$ input letters for state-output machines. For two machines take their disjoint union with $N=n+m$. Even when some states are equivalent, the initial partition has at least two blocks if any pair differs, each strict refinement gains a block, and a plateau is invariant under every letter. Mealy outputs arrive on transitions and give the separate bound of $N-1$ letters. These are pairwise statements. An adaptive experiment selecting different actions on incompatible copies is a separate implementation problem. [Moore, original chapter, particularly pp. 145–148](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~cdm/resources/Moore1956-gedanken-experiments.pdf)

**Nerode.** A. Nerode's *Linear automaton transformations* (1958), Lemma 2, characterizes the causal transformations of streams over finite alphabets realizable by a finite automaton through finiteness of their intrinsic states. Their number is the least realization size. The article's main theorem addresses linear transformations over a finite commutative ring. For the language version, take words $u,v$ equivalent when every suffix $z$ gives the same membership of $uz,vz$ in the language. Substituting $az$ for $z$ makes this a right congruence, so appending $a$ is a well-defined transition on classes. Conversely, two words reaching one deterministic state agree on every continuation. Hence a finite recognizing machine exists iff the residual classes are finite, and the reachable quotient is minimal. This argument uses the **whole suffix family**. SUCC's fibre-constant target theorem alone only factors a specified target through a readout. A readout of the present state can fail to descend a single labelled successor. The [labelled deterministic bridge](lean/ObserverBridge.lean) supplies the missing condition. The Lemma 2 text was checked in a transcription of the original article. [AMS DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1958-0135681-9) · [Article text](https://www.academia.edu/59181520/Linear_automaton_transformations)

**Fine and Wilf.** A finite word with periods $p,q$ and length at least $p+q-\gcd(p,q)$ has period $\gcd(p,q)$. For two one-sided periodic sequences the common block covers a whole period of each, so agreement extends to their entire futures. FH-7 first discards $T=\max(t_1,t_2)$ transient terms. Thereafter the periods $p_i$ satisfy $t_1+p_1\le n,\ t_2+p_2\le m$, giving $T+p_1+p_2-\gcd(p_1,p_2)\le n+m-1$ observed terms. The chain/cycle sharpness construction is supplied in FH-7. The original 1965 publisher PDF was unavailable. The word theorem and its proof were checked directly in Halava, Harju and Ilie, *Periods and binary words*, Lemma 1. [Fine and Wilf, original article DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1965-0174934-9) · [Primary proof of the word form](https://www.csd.uwo.ca/~ilie/JCTA00.pdf)

## First separating word for weighted and probabilistic machines

Let $n,m\ge1$, $N=n+m$. Both machines share the same finite input alphabet. Write their scalar laws as $f_i(w)=\alpha_i M^i_w\beta_i$, with the identity matrix for the empty word. The horizon in the table counts **letters in the shortest distinguishing word**, equivalently the largest required test length when checking every shorter word too.

| Class | Mass condition | Sharp worst-case length | Reading |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Weighted over a field | No restriction on letter operators | $N-1$ | A word's scalar weight |
| Reactive stochastic acceptor | Initial unit masses and each letter matrix stochastic | $N-2$ | Acceptance probability for a chosen input with a Boolean acceptance vector, or a terminal expectation with general reward |
| Generative finite-state process | Sum of nonnegative letter matrices stochastic, terminal vector all ones | $N-1$ | Probability of each emitted prefix, with at least two symbols for the sharp examples |

**Upper bounds.** Form the row $r=(\alpha_1,-\alpha_2)$, block diagonal operators $A_a$ and stacked output column $b=\operatorname{col}(\beta_1,\beta_2)$. Set $V_j=\mathrm{span}\{rA_w:|w|\le j\}$. A plateau is right invariant under every letter matrix. Starting at dimension one for $r\ne0$, the eventual span $V_\infty$ is reached by length $\dim V_\infty-1$. If any discrepancy exists, multiplication by $b$ detects one among the spanning rows by that length. The zero row yields identical laws. The ambient dimension is $N$. For reactive acceptors the block column of ones $e$ satisfies $A_a e=e$ and $re=0$, so $V_\infty\subseteq e^\perp$ and its dimension is at most $N-1$. The generative normalization only ensures $(\sum_a A_a)e=e$. A single letter can change total mass and the discrepancy measures exactly this mass. Fable FH-9 proof item 4 has a slip in the displayed containment. For shortest distinguishing length $k_0>0$ one has $V_{k_0-1}\subseteq b^\perp$, while generally $V_\infty\not\subseteq b^\perp$. Only $V_\infty\subseteq e^\perp$ is needed in the reactive case.

**Lower bounds.** Binary-output autonomous deterministic machines witnessing FH-7 first differ in state output at index $N-2$. Realizing them as unary deterministic reactive acceptors retains this index as the first separating input length. Making each deterministic generative machine emit its current state output and then step shifts the first separating **prefix length** to $N-1$. The previous emitted prefixes have the same point mass, and a length-$N-1$ prefix has probability one under exactly one law. The weighted bound holds over any field and its sharp unary examples are the recurrence constructions of FH-3 and FH-9. In particular the generative construction also supplies sharp weighted examples over any field when a binary alphabet is allowed. Each assertion compares exact laws on named words. The operational acquisition of their probabilities from samples requires separate error and access hypotheses.

**Primary attribution.** Kiefer, Murawski, Ouaknine, Wachter and Worrell, *On the complexity of equivalence and minimisation for Q-weighted automata*, Proposition 3.1, explicitly give the forward-span and $d-1$ nonzero-witness result over any field in dimension $d$. Applying their difference construction gives $N-1$. Rabin (1963) supplies a reactive stochastic-input model with Boolean acceptance and a cut-point language. FH-9 compares exact acceptance values, a stronger criterion than threshold-language equality. Tzeng's (1992) publisher abstract verifies the polynomial-time equivalence algorithm. Murawski and Ouaknine, discussion following Definition 3, discuss the $N-1$ sufficient bound attributed to Paz, allowing missing mass via a sink which can change the state count. Mateus, Qiu and Li (2012), Theorem 3, explicitly state the sufficient $N-1$ bound for their letter-stochastic acceptors with Boolean terminal vectors. The smaller local bound therefore improves the numerical certificate **as stated in that paper**, including on its own normalization assumptions. We have not verified a sharp $N-2$ priority assertion in Paz or Tzeng. For normalized reactive acceptors the sharper statement follows above from the classical span argument and mass constraint. No external originality attribution follows. [Kiefer et al., Prop. 3.1](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/james.worrell/fossacs12-journal.pdf) · [Rabin, publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0019-9958(63)90290-0) · [Tzeng, publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1137/0221017) · [Murawski and Ouaknine, original paper](https://people.mpi-sws.org/~joel/publications/probprogequiv05.pdf) · [Mateus, Qiu and Li, publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0890540112001149) · [Original text, Theorem 3](https://www.academia.edu/29933393/On_the_complexity_of_minimizing_probabilistic_and_quantum_automata)

## Recurrence identification, realization, and the sampling contract

**Gold.** E. M. Gold, *Language identification in the limit* (1967), defines eventual stabilization to a correct language hypothesis under every allowed presentation of that language. The criterion supplies no observable stopping signal, and text versus informant presentations yield different results. Fable FH-5 specializes the learning distinction to sequential scalar **field values**. Every finite prefix has two different eventually zero extensions, obtained by prescribing different next terms and zeroing the subsequent tail. Each extension has finite recurrence order because a sufficiently high power of the shift annihilates it. Thus a globally sound certifier of complete sequences on the union of all finite recurrence orders can halt on no prefix. This is a direct local proof with a different target and data protocol from Gold's language classes. [Gold, original article, pp. 447–449](https://home.uni-leipzig.de/gkobele/courses/2023.WS/Colloq/files/Gold67.pdf)

**Berlekamp and Massey.** E. R. Berlekamp's *Algebraic Coding Theory* (1968) is the decoding antecedent. J. L. Massey's *Shift-register synthesis and BCH decoding* (1969) gives the LFSR interpretation and Theorem 3 on the shortest register for a given prefix. If the prefix length is $s$ and its minimal register length $L$ has $2L\le s$, its minimal connection polynomial is unique. Under a global order promise $d$, two candidate continuations are annihilated in difference by a product polynomial of degree at most $2d$. Their first $2d$ terms therefore determine equality. Thus the minimal-prefix learner eventually gives the true continuation after $2d$ observations of an order-$d$ source, although in the unbounded class a higher-order continuation remains compatible at every finite stage. An executable learner requires effective exact field arithmetic and equality. The 1968 book was checked bibliographically only. Massey's article and Theorem 3 were inspected directly. [Berlekamp, IEEE bibliographic record](https://www.itsoc.org/publications/papers/algebraic-coding-theory) · [Massey, original article](https://crypto.stanford.edu/~mironov/cs359/massey.pdf)

**Ho and Kalman.** B. L. Ho and R. E. Kalman (1966) construct minimal finite-dimensional linear state realizations from Markov parameters. In FH-4, $h_k=CA^kB$ factors a block Hankel matrix into stacked $CA^i$ times concatenated $A^j B$. A rank-$r$ observed minor forces dimension at least $r$. The standard infinite Hankel construction realizes the shifts of its columns on their span when that span has finite dimension. An upper bound on the rank of a single observed block supplies no upper bound on the infinite span. Indeed the impulse at index $h$ agrees with zero on the first $h$ terms, while its leading $(h+1)$-square Hankel matrix is a reversed identity. These are exact Markov responses with a fixed clock and initialization. A separate direct feedthrough term $D$ must be compared if the output model includes it. NASA's publications record provides the original citation and description. A full copy of the 1966 article was unavailable here, so the algebraic implications in this paragraph are derived locally. [Ho and Kalman, NASA record](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19670049337) · [Journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1524/auto.1966.14.112.545)

**What a single executed input can establish.** J. C. Willems, P. Rapisarda, I. Markovsky and B. L. M. De Moor (2005) give a different acquisition theorem. For a controllable discrete-time LTI behavior of state dimension $n$, one exact input/output trajectory whose input is persistently exciting of order $L+n$ has length-$L$ windows spanning the entire length-$L$ behavior. Persistent excitation means full rank of the corresponding input Hankel matrix. This is a sufficient condition on the **executed input and model class**. The switched full-word comparison of Astra Book IV instead quantifies over every word up to a dimension bound, or calculates a basis from supplied operators. One arbitrary trajectory need satisfy neither protocol. The all-zero input and zero initial state of a linear system yields the same zero record for every input gain. [Willems et al., Theorem 1](https://homes.esat.kuleuven.be/~sistawww/smc/jwillems/Articles/JournalArticles/2005.2.pdf)

## Quantales, Lawvere distance, and exact abstraction

**Value types and geometry.** K. I. Rosenthal (1990), Definition 1, presents a quantale as a complete lattice with associative multiplication preserving arbitrary joins in each argument. A unital quantale adds a two-sided unit. F. W. Lawvere (1973) interprets generalized metric spaces as categories enriched over the reversed order on the extended nonnegative reals, with addition as tensor and zero as unit. The identity and composition inequalities are $d(x,x)=0$ and $d(x,z)\le d(x,y)+d(y,z)$. Symmetry, separation, and finiteness require further assumptions. Assigning edge costs then taking the infimum of finite path costs gives one such distance. The choice of cost composition and measurement units is an input to the construction. Arbitrary signed interference does not obey a join-of-paths rule. [Rosenthal, original article, Definition 1](https://www.numdam.org/item/CTGDC_1990__31_1_3_0.pdf) · [Lawvere, original article, 1973](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mattearnshaw/lawvere/master/pdfs/1973-metric-spaces-generalized-logic-closed-categories.pdf)

Fable's Lean structure uses a preorder and takes equivalence to mean mutual comparison. It assumes associativity and a right unit up to order equivalence, and only $x\le 1\otimes x$ on the left. Thus every unital quantale provides data, while a generally defined left multiplication by the designated unit need not be equivalent to the identity. The QG-3/QG-10 characterization of exact endpoint values after a particular observation map is additional content about a graph, its report fibres, and closure. Lawvere's enrichment alone does not assert endpoint exactness, prescribe an observer, or identify a physical cost. [Lean definitions and quotient results](../../../formal/ObservatorisQuantale.lean) · [Compiled model and axiom inventory](lean/README.md)

**Cousot and Cousot, completeness and QG-7.** P. Cousot and R. Cousot (1977) compare fixpoint semantics under ordered abstractions and prove sound approximations. Giacobazzi, Ranzato and Scozzari (2000), §1.2, distinguish sound approximation from the operation-specific identity $\alpha f=f^\sharp\alpha$, and also distinguish exactness at a least fixpoint. For the unary closure-operator convention, Ranzato and Tapparo give **forward completeness** of $\rho$ for $f$ as $f\rho=\rho f\rho$. This last identity has a concrete specialization here. [Cousot and Cousot, original POPL paper](https://www.di.ens.fr/~cousot/COUSOTpapers/publications.www/CousotCousot-POPL-77-ACM-p238--252-1977.pdf) · [Giacobazzi, Ranzato and Scozzari, JACM](https://www.sci.unich.it/~scozzari/paper/JACM00.pdf) · [Ranzato and Tapparo, journal article](https://doi.org/10.1093/logcom/exl035)

Let $C=\mathrm{Cl}_R$ be source closure on potentials and $S=q^*q_!$ fibre saturation on the same ordered domain. In a preorder the displayed equalities are mutual inequalities. The fixed points of $C$ are the source-closed potentials. Preservation of those fixed points by $S$ is precisely $CSC=SC$, forward completeness with **$\rho=C, f=S$**. Indeed evaluate the fixed-point criterion at $C\phi$ and use idempotence, or substitute a closed $\phi$ to recover preservation. It follows that $SCSC=SC$ by applying $S$. Conversely, $SC\le CSC\le SCSC$ by extensivity, so idempotence of $SC$ implies the same fixed-point criterion. This proves the correspondence left as a question in Fable QG-7, after taking the order-equivalence quotient if necessary. QG-3 then equates it to exactness at **all** source starts, and QG-10 equates it to edge realization from every representative. This identifies precisely which abstract domain and semantic operation occur in the established notion. Swapping $C,S$, claiming all labelled histories lift, or claiming that a given instrument realizes $q$ needs other evidence. ## Scope of attribution

Moore refinement, residual-state minimization, periodic-word uniqueness, finite LFSR synthesis, finite-dimensional spans, persistently exciting input, Lawvere enrichment and completeness of abstraction have established antecedents. The normalized stochastic bound, the explicit SUCC access contracts, their sharp witnesses and the typed QG-7 specialization have derivations under the local conventions above. Identifying the first publication of the sharp normalized bound remains open in this audit.
