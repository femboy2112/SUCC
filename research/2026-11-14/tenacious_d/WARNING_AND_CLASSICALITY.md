## D3. An exact finite deadline test

A warning is actionable if an observer can select a **timely sufficient action** in every source still compatible with the same actual record. Identification of a cause and existence of such an action are different questions.

Let a finite set of original sources be Ω and a finite set of current states be X. A decision clock has integer time t≤D. Include phase, instrument state, actuator queues, irreversible losses and pending observations in X when relevant. Let E be the interpreted record available at decision time. In a deterministic experiment, its nonempty fibers define sets of joined pairs

\[
B_h=\{(s,x_t(s)):s\in\Omega,\ E(s)=h\}\subseteq\Omega\times X.
\]

With exogenous nondeterminism use every compatible joined pair. Keep each original source tied to its own possible current states. An independent Cartesian product of original and current marginals can admit impossible pairs. For each joint pair and t let \(I_t(s,x)\) be the finite set of allowed terminal commands whose execution from x satisfies the specified safety criterion on source s by D. Derive this table from the source law and intervention semantics. A command whose effect arrives late is excluded. Equality at the deadline is allowed here when the target is checked **after** that arrival. For a strict deadline replace ≤D by <D. For a no-breach-ever objective the execution must satisfy safety at every relevant prefix.

**TD-W1 (one decision, PROVED PAPER).** An observation-based terminal selector succeeds on every reachable case exactly when

\[
\forall h\quad \bigcap_{(s,x)\in B_h} I_t(s,x)\ne\varnothing.
\]

Indeed the selector issues the same command in every case in its fiber, proving necessity. Choose a member of each nonempty intersection for sufficiency. Finite tables make this selection effective, including its worst-case resource bound once execution costs are supplied. With unique safe commands this reduces to Astra PO-K1/K2. With overlapping command sets it is strictly more permissive than identification. A common neutralizer can save two indistinguishable sources. Conversely, with three safe sets {0,1}, {1,2}, {2,0}, every pair admits a common response while the joint fiber admits none. Source-provided warning lights can therefore agree on pairwise test cases and still fail a three-case obligation. If current values are damaged by a probe, retaining only current values can give an erroneous success for a target about the original source.

**TD-W2 (sequential interception on one copy, PROVED PAPER).** Let each allowed preterminal operation a have known positive integer duration \(\ell(a)\), finite reported output o, and a finite nonempty successor relation \((x',o)\in L_{s,t,a}(x)\) whenever legal. Legal actions at an information state must be legal on **every** joined pair. All observations and intermediate irreversible failures during the operation must be included in its outcome/current state or in its legality. With a black-box operation, no intermediate feedback is available. With observable intermediate events, split the operation at the decision epochs, recording any pending actuator command. Define

\[
B_{a,o}=\{(s,x'):\exists (s,x)\in B,\ (x',o)\in L_{s,t,a}(x)\}.
\]

For nonempty B define W(t,B) recursively. It is true when the W1 intersection is nonempty, or when a common legal operation a satisfies \(t+\ell(a)\le D\) and W(t+ℓ(a),B_{a,o}) holds for every nonempty child. **W(t,B) is true exactly when a record-driven policy guarantees interception by D on all executions compatible with B.** Proof by induction on D−t. Every policy either terminates with a common command or has an initial operation with winning continuations on all possible reports. Conversely one may paste those child policies behind the corresponding observed outputs. At D only a timely terminal decision can succeed. The induction terminates because preterminal durations are positive. For finite Ω,X, actions and D this is a finite algorithm on at most \((D+1)(2^{|\Omega||X|}-1)\) nonempty information states. Its certificate is a policy tree or, at a losing node, an unsafe witness for each terminal command and a losing possible report for every legal operation. This is finite strong planning with partial observation. Original/current pairing is inherited from SUCC. The extension here makes interception and latency explicit. A finite prior means a finite **set of possibilities** here. No average-case guarantee or prior probabilities enter this theorem.

**Timing controls.** Take a passive binary sensor available at t=1, hidden cause h∈{0,1}, and a target first threatened at D=2. Only terminal command h prevents loss. With actuator latency 1 both branches are salvageable. Increasing latency to 2 leaves the same warning and jeopardizes both branches. Before observing, the two commands have disjoint safe fibers. If reading the sensor at t=1 also disables the actuator, the same accurate report leaves no successful command. Thus a positive warning margin alone fails. If each of four sources requires its own command and two binary observations have unit duration, two bits suffice when the two readings are compatible and enough actuation time remains. One bit cannot separate four distinct commands. Generally, a policy tree of at most b binary outcome bits has at most 2^b leaves when actions are deterministic functions of the available transcript and termination is a leaf. A count of 2^b available leaves need not give an executable tree. Hidden outcome latency, variable response time and observable refusals count as channels whenever the policy can use them.

**Observer feedback.** For an explicit synchronous source with sensor read at time t and overrides applied to the next state, unroll the **joint** source/controller into a directed acyclic graph indexed by event time. Include sensor-to-memory reads, memory-to-command updates, actuator queues, and their actual causal ordering. If two initial preparations differ only at H and share the same initial controller and independent random seed (fixed across both runs), every differing value is reachable from H by a directed dependency path in this unrolled graph. Proof by induction in a topological order: a node with identical parents and identical deterministic transition takes identical values. This also works for an asynchronous schedule specified in the unrolling. If the schedule depends on the source, expose its scheduling decisions and dependencies as nodes. Unmodeled scheduler timing defeats a cone inference. The implication is one-way because signs or gates can suppress a path. The prior HQ-3 countermodel has plant edges x→s and a→y with the lawful policy a←s. The unrolled path x(0)→s(1)→a(2)→y(3) explains the difference with no missing plant edge. A cone violation witnesses a mismatch in the **supplied whole model**, which could concern a missing feedback wire, incorrect timing, a failed single-site preparation promise, or a hidden plant connection. Deducing the last alternative requires independently ruling out the others.

### Passive sensing with one correction pulse

**TD-W3 (linear warning and interception, PROVED PAPER).** Fix a field K, an initial prior equal to the whole finite-dimensional vector space \(K^n\), rational data if an exact algorithm is desired, synchronous passive dynamics \(z_{k+1}=Az_k\) before action, and exact sensor readouts \(Cz_k\) through epoch t. Write \(O_t=\operatorname{col}(C,CA,\ldots,CA^t)\). Let an unrestricted correction pulse u be issued at t and added as Bu after the ordinary state update at epoch \(\tau=t+\ell\), with ℓ≥1 and τ≤D. The target is a **designated terminal equality** at D, after the pulse has propagated. Then

\[
y_D=Lz_0+Gu,\qquad L=FA^D,\quad G=FA^{D-\tau}B.
\]

There exists a controller \(u=f(O_tz_0)\), allowing even nonlinear f, with y_D=0 for every z_0 exactly when

\[
\ker O_t\subseteq\ker L\quad\text{and}\quad \operatorname{im}L\subseteq\operatorname{im}G.
\]

Moreover a linear controller always suffices. If z_0 and z_0' have the same warning they receive the same u, so subtraction gives the kernel inclusion. Every Lz_0 is canceled by Gu, hence the image inclusion. Conversely L factors as \(\bar L O_t\) on im O_t. Choose a linear right inverse of G on im G by lifting a basis. Set \(f=-G^{\rm right}\bar L\) there and extend linearly to the whole readout space. This proves both directions and yields a Gaussian elimination certificate over the rationals. If τ>D set G=0, and exact correction then requires L=0. Quantized sensors, bounded commands, a finite source prior, safety before D, and feedback during the designated passive interval change the theorem. For a finite prior use W1 with the actual quantized fibers and safe command sets. For a first-effect deadline verify independently that the uncontrolled target was safe at all preceding times, and that the intervened trajectory remains safe for the declared objective.

**Exact latency example.** Use the shift \(Ae_1=e_2, Ae_2=e_3,Ae_3=0\), initial subspace \(z_0=h e_1\), C=e₂ᵀ, F=e₃ᵀ, D=2, t=1. Parametrize the prior by scalar h in K. The warning trajectory is (0,h) and the passive terminal target is h. With ℓ=1, B=e₃ and u=−h, compensation at time 2 is exact. Holding those two times fixed but changing B to e₂ gives G=0 and failure. Holding B=e₃ fixed while delaying arrival to time 3 also gives failure. Reading only C=e₃ᵀ through t=1 gives O_t=0 on the initial subspace and failure despite full actuator authority. The sensor test and actuator test can therefore fail separately.

## D5. Three meanings of classical prediction

Astra PO-C1 treats an affine state compression \(q(\rho)_i=\operatorname{tr}(\rho e_i)\) on **all** density states for a fixed commutative algebra D. It gives a classical instrument quotient exactly when each outcome adjoint sends D into D. Fable CA-5 has a deterministic bit representation from a computational basis preparation. CA-6 fixes the initial state |0⟩ and uses the four rays reachable under X, H and selective projective Z. Preparing an arbitrary probability distribution over those four rays extends the construction to their mixtures. Nonselective preservation, individual outcome preservation, preservation of coarse state, and preservation of the entire microscopic quantum state refer to different maps.

Write x=tr(ρX), z=tr(ρZ) for qubit Pauli expectations. Under the Pauli-X unitary they evolve as (x,−z), and Hadamard H sends them to (z,x). A selective Z observation with outcome v∈{−1,+1} has probability (1+vz)/2 and positive-probability posterior (0,v). These laws depend on x,z only, including for a qubit with a nonzero imaginary coherence. Independent controller randomness is fixed before comparing adaptive protocols and then averaged.

**TD-C1 (four-state simulator with optimal state count, PROVED PAPER).** For every initial density state, all finite adaptive transcripts in {X,H,selective Z} have a common classical simulator with four hidden states (u,v)∈{−1,+1}². The preparation encoding may depend nonlinearly on the state. Use

\[
p_{u,v}(\rho)=\tfrac14(1+ux)(1+vz).
\]

X flips v, H interchanges u and v, and Z reads v and refreshes u by an independent fair bit. The resulting first moments are exactly x,z. After X and H they obey the two transformations above, and after a Z event they reset to (0,v). Induction on each positive-probability history proves equality of every adaptive transcript law. Zero-probability branches have zero mass in both laws. For {X,Z} alone the single sign v suffices, agreeing with CA-5 on the basis states. The construction uses the known preparation data to set p. Arbitrary preparation mixtures need not be represented by the same mixture of hidden distributions under this encoding. Even after a Z measurement has destroyed some coherence the future comparison still depends on the chosen operations.

Four hidden states are necessary for any fixed finite-state classical simulator allowing arbitrary preparation distributions even just for the four X and Z eigenstates. Each hidden state λ yields a point \((r_X(\lambda),r_Z(\lambda))\) in the square [−1,1]² from its expected ±1 outputs under H→Z and Z. All preparations give points in their convex hull. The four eigenpreparations require the four vertices (±1,0),(0,±1) of a diamond of area 2. Three points in the square have triangular hull of area at most 2: the absolute determinant giving twice the area is separately convex in each coordinate, so its maximum occurs at square vertices, where it is 4. Any triangle containing the whole diamond would therefore have the same area and equal the diamond, impossible. For compact convex polygons with interior, strict inclusion adds positive area. The lower bound also applies to preparation-independent randomized update kernels by averaging their responses. It does not charge extra hidden memory stored outside the stated finite-state simulator: such memory must be included in λ.

**TD-C2 (maximal affine real preparation domain, PROVED PAPER).** Fix a **convex real-qubit** preparation class containing the four eigenstates above. A finite classical simulator with a positive affine preparation encoding and with fixed response kernels for all {X,H,Z} transcripts exists exactly when the class is the stabilizer diamond \(|x|+|z|\le1\). Equivalently, among admissible classes containing that diamond none extends beyond it. Necessity already follows from the two readout choices H→Z and Z.

For proof, if r_u(λ),s_v(λ) are their outcome probabilities, define the synthetic joint law \(J_{u,v}(\rho)=\sum_\lambda p_\lambda(\rho) r_u(\lambda)s_v(\lambda)\). This is a mathematical coupling of two counterfactual response functions. No joint quantum measurement was performed. J is positive and affine in the preparation and has the exact two marginals. Its correlation c=ΣuvJ_{u,v} is affine in x,z on the real affine plane. At each of the four axis eigenstates one outcome is deterministic and the other fair, so c vanishes there. Three noncollinear such points force c=0 throughout the convex class (which has nonempty interior). Binary Fourier inversion gives

\[
J_{u,v}=\tfrac14(1+ux+vz+uvc)=\tfrac14(1+ux+vz).
\]

Nonnegativity for all signs forces |x|+|z|≤1. Conversely these same four entries define an affine encoding on that diamond. X and H permute the hidden signs as above, and each Z branch resets the first sign. The ray updates and inductive proof of TD-C1 apply. The state \((x,z)=(3/5,4/5)\) is a legitimate pure real qubit and would force \(J_{-,-}=-1/10\). It therefore refutes a positive affine extension while retaining the nonlinear simulator. For all complex qubit states the real slice already obstructs an affine full-state extension. No maximal-domain statement is made for lower-dimensional preparations or general three-dimensional convex subsets. This result is compatible with PO-C1, which prescribes q and D in advance: H sends Z to X outside the fixed diagonal algebra. On the diamond it is the **preparation domain** and its four classical coordinates that allow the alternative representation.

**TD-C3 (exact error price of affine preparation for two sharp readouts, CLASSICAL compatibility mathematics / PAPER).** Use the full qubit state space, a finite positive affine preparation encoding, and two fixed binary response kernels intended to simulate Z and H→Z. The smallest simultaneous worst-state total variation error is

\[
\epsilon_*=(1-1/\sqrt2)/2.
\]

The lower bound applies to any sequential simulator containing these choices. The attained upper bound here concerns those two readouts alone. In finite dimensions a positive affine map on all density states into a finite simplex has the form p_λ(ρ)=tr(ρF_λ) with F_λ≥0 and ΣF_λ=I: extend each affine functional from the trace-one hyperplane by homogeneity on the positive cone, then by differences to Hermitian matrices and use trace duality. The synthetic joint laws thus give positive effects G_{uv} summing to I. Write S=Σu G_{uv} and T=Σv G_{uv}, with both sums over all sign pairs. Uniform binary TV error ε implies \(\|X-S\|_{op},\|Z-T\|_{op}\le2\epsilon\) by separation by density states. Set \(\eta=2\epsilon\). Since X and Z have trace norm 2,

\[
4-4\eta\le\operatorname{tr}(XS+ZT)
=\sum_{u,v}\operatorname{tr}[(uX+vZ)G_{uv}]
\le\sqrt2\sum_{u,v}\operatorname{tr}(G_{uv})=2\sqrt2.
\]

The last bound uses \((uX+vZ)^2=2I\). Thus η≥1−1/√2. The POVM \(G_{uv}=(I+(uX+vZ)/\sqrt2)/4\) is positive, sums to I, and has the two noisy sign marginals X/√2,Z/√2. Reading one sign gives error exactly ε* on the corresponding eigenstates. This proves sharpness. The boundary between jointly measurable approximate spin observables is established physics mathematics. A primary comparison is [Heinosaari, Stano and Reitzner (2008)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.2712); the exact sharp-observable joint-measurability criterion is in [Heinosaari, Reitzner and Stano (2008), Proposition 8](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.0783). The direct proof here fixes the metric, quantifiers and operational scope of the transfer. No priority claim.

**Free observation versus closure.** CA-1 assumes every allowed deterministic operation preserves complete behavioral equivalence of the current state. PO-C3 instead compares the nonselective sum of one observation instrument with the identity on a declared target algebra while future controls are separately specified. On a bit, an ordinary flip is closed on the classical state space but changes its behavior relative to a future bit read. In contrast, a Z measurement ignored on a qubit fixes the diagonal state and all later Z/X-flip statistics, yet can change the later H→Z law (Q₊ goes to I/2). The profile entry marking {X,Z} “free” in CA-3 uses passive measurement of the basis label. It cannot literally satisfy CA-1 for **all** operations because the Pauli-X actuator flips the bit. Designating only the Z measurement as a probe certifies free readout on basis states. Applying CA-1 to the entire action family requires the bit flip itself to preserve behavioral equivalence, which it fails for the bit-readout target. A useful model can therefore have exact compositional predictions with detectable state changes. For the same averaged channel and the same algebra, pointwise fixation implies invariance. An ordinary bit flip disproves the reverse implication. For selective experiments, recording the outcome adds the separate componentwise closure obligation in PO-C1.
