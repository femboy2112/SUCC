## IC-01. Integer characters classify the remaining gauges

Let the candidate phases be \(z_j=e(x_j)\), with
\(x\in\mathbb T^N=(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^N\). A declared integer
monomial probe has exponent row \(a\in\mathbb Z^N\) and reports
\(\chi_a(x)=e(a\cdot x)\). Let \(L\) be the integer lattice
generated by the measured rows.

**DISCLOSED on this full candidate torus.** Two phase vectors give all
the same measured characters iff their difference belongs to

\[
L^\perp=\{\delta\in\mathbb T^N:
             a\cdot\delta\in\mathbb Z\text{ for every }a\in L\}.
\tag{IC1}
\]

A target character \(\chi_b\) is determined by those reports iff
\(b\in L\). The forward implication uses the same integer combination
of observed characters. For the converse, put the integer row matrix
into Smith normal form \(UAV=D\), where \(U,V\) are unimodular.
In coordinates \(x=Vy\), the kernel conditions are
\(d_i y_i\in\mathbb Z\) on pivot coordinates, with all remaining
coordinates free. A target row descends iff its transformed coefficient
is divisible by \(d_i\) in each pivot coordinate and is zero in every
free coordinate. These are exactly the integer row-lattice membership
conditions. If one fails, a rational root-of-unity displacement or a
rational displacement in a free coordinate gives an explicit rival.

Integer lattice, rather than real span, is essential: observing
\(e(2x_1-2x_2)\) leaves a half-turn ambiguity in
\(e(x_1-x_2)\). Products and conjugates of the measured characters
generate \(L\); they do not remove its finite-index aliases.
This is a report-grammar classification. Restricting the candidate set
to an authenticated arithmetic orbit can remove some rivals and must
be analyzed separately.

### Pair graph specialization and references

Suppose the complete **complex** pair reports
\(z_i\overline{z_j}\) are available on a labelled graph. Their
integer rows are \(e_i-e_j\). Equality of reports says that
\(z_i'/z_i=z_j'/z_j\) on every edge; following paths proves that
the ratio is constant on each connected component. Conversely any such
componentwise rotation preserves all reports. Thus there is exactly one
\(U(1)\) gauge per component.

A calibrated complex phase reference in each component removes all
these gauges; a component with no reference remains ambiguous. Merely
measuring one real quadrature of a reference is not the same operation.
Complete real pair data also retain a global conjugation ambiguity;
the full complex edge convention keeps its orientation.

With a connected pair graph, adding monomial probes of global charges
\(q_a=\sum_j a_j\) reduces the remaining global rotations to the
\(d\)-th roots of unity, where \(d=\gcd_a q_a\). If all charges
are zero, the full global circle survives. In particular, arbitrarily
high **balanced** tensor interactions cannot recover absolute phase.
A charge-one reference removes it; charge two alone does not; charges
two and three together do.

## IC-03. A genuine periodic orbit can pass an arbitrarily large finite Fourier band

Take \(q=3^a5^b\), with integers \(a,b\ge1\), and set \(M=q/15\).
Then the multiplicative order of 16 modulo \(q\) is exactly \(M\).
For completeness, for \(p=3,5\), the binomial expansion proves

\[
v_p(16^n-1)=1+v_p(n).
\]

If \(p\nmid n\), the first term of \((1+15)^n-1\) has valuation
one and the others at least two. Raising a number congruent to one modulo
\(p\) to its \(p\)-th power increases this valuation by exactly
one; iteration gives the formula. Hence \(q\mid16^n-1\) iff
\(M\mid n\).

The orbit of \(1/q\) under multiplication by 16 has \(M\) distinct
points, all with residues congruent to one modulo 15. It therefore is
exactly the affine circle coset

\[
\left\{\frac{1+15j}{q}:0\le j<M\right\}
=\left\{\frac1q+\frac jM:0\le j<M\right\}.
\]

Its invariant mean character is

\[
\boxed{\widehat\mu(h)=
\begin{cases}0,&M\nmid h,\\e(h/q),&M\mid h.\end{cases}}
\tag{IC3}
\]

This follows by summing a complete geometric progression. Taking \(M>H\)
passes **every** test harmonic \(1\le h\le H\) exactly, yet the
fixed harmonic \(h=M\) has modulus one. It is a nonnormal actual
periodic radix point. The exact finite code checks denominators
405, 675 and 1125, with periods 27, 45 and 75.

Equation (IC3) is exact for the invariant orbit law and for complete-period
sampling. Arbitrary finite chronological shells have an incomplete
period remainder; for fixed \(M\) it vanishes as their lengths grow.
The code rejects incomplete arrays when requesting the complete-coset
certificate.

Attaching \(1/q\) to original forcing requires the changed seed
\(s=1/q-\pi/16\). Then \(U_n(s)=16^n/q-T_n\), and the inherited
continuous-character tail transport gives the same limiting means.
This controls a proposed inference from forcing plus finite modes; it
does not refute the authenticated zero-seed claim.

## IC-04. Finite word tests admit periodic de Bruijn counterfeits, also close at low Fourier modes

The directed de Bruijn graph of order \(k\) over an alphabet of size
\(b\ge2\) has vertices the \((k-1)\)-letter words and one edge
for each \(k\)-letter word, from its prefix to its suffix. Each vertex
has in-degree and out-degree \(b\), and the graph is strongly connected
by successively appending a target vertex's letters. An Euler circuit
therefore gives a cyclic word of length \(L=b^k\) containing each
\(k\)-letter word exactly once. The implementation uses this Euler
construction, separately from the inherited Lyndon-word generator.

Repeat that word forever and let

\[
\xi=0.\overline{d_0\cdots d_{L-1}}_b
=\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}d_jb^{L-1-j}}{b^L-1}.
\tag{IC4}
\]

For this nonconstant word, the radix expansion is unambiguous. The
actual orbit under multiplication by \(b\) reads its successive
cyclic shifts. For every \(1\le\ell\le k\), each \(\ell\)-word
occurs exactly \(b^{k-\ell}\) times per period, so its limiting
frequency is exactly \(b^{-\ell}\). At least one
\((k+1)\)-word is absent, because there are \(b^{k+1}>L\) possible
words but only \(L\) starting positions. Thus the periodic point
passes all word-frequency tests through the specified finite depth and
is not normal.

This also gives a simultaneous finite-precision Fourier control. There
is exactly one orbit point in each length-\(b^{-k}\) radix cell.
Couple that point to normalized uniform measure inside its own cell.
The mean distance within a cell is at most half its width, hence

\[
W_1(\mu,\lambda)\le\frac12b^{-k},\qquad
|\widehat\mu(h)|\le\pi|h|b^{-k}.
\tag{IC5}
\]

The second inequality uses the Lipschitz constant \(2\pi|h|\) of
the circle character, viewed on the interval. Therefore for any finite
harmonic band and positive tolerance, a sufficiently deep periodic
de Bruijn point passes that band to tolerance while passing all word
tests through its chosen depth exactly. The candidate changes with
the depth; this does not produce one nonnormal point passing every
depth in the limit.

The production test decodes the actual 256-point hexadecimal order-two
orbit from (IC4). The surrogate array \(d_n/16\) is separately rejected
as an orbit: multiplication by 16 sends each of its entries to zero.
This explicitly avoids the inherited backend's former invalid source
substitution. For a changed-seed attachment to BBP, digit comparisons
also need a boundary margin: the finite nonterminating periodic orbit
stays a positive distance from any fixed finite digit grid, so a
vanishing Green tail eventually preserves those fixed-depth digits.
No common margin for growing depth is asserted.

## IC-05. Marginals leave an exact interaction nullspace

For signs \(A,B\in\{-1,1\}\), the family

\[
\Pr(A=a,B=b)=\frac{1+\rho ab}{4},\qquad -1\le\rho\le1,
\tag{IC6}
\]

has identical fair marginals and \(\mathbb E AB=\rho\). Product,
locked and antialigned controls have \(\rho=0,1,-1\). They show
exactly why a prime marginal cannot determine a signed prime/complement
product. More generally, writing \(m=\mathbb EA\) and
\(u_\pm=\mathbb E[B\mid A=\pm1]\), when both fibers have positive
mass,

\[
\mathbb E AB=\frac{u_+-u_-}{2}
+m\frac{u_++u_-}{2}.
\]

Marginal balance controls the second term, not the conditional contrast.
This is the finite version of the frontier's inherited two-fiber
identity; it supplies no new estimate on the actual complement.

For \(d\) binary coordinates, Walsh characters
\(\chi_S(x)=(-1)^{\sum_{j\in S}x_j}\) are an orthonormal basis
under uniform measure. The laws

\[
\Pr_\epsilon(x)=2^{-d}[1+\epsilon\chi_S(x)],
\qquad |\epsilon|\le1,
\tag{IC7}
\]

have only one nonconstant Walsh moment: \(\mathbb E\chi_S=\epsilon\).
For any selected set of measured masks, an omitted mask gives the
explicit positive rivals (IC7). Conversely all Walsh moments reconstruct
the complete finite law by Fourier inversion. This is an exact
classification of the missing interaction coordinates, not a numerical
independence test.

In particular, all proper-coordinate marginals are uniform iff the
law has form (IC7) with \(S\) the full coordinate set. Lower-order
observations cannot determine its one remaining top-order parameter.

## IC-06. Aggregated lag responses and phase transport have distinct blind spots

Complete labelled complex pair data and summed cyclic lag data are
different reports. For a cyclic stream \(z\),
\(R_k=N^{-1}\sum_nz_{n+k}\overline{z_n}\) determines the squared
magnitudes of the discrete Fourier coefficients, by finite Fourier
orthogonality. It discards their relative phases. The intersection of
this ambiguity with unit-phase or source constraints must be checked;
arbitrary spectral rotations need not preserve those constraints.

The explicit sign control has negative sites \(\{0,1,3\}\), with
all other signs positive, on cycle lengths 11, 13 and 17. Its reflected
stream has exactly the same \(R_k\) for every lag, since real cyclic
autocorrelation obeys \(R_{-k}=R_k\). The streams are not cyclic
shifts of each other. Their digit words differ: cyclic `1101` occurs
once in the first and never in the reflection. Repeating each 0/1 word
as a hexadecimal expansion gives actual periodic radix points with
those **digit-parity** streams. This is not an identification of digit
parity with the smooth phase \(e(8x)\).

Indeed at \(x=1/32\), the leading hexadecimal digit is zero, so
digit parity is +1, whereas \(e(8x)=i\). At
\(x_\pm=1/16\pm\epsilon\), with \(0<\epsilon<1/16\), the
phase inputs approach each other but the two digit parities differ by
two. There is no global Lipschitz transfer from continuous-character
error to a discontinuous digit event. The completed/rational Green
comparison needs an explicit cell-margin certificate for such events.

Finally, every finite rational phase list has a common denominator
\(Q\), and its mode \(h=Q\) is perfectly locked. The exact test
checks this on original-zero clocks 19 through 41. This refutes an
unrestricted all-frequency finite-horizon decay bound. It does not
refute a statement with each frequency fixed before the horizon tends
to infinity. Similarly, a bound proportional to \(|h|/N\) cannot
be discarded on a growing-frequency schedule without paying its rate.

## IC-07. The universal mixture has no positive gap over nonnormal controls

The root proposed the following universal mixture after the finite-mode
control above was derived. The calculation has been checked directly.
For a phase law \(\mu\), define

\[
E(\mu)=\sum_{h\ge1}2^{-h}|\widehat\mu(h)|^2.
\]

For shell laws, \(E_B\to0\) iff every fixed nonzero character mean
tends to zero: one direction follows by domination of each nonnegative
term, the other by truncating the summable tail and taking the finite
head limit. This is an equivalent all-mode criterion, not a source
estimate.

Choose \(H\) with \(\Pr(H=h)=2^{-h}\), sample two independent
points from \(\mu\), and accept with conditional probability
\([1-\operatorname{Re}e(H(x-y))]/2\). Its mean click probability
is \((1-E(\mu))/2\). For the actual periodic coset law (IC3),

\[
E(\mu)=\epsilon_M:=\frac1{2^M-1}.
\tag{IC8}
\]

It can therefore approach the Haar value arbitrarily closely while
remaining periodic. The de Bruijn law also satisfies the independent
bound \(0<E(\mu)\le6\pi^2 16^{-2k}\), by (IC5) and
\(\sum_{h\ge1}h^2 2^{-h}=6\).

### Retained and forgotten frequency labels have different detection costs

Compare the **reported law of (H, click)** under ideal Haar and the
periodic coset. Conditional on \(M\nmid H\), both have a fair coin.
Conditional on \(M\mid H\), the coset coin is always zero, while
the Haar coin is fair. The geometric mass of those resonant labels is
\(\epsilon_M\). Direct summation gives

\[
\chi^2(P_{\rm coset}\Vert P_{\rm Haar})=\epsilon_M.
\tag{IC9}
\]

After forgetting \(H\), the comparison is between
\(\mathrm{Bernoulli}((1-\epsilon_M)/2)\) and a fair coin, giving
chi-square \(\epsilon_M^2\). For \(N\) independent trials,
\(1+\chi^2(P^N\Vert Q^N)=(1+\chi^2(P\Vert Q))^N\), while
\(\operatorname{TV}\le\sqrt{\chi^2}/2\). Equal-prior testing
error at most \(1/4\) therefore requires respectively

\[
N\ge\frac{\log2}{\log(1+\epsilon_M)}
\ge\frac{\log2}{\epsilon_M},\qquad
N\ge\frac{\log2}{\log(1+\epsilon_M^2)}
\ge\frac{\log2}{\epsilon_M^2}.
\tag{IC10}
\]

These orders are attained up to constants. With labels retained, a
resonant label and click one is impossible under the coset and occurs
under Haar at rate \(\epsilon_M/2\). The corresponding one-sided
test has equal-prior error
\(\tfrac12(1-\epsilon_M/2)^N\), requiring order
\(\epsilon_M^{-1}\) trials. Without labels, thresholding the sample
mean halfway between the two means has error at most
\(4/(N\epsilon_M^2)\) by its variance bound, giving order
\(\epsilon_M^{-2}\).

The finite exact instrument groups \(H\) by its residue modulo
\(M\), whose positive residue weights are
\(2^{M-r}/(2^M-1)\), \(1\le r\le M\). This yields a finite
exact chi-square check of both costs. It compares only the specified
reported variables. Retaining sampled clock identities, the full
phase values, or extra source information may distinguish the rivals
more cheaply; (IC10) is not a lower bound for that richer report or for
pi computation. Exact periodic probabilities use complete-period
sampling or the invariant law, not an arbitrary partial shell.
