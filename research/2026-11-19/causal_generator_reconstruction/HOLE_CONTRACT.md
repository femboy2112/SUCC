## The owner's question

What is the least costly lawful family of coherent causal observations from
which an observer can reconstruct a generating diagram, its composed path sums,
and a specified global target? The observer advances forward and retains records;
mathematical comparison of past paths is not physical backward travel.

The three obligations are **separation**, **generative closure**, and
**completion with signed transport**. They are distinct hypotheses. A diagram
means specified maps and composition laws, not a picture standing in for them.

## Exact proposed statements

1. **General finite response theorem.** For a response on finite action words,
   an authenticated global finite Hankel-rank bound and an accessible nonsingular
   response block reconstruct the minimal predictive realization, up to similarity.
   A finite observed rank alone does not authenticate that bound.
2. **Forward phase comparison.** On a known rooted directed graph with an
   out-arborescence, forward path comparisons identify independent chord phases
   up to vertex gauge. Access to both histories and a common phase reference are
   required; neither a return in time nor a physical quantum interpretation is.
3. **A concrete original-source acquisition theorem.** In the declared BBP
   four-pole coefficient-and-seed family, five unwrapped scalar state samples at
   any five strictly increasing nonnegative integer times reconstruct all five
   parameters. Fewer than five cannot do so on an open parameter class, even
   under deterministic adaptive choice of sampling times.
4. **Quantitative completion.** A specified stability or target-specific signed
   defect bound transports finite reconstruction to the proposed infinite target.
   Identification alone supplies no estimate on signed chronological means.
5. **Compression discrimination.** An exact nonlinear finite generator may have
   an infinite-dimensional linear response. For normal radix phases, a proposed
   bounded-rank linear approximation is subject to a quantitative Hankel-Gram
   error lower bound, including when the model is refitted at every horizon.

## Truth states and novelty tax

At registration these are candidate theorem statements, to be promoted only by
the proofs and audit in this directory. Minimal linear realization, Cauchy
determinants, spanning-tree gauge reduction and operator telescoping are classical.
SUCC already contains target descent, finite realization with rank promises,
irrational-phase infinite Hankel rank, exact BBP forcing-rank discrimination and
source/seed inversion. Those receive explicit antecedent credit.

The intended additions are an executable common theorem interface; the sharp
five-raw-sample causal acquisition result; forward-path rather than backward-loop
phase reconstruction; quantitative finite-rank compression discrimination; and
target-specific signed completion. This is not a claim of academic priority.

## Dependencies closed if proved

`lawful samples -> unique source model in a stated family -> composable rule`
and, separately,
`certified response model + uniform target error -> global target transport`.

For pi, the final source-selected signed inequality is still missing. R0 and
normality are not premises of the acquisition theorem and are not its conclusions.
The compression theorem's normality specialization is conditional on normality;
its finite matrix inequality applies to arbitrary data.

## Cheapest discriminators, fixed before new implementation runs

- Positive: Fibonacci two-dimensional rational realization; noncommuting
  two-letter rational systems; original BBP coefficient vector and zero seed.
- Negative: delayed hidden transition; an unobservable added mode; a changed
  graph support under similarity; constant locked phase; intensity-only phases.
- Null/gauge: vertex phase changes and invertible state coordinates with every
  boundary anchor transported; retained old records under a new readout.
- Source controls: literal wrong seed, rational `1/16` alias, changed four-pole
  coefficients, wrapped-state data, and an out-of-family delayed forcing change.
- Acquisition calibration: consecutive times `(0,1,2,3,4)`; disjoint irregular
  blocks; all increasing five-tuples from a bounded early set; later irregular
  and Fibonacci schedules as implementation holdouts.
- Mutation: omit a block's 16-power weights, discard the seed anchor, reverse
  noncommuting matrix order, or discard a relative phase. Each must be detected.

Pass means the exact identity or explicitly quantified bound holds on its stated
domain and the mutant is rejected. Ambiguous means source/access/precision or a
global closure premise is unpaid. Finite agreement is not a universal proof.
