# All-clock source observation closure and the sharp binary precision cost

**Status:** proved algebraic statements for the specified four-pole coefficient
family, with exact finite implementation checks. This note concerns the complete
family of **cleared source innovations only**. It does not assert closure of the
five-channel observer, a complete knowledge state, or the chronological pi phase.
It supplies no R0 or normality estimate.

The distinction is essential. If the seed is already fixed at zero, the fifth
terminal channel is another coefficient functional and can refine these fibers.
Stronger coefficient priors, nonuniform or increased precision, different
observables, and averaging noisy real measurements are different contracts.
The sharpness below is a worst-case statement over arbitrary coefficients in
`Z_2^4`, or unbounded integer coefficients, at uniform exact congruence precision.

## 1. The declared observation family

Fix `d=(7,4,3,2)` and

\[
r_c(m)=\sum_{j=1}^4\frac{c_j}{8m-d_j},\qquad m\ge1.
\]

Its innovation is `U_m-16U_(m-1)=r_c(m)`, independently of the seed. Write

\[
C_m=\left(\frac1{8m-d_j}\right)_{j=1}^4,\quad
L_m=\operatorname{lcm}_{j}(8m-d_j),\quad b_m=L_m C_m\in\mathbb Z^4.
\]

The measured cleared innovation is `b_m c=L_m r_c(m)`. The row scales are
explicit parts of the observation contract. A claim about these cleared values
does not automatically transfer to raw innovations or completed circle phases.

Choose a positive integer start n with `n=2 mod3` and `n!=0 mod5`, and let
`B_*` have rows `b_n,b_(n+1),b_(n+2),b_(n+3)`. The universal repair theorem

Such a four-clock block can be obtained within nine further source ticks after

## 2. BC-01: every cleared clock row is an integer combination of four rows

**Theorem.** For every integer m >= 1,

\[
\boxed{\lambda_m:=b_m B_*^{-1}\in\mathbb Z^4.}
\]

Consequently the full integer row lattice is already generated by this block:

\[
\sum_{m\ge1}\mathbb Z b_m
=\mathbb Z b_n+\mathbb Z b_{n+1}+\mathbb Z b_{n+2}+\mathbb Z b_{n+3}.
\]

The equality remains true if the left side includes only clocks after an
arbitrary horizon and the chosen block lies after that horizon. Its index in
the integer coefficient-covector lattice is `2^19`.

### Proof: first establish the complete binary row module

Let `Q(t)=prod_j(t-d_j)` and define

\[
A_m=4C_m,\qquad u_m=\frac{Q(8m)}8.
\]

The valuations of the four denominators at 2 are always `(0,2,0,1)`. Hence
`u_m` is an odd integer, and `L_m/4` is also an odd integer. Moreover,

\[
P(m):=u_m A_m
=\left(\frac12\prod_{k\ne j}(8m-d_k)\right)_{j=1}^4
\in\mathbb Z[m]^4
\]

is a polynomial vector of degree at most three. Its coefficients are integral:
after deleting any one pole label, at least one of the two even polynomial
factors `8m-4` and `8m-2` remains, so the product is divisible by 2 in `Z[m]`.

Newton's finite-difference identity gives, for every integer m,

\[
P(m)=\sum_{r=0}^3\binom{m-n}{r}\Delta^rP(n).
\]

Each generalized binomial coefficient is an integer, including negative m-n;
for negative arguments this follows from
`binom(-t,r)=(-1)^r binom(t+r-1,r)`. Each difference `Delta^rP(n)` is an
integer combination of `P(n),...,P(n+r)`. Thus every `P(m)` is an integer
combination of the four consecutive polynomial rows. Dividing by the odd
units `u_m` and `u_(n+i)` proves that every `A_m` is a `Z_2`-linear combination
of `A_n,...,A_(n+3)`.

This argument works for any four consecutive integer clocks; admissibility of
n was not needed for this binary step. Multiplying rows by the odd units
`L_m/4` then proves

\[
b_m B_*^{-1}\in\mathbb Z_2^4.
\]

### Upgrade from binary integrality to integer coefficients

Since `B_*` is integral and its determinant is `2^19`, the adjugate formula
gives `B_*^-1 in M_4(Z[1/2])`. Therefore `b_m B_*^-1` is a rational dyadic
vector. A dyadic rational that is also 2-adically integral is an integer:

\[
\mathbb Z[1/2]\cap\mathbb Z_2=\mathbb Z.
\]

This proves the boxed assertion. Every row on the left belongs to the four-row
lattice, and those four rows themselves occur in the full family, proving
equality. Restricting to a future family containing the four rows preserves the
same two inclusions. The lattice index is the absolute determinant. No infinite
computation or interchange of limits is used. **QED.**

The coefficients `lambda_m` are constructive: compute the finite integer row
and multiply by the fixed rational adjugate inverse. The theorem proves that
the result is integral; it does not bound its ordinary real magnitude.

## 3. BC-02: exact closure of a fixed-precision knowledge fiber

For any positive integer modulus M and any integer coefficient vectors c,c',
BC-01 gives

\[
\boxed{
B_*c\equiv B_*c'\pmod M
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
b_mc\equiv b_mc'\pmod M\quad\text{for every }m\ge1.}
\]

The reverse implication uses the four basis clocks; the forward implication
uses their integer linear combinations. Thus the complete cleared-innovation
transcript at the **same uniform modulus** does not further refine the fiber
determined by these four reports. Every future report is computable from them
as `b_m c=lambda_m (B_*c)` modulo M.

For binary absolute precision k >= 1 and a consistent report, the compatible
fiber in `Z_2^4` has normalized Haar volume

\[
\boxed{2^{-I_2(k)},\qquad
I_2(k)=k+(k-2)_+ +(k-6)_+ +(k-11)_+.}
\]

Equivalently, there are
`2^(min(k,0)+min(k,2)+min(k,6)+min(k,11))` compatible coefficient vectors
modulo `2^k`. This follows from the stated Smith form, as in
[KNOWLEDGE_VOLUME.md](KNOWLEDGE_VOLUME.md). For every odd p the four Smith
factors are units, so coefficient precision modulo `p^k` is fully recovered
from cleared measurements at that same precision.

This is an exact plateau of one specified observer's indistinguishability
fiber. It is not a probability of truth, a claim of independent prime evidence,
or a claim that every available observation has been exhausted. In particular,
the terminal anchor, known seed, and coefficient restrictions are absent from
the equivalence just proved.

## 4. BC-03: eleven additional binary digits are uniformly sufficient and sharp

**Theorem.** For every target precision a >= 1, uniform cleared-innovation
accuracy modulo `2^(a+11)` suffices to recover all four coefficients modulo
`2^a`. Uniform accuracy only modulo `2^(a+10)` does not suffice in the worst
case, even if all future cleared innovations are supplied.

**Sufficiency.** The Smith exponents are `(0,2,6,11)`, so every inverse entry
has valuation at least -11. An input error of valuation at least `a+11` therefore
induces coefficient errors of valuation at least a. Equivalently, invert the
Smith diagonal after its unimodular coordinate changes.

**Sharpness.** Write `E B_* F=diag(1,4,64,2048)`, where E and F are integer
unimodular matrices. The integer coefficient difference

\[
\delta c=F(0,0,0,2^{a-1})^T
\]

is not zero modulo `2^a`, because F preserves divisibility. Nevertheless,

\[
B_*\delta c=E^{-1}(0,0,0,2^{a+10})^T
\equiv0\pmod{2^{a+10}}.
\]

BC-01 then gives `b_m delta c=0 mod2^(a+10)` at every clock. These two source
coefficient vectors are indistinguishable under the entire stipulated transcript.
This is an actual information boundary of the fixed-precision contract, not a
failure to search enough future clocks. **QED.**

This theorem concerns innovation precision. Recovering the fifth seed coordinate
has the additional inverse budget in FC-05. If the seed is known in advance,
the terminal channel can instead impose another coefficient condition. Neither
case is covered by the innovation-only impossibility claim. Nor does the theorem
forbid improvement from a restricted finite coefficient set or real-error model.
