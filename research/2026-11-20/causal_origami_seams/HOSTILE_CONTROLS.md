# Hostile controls: seam fidelity, asymptotic statistics and numerical conditioning

**H1 — same-source locked counterseed:** `U_n(s)=16^n(s+P_n)`, `Y_n(s)=U_n(s)+T_n=16^n(s+π/16)`. For `s=−π/16`, `Y_n=0` exactly, yet every authentic `c_n`, the first-pole seam clock `j_p`, `m_n`, source-time F(z), Hankel determinant, and Fabry natural boundary are unchanged. **Rejection:** source-anchor `s=0` measured with an unwrapped head. **Not permitted:** claiming positive source spectral energy implies R0.

**H2 — late circle seed alias:** `s=1/16` gives `U_n(s)−U_n(0)=16^(n−1)∈Z` for `n≥1`. All late phase/Gaussian heads coincide; history/root heads distinguish. Adding more clones of the same circle head does not repair missing source coordinates.

**H3 — matched-first-jet source mutant:** `Q*=Q+(1−t)²` adds `2/[(8n−7)(8n−6)(8n−5)]` to r(n), preserving Q(1)=0 and Q'(1)=−15. At every first-pole prime-square seam, the p-primary fraction changes `4/p²→5/p²` while **timestamps do not change**. Both have infinite Hankel rank/natural boundary. A source-identification head must read the mark, not only a binary timestamp. Tests reconstruct these fractions independently.

**H4 — delayed out-of-family source impulse:** modify r(n) only after the last recorded clock. Any finite journal remains identical but the future differs. Neither a full-rank finite observer code nor a finite analytic polynomial authenticates an unrestricted future generating law. A source-family contract is load-bearing.

**H5 — false Yoneda:** two carrier states can generate identical scalar journals forever while differing in an unmeasured coordinate. The full Yoneda theorem concerns *all* hom probes in a declared category; a restricted pinbed must establish density or accept the observational quotient.

**H6 — source-time Fourier ↛ π Weyl:** positive c_n and nonzero finite Fourier interference, even a natural boundary, do not prove phase anti-lock. A Gaussian-smoothed source projection can be entire and still represent exactly the same locked wrong-seed phase countermodel. Gaussian regularity alone does not select Haar.

**H7 — memory lossy inverse:** given target `(P',G')`, every choice c gives a predecessor `(P'−c,c+zG')`. Invertibility and global distinguishability need the atom stored in an append-only journal or another source-adequate recovery channel. The scalar conservation `P+G(1)=x` is not a conservation law for all possible microscopic differences.

**H8 — finite vs infinite spectral realization:** a short program with growing integer clock can decide whether `8n−7` is a prime square (sqrt + primality); it does NOT follow that a fixed finite-state autonomous Moore machine, finite stationary Fourier sum, or finite constant-coefficient linear realization can output that infinite seam sequence. The explicit Hankel determinants witness the failure. Denying a bounded stationary representation does not demonstrate quantum mechanics, randomness or computational intractability of integer primality tests.

**H9 — Gaussian probability fabrication:** a formal Fourier multiplier `e^(−λn²/2)` does not automatically turn arbitrary chronological coefficients into a positive probability density. To assert probability, first declare a *positive, normalized* event-time measure and then convolve with a positive periodized Gaussian. Its apparent distribution depends on a declared clock-scale/weight measure, as #674 explicitly showed (unweighted prime-square age law `du/(2√u)` versus p-weighted uniform age law). Neither equals π's real-phase law automatically.

**H10 — rank versus conditioning:** nonzero Hankel determinant proves algebraic separation, not numerically well-conditioned inversion. For `L×L` binary seam Hankel `det=±1` and Frobenius norm ≤L, singular-value product=1 implies `σ_min≥L^(−(L−1))`, an extremely weak lower bound that worsens with L. No unbounded precision oracle is silently supplied. Source-marked rational Hankel determinants may shrink very rapidly.

**Next decisive mutation:** after a candidate signed cross-place witness is fixed in advance, run both exact original-zero and wrong-seed/mutant/jittered-clock probes using *the same* witness, demonstrate whether the root-conditioned phase response gains a uniform all-B sign, and exhibit a source-computable lower bound. Any proposed estimate that holds on H1 or requires the target phase itself to define its witness fails immediately.
