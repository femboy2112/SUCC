## A. What are we trying to prove?

The Riemann hypothesis states that every nontrivial zero of the Riemann zeta function has real part exactly 1/2. Because of the functional equation, it suffices to show **there are no zeros with real part greater than 1/2**.

There is a prime-counting observable that detects such zeros. For any real x>=1, define von Mangoldt's function

```
Lambda(n) = log(p)  if n=p^k for a prime p and integer k>=1
          = 0       otherwise.
```

Define the smoothed prime sum and its remainder:

```
S(x) = sum_{n<=x} Lambda(n)*(x/n-1),
G(x) = S(x) - x*log(x) + (1+gamma)*x,
```

where gamma is Euler's constant. The main arithmetic achievement of the released proof is ultimately an **upper bound** for G on arbitrarily large, sufficiently long intervals, of strength x^(1/2+epsilon). The fact that such *one-sided bounds on intervals* exclude zeros right of 1/2 is an **IMPORTED** analytic theorem; see Rubinstein's zeta guide, Part I.1 and Part II.1, and Slijepcevic's Proposition 2.2.

### Why the prime sum detects zeros — PROVED HERE modulo classical continuation

For complex s with Re(s)>1, ordinary integration gives for every integer n>=2:

```
int_{x=n}^infinity (x/n-1)*x^(-s-1) dx = n^(-s)/(s*(s-1)).
```

The Euler product yields the classical identity `-zeta'(s)/zeta(s) = sum Lambda(n)/n^s`. Hence the Mellin transform of G is **exactly**

```
int_{1}^infinity G(x)*x^(-s-1) dx
  = -(zeta'(s)/zeta(s))/(s*(s-1))
    - 1/(s-1)^2 + (1+gamma)/(s-1).
```

The two explicit rational terms cancel the principal part at s=1, because `-zeta'/zeta = 1/(s-1) - gamma + O(s-1)` there. A nontrivial zero rho gives a pole to the meromorphic right-hand side. The point of the one-sided criterion is to prevent those poles from occupying the half-plane to the right of the asserted error exponent.

**Careful:** this Mellin computation is fully proved here on Re(s)>1; the continuation of zeta and the stronger *sparse-interval one-sided criterion* are established classical/the-author-Landau inputs, not re-proved in this dossier.

## B. Where FUCC enters *literally* — PROVED HERE

The original SUCC/FuckingRH FUCC operator is generated by prime isometries `V_p|n>=|pn>` on `ell^2(N_{>0})`. Define the logarithmic multiplication operator `D|n>=(log n)|n>`. All commutators in this section are first interpreted on finitely supported vectors; finite sums extend boundedly as stated.

Then `[D,V_{p^k}] = k log(p) V_{p^k}`. The `1/k` in the Euler-logarithm series therefore cancels the `k` supplied by differentiation:

```
L_X = sum_{p^k<=X} V_{p^k}/k,
A_X = [D,L_X] = sum_{p^k<=X} (log p)*V_{p^k}
    = sum_{n<=X} Lambda(n)*V_n.
```

Let `|R_X> = sum_{n<=X}(X/n-1)|n>`, a finite vector. Since `V_n|1>=|n>`, the identity

```
<R_X|A_X|1> = S(X)
```

is **exact** for every real X>=1 (the cutoff selects integer n<=X). Therefore

```
G(X) = <R_X|[D,L_X]|1> - X*log(X) + (1+gamma)*X.
```

The **same one-sided prime statistic** in the Alpöge–Claude proof has now been reconstructed through the original FUCC multiplication channels. This is an exact *SUCC interface theorem*, although the algebra is a classical Euler-product computation in operator language and is not claimed globally novel.

We have not proved any upper bound on that matrix coefficient from its representation alone. The operator is **finite-wavefront**, the vector depends on X, and taking its scalar norm discards exactly the sign/cancellation needed for the one-sided estimate.

## C. Why FUCC's log commutator is not yet arithmetic multiplicity — PROVED NO-GO

The prime shift `V_p` changes the **label** of a basis vector:

```
(V_p)^a|1> = |p^a>.
```

Its only nonzero coefficient is **one**, with `v_p(1)=0` for every a. Thus increasing multiplicative support depth is NOT increasing divisibility of any nonzero coordinate coefficient. The apparent factor `(log p)^a` from iterating `[D,·]` is a real logarithm, not a p-adic integer with an available p-adic valuation.

An unbounded evaluation functional sending `|n>` to n would produce `p^a` from `V_p^a|1>`, but this does not give a bounded linear functional on `ell^2`, much less a common Archimedean control on integral minors. **Therefore the FUCC label geometry alone does not provide the required global arithmetic inequality.**

This is a concrete obstruction, not a claim that SUCC was conceptually unrelated.

## D. How to manufacture arithmetic multiplicity — PROVED HERE in a formal local model

Let p be a prime and work in Q_p[[T]] with the formal power series

```
l_p(T)=T + sum_{j>=1} T^(p^(2j))/p^j.
```

Because its linear coefficient is 1, there exists a unique formal inverse `U_p(z)=z+...` satisfying `l_p(U_p(z))=z`.

**Theorem (local height-two analogue).** If `f(T)` has coefficients in Z_p, then the a-th ordinary derivative of `f(U_p(z))` at z=0 satisfies

```
v_p((d/dz)^a f(U_p(z))|_{z=0})
   >= v_p(a!) - (a-1)/(p^2-1).
```

This rational lower bound is proved coefficient-by-coefficient in `TECHNICAL_PROOFS.md` (and verified at finite degrees by two computational routes). Legendre's formula gives

```
v_p(a!) = sum_{j>=1} floor(a/p^j)
        = a/(p-1) - O(log_p(a+1)).
```

Consequently

```
v_p(the derivative)
  >= a*(1/(p-1)-1/(p^2-1)) - O(log_p(a+1))
   = a*(1/p+1/p^3+1/p^5+...) - O(log_p(a+1)).
```

**This is the kind of growing arithmetic vanishing missing from a bare FUCC log commutator.** It is directly modeled on the local calculation in Rubinstein Part II §2 (not a new theorem about theta functions).

**What is still missing:** no source-authenticated SUCC/FUCC map has been proved from this formal power series to Alpöge's theta kernels on CM elliptic curves; no compatibility with integral lattices, number fields, all finite places, ranks or norms has been established here. ## E. The outside-in capacity inequality — PROVED HERE, elementary determinant model

Let A be an r-by-r matrix with integer entries and nonzero determinant. Suppose independent arithmetic construction has proved `v_p(det A)>=e_p>=0` for a finite set of primes. The determinant and Hadamard inequalities give

```
sum_p e_p*log(p) <= log|det A|
                   <= sum_{i=1}^r log ||row_i(A)||_2.
```

This makes the user's **inside-out** observer picture exact:

- An arithmetic observer sees many prime-adic orders of vanishing in one object.
- A real observer independently measures its maximum possible size.
- A nonzero object cannot have too much divisibility for its real size.

This is **a proved prototype of the capacity mechanism**, not the lattice-slope estimate used in Alpöge's RH proof. The released proof replaces our elementary finite-dimensional row norms with Hermitian lattice slope/covolume comparisons and controls a growing family of period maps, while retaining rank and denominator expenses.

## F. The real arithmetic summit — IMPORTED from the released Lean development

In Rubinstein's working guide, the main inequality is labelled **(M)** (Part II §4). One of its consequences is a bound, on the selected auxiliary-prime windows, schematically of the exact displayed form

```
R_{q,d}(x) <= x^(C*epsilon) * (
       x/m + (x*m)/k + m + k/n^2),
```

where a large family of theta-lift maps has dimensions/depth parameters

```
m ~ x^(1/2+O(epsilon)),
k ~ x^(9/2+O(epsilon)),
n ~ x^(2+O(epsilon)).
```

At these choices the four displayed terms are at most `x^(1/2+O(epsilon))`, and Rubinstein explains how the two auxiliary character estimates remove extraneous twists. A one-sided bound `G(x)<=x^(1/2+epsilon)` on arbitrarily large long windows then excludes zeros to the right of 1/2. **This is the genuine RH theorem chain, accepted by the external Lean verification.**

**The (M) boundary is nonnegotiable.** The available 164-page working guide explicitly states that it **does not yet supply a complete human-readable proof of (M)**; it imports (a) theta-kernel integrality, (b) arithmetic period/norm formulas and covolumes, (c) trace-formula rank/overlap estimates, (d) Galois cohomology denominator bounds, and (e) construction/assembly of the weighted maps from Lean.
